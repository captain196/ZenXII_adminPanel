## 0 · The central floor above all eight jurisdictions

**RTE Act 2009 s.5 — "Right of transfer to other school".** **[A]** ·
https://indiankanoon.org/doc/154784849/

- **s.5(2)** — a child moving *"from one school to another, either within a State or outside,
  for any reason whatsoever"* has a right to seek transfer.
- **s.5(3)** — the head-teacher *"shall immediately issue the transfer certificate"*; **delay in
  producing a TC may not be a ground for delaying or denying admission** elsewhere; a
  head-teacher who delays issuance **is liable to disciplinary action**.

**Scope limit:** s.5 sits in the elementary chapter — it protects a child through **class VIII**.
No equivalent central protection for classes IX–XII was found. That limit bites in this region:
the Telangana order in §6.1 is issued on the *Secondary Education* track **for class VIII**, and
the Kerala petitioner in 2025:KER:69076 was in class 10→12, outside the band — yet the court
still granted relief (§2.5).

**RTE Act s.2(a) — the "appropriate Government".** **[A]** ·
https://indiankanoon.org/doc/186552640/ (full Act: https://indiankanoon.org/doc/30032725/)

> *"(B) a Union territory having Legislature, the Government of that Union territory…"* — a UT
> **without** a Legislature falls to **the Central Government**.

| UT | Legislature? | Appropriate Government | Rules that therefore apply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Puducherry** | **Yes** | Government of Puducherry | its **own** RTE Rules |
| **Lakshadweep** | No | Central Government | **Central RTE Rules 2010** |
| **Andaman & Nicobar** | No | Central Government | **Central RTE Rules 2010** |

Both premises are level A; the conclusion is structural inference. What is **NOT FOUND** is a
notification naming Lakshadweep or A&N specifically. See §7 and §8.

**Central RTE Rules 2010 — the fallback parameters.** **[A]** ·
https://indiankanoon.org/doc/151475529/ — **rule 15** governs recognition; **Form 1** is the
self-declaration-cum-application; the certificate is issued by the **District Education
Officer**; the template grants *"provisional recognition to the … school for Class ___ to Class
___ for a period of **three years**"*. No renewal lead time appears in the rule text read.

## 1 · Karnataka

### 1.1 Recognition — RTE Rules 2012, rule 11

The **Karnataka Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Rules, 2012** were gazetted
and came into force **28.04.2012**. **[A (via judgment)]** · 2024:KHC:17757 ·
https://indiankanoon.org/doc/195737112/

**Rule 11** is the recognition rule. **r.11(2)** requires every Governing Council of a
non-government school established before the Act to file a **Self-Declaration in Form-I within
six months**, **to the concerned Block Education Officer (BEO)**, on compliance with the
Schedule norms. The court held this is a **mandatory statutory obligation, "there being no
exceptions"**, reading RTE s.18 with rule 11. **[A (via judgment)]**

### 1.2 Validity — 5 years, amended in 2024 to 10-then-permanent

This is Karnataka's analogue to AP's GO.38. Gazette text reproduced verbatim in two judgments:

> *"GOVERNMENT OF KARNATAKA / No: EP 64 PGC 2024 … DATED: 26.08.2024 — **2. Amendment of rule
> 11.** In the Karnataka Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Rules, 2012, in rule
> 11, in sub-rule (6), for the words **'Validity of such recognition shall be for five years'**,
> the words and figures **'Validity of such recognition shall be for ten years for the first
> time renewal and permanent for the next time subject to provisions of rule 12'**, shall be
> substituted."* — (Mahantayya S. Hosamath), Under Secretary to Government (Primary), Dept of
> School Education and Literacy

**[A (via judgment)]** · 2024:KHC:35018 https://indiankanoon.org/doc/132257957/ ·
2025:KHC:16527 https://indiankanoon.org/doc/195573899/

In both cases the court **quashed** the departmental circular of 22.03.2022 and consequent
notices as not surviving the amendment.

**Two caveats that must travel with this finding.**

1. The reproduced text is the **draft-publication** preamble, and its rule 1(2) says the
   amendment commences on **final** publication. The courts nonetheless treated it as operative
   from 26.08.2024. **The final gazette publication was NOT FOUND.**
2. **CONFLICT.** In 2024:KHC:17757 the court says it is reproducing rule 11 and then prints
   *"11. Admission of children belonging to weaker section and disadvantaged group"* — the
   **central** rules' subject-matter, not recognition. The same judgment nevertheless treats
   r.11(2) as the recognition self-declaration, and the 2024 amendment amends r.11(6) on
   recognition validity. Most likely a mis-paste in the judgment. **The authentic Karnataka RTE
   Rules 2012 text could not be obtained to resolve it** — recorded, not resolved.

**Renewal lead time for Karnataka: NOT FOUND.**

**Rule-vs-practice conflict.** A circular of 09.12.2015 permits students of high schools *"whose
recognition was not renewed for 2016-17"* to sit the SSLC exam — framing that implies the
department was administering renewal **annually**, which sits awkwardly with a five-year rule.
**[B]** · https://schooleducation.karnataka.gov.in/uploads/schooledu_pdfs/pdffiles/SSLCExamPermission091215.pdf

### 1.3 Karnataka Education Act 1983 — registration and recognition are two statuses

Karnataka runs **registration** (s.31 conditions; **s.34 cancellation**) and **recognition**
(**s.39 withdrawal**) separately; both were invoked together in 2023:KHC:31782.
**[A (via judgment)]** · https://indiankanoon.org/doc/72355766/

**s.39(3) is the commercial teeth:** a school without recognition, or whose recognition is
withdrawn, cannot **(a)** receive grant-in-aid or government assistance, or **(b)** *"send up or
present candidates for examinations in courses of study conducted by a University or the
Government."* Loss of recognition therefore **blocks board-exam entry**.

**s.34(1)–(2)** require an enquiry, a report to the competent authority, and an **opportunity of
being heard** before cancellation. In 2023:KHC:31782 the DDPI passed the order without
considering the reply — a natural-justice failure.

**Officer chain, evidenced from litigation:** **BEO** (receives Form-I, inspects, reports) →
**DDPI** (issues and cancels recognition) → **Director of Public Instruction** → **Commissioner
for Public Instruction**. **[A (via judgment)]**

### 1.4 Scope of recognition

- **Per class range: YES.** In 2023:KHC:31782 the DDPI rejected a medium-of-instruction change
  *"on the plea that first recognition for Standard VI to VIII as not having been obtained by
  the management"* — recognition is held for a named standard range and one range does not carry
  another. **[A (via judgment)]**
- **Premises-bound: YES.** The same school had to seek — and was refused — *"post-facto
  permission to shift"* standards I–V after relocating; cancellation flowed from the unpermitted
  shift. **[A (via judgment)]**
- **Granted to the trust/society: partially.** The petitioner throughout is the Trust and it is
  the Trust's registration that is cancelled. No Karnataka certificate naming the society as
  grantee was seen. **[D]**
- **Per SECTION: NOT FOUND.** The AP/Telangana "one section" pattern is **not confirmed** for
  Karnataka.

### 1.5 Transfer certificates — countersignature was abolished in 2016

**This is the most operationally important Karnataka finding.** Circular of the **Commissioner
for Public Instruction dated 01.06.2016**, in English. **[B]** ·
https://schooleducation.karnataka.gov.in/uploads/schooledu_pdfs/Prypdfs/GenCirculars/TCCountersignCircular010616.PDF

> *"The transfer Certificates issued from private schools were to be countersigned by the **BEO**
> as per directions issued vide … **dated 25.5.2016**. This was done to ensure that the school in
> which the student studied is a recognized school. However, there are widespread complaints
> that this system causes inconvenience … the department has established a database of all
> schools in the state through **U-DISE**. Hence, the directions issued vide the circular
> referred (1) above are **hereby withdrawn**. **The transfer certificates issued by any schools
> in the state need not be sent for any countersignature.**
>
> The Private Schools are hereby directed to **issue the transfer certificate compulsorily
> mentioning their DISE code** … By entering the DISE code in the school search, the genuineness
> of the previous school can be verified on line."*

So Karnataka imposed BEO countersignature on **25.05.2016** and **withdrew it one week later on
01.06.2016**, replacing it with a **mandatory DISE code field on every TC** plus online lookup.

**That is the one Karnataka-mandated TC field that can be evidenced.** No prescribed Karnataka TC
format, schedule or form number was found.

*Minor internal oddity:* the circular's subject line refers to government-school children while
the body governs private schools' TCs; the operative sentence is general — *"any schools in the
state."* A companion circular of 26.05.2016 exists but is an image-only scan with no extractable
text: https://schooleducation.karnataka.gov.in/uploads/schooledu_pdfs/pdffiles/countersign_tc_26052016.pdf

**This finding bears directly on the corpus's open question.** It is independent evidence that
the **direction of travel on countersignature is abolition, replaced by machine-verifiable
identifiers** — the same move the CBSE staleness flag suspects. It does not settle the CBSE
question, but it makes "verify the identifier, don't demand a wet countersignature" the safer
default.

### 1.7 No dues — Karnataka: **prohibited. Level A.**

**Rohit .G v. Sree Saraswathi Trust**, W.P. No. 31492 of 2024 (EDN-RES), **2025:KHC:5986**,
**10.02.2025**, Justice K.S. Hemalekha. **[A]** · https://indiankanoon.org/doc/102361092/

Facts: student completed class 10 in 2024; ₹1,71,000 claimed for 2020–2023. The parents said
they had paid in cash/online to school staff as instructed, and the trust had itself found
**fraud by its own administrative staff**. The school's stated reason for withholding is the
revealing part: it said the TC *"would mention that no fees are due and this would act as a
barrier for respondent No.1-trust to take any further action"* — **it was withholding precisely
because the TC form certifies no-dues.**

> *"**The certificate of a student is the student's property and respondent No.1 holds the
> Transfer certificate/documents as a trustee**, who cannot retain the educational certificate on
> the ground that the monetary claim … is not paid by the said student. The petitioner was a
> minor while he was studying … and **if there is any default or non payment of the school fees,
> it can only be attributed to the parents and not the student** … for non payment of school
> fees, the student cannot be penalized and the documents which is sought by the student is
> entirely the student's property and not the property of the parents or of the institution…"*

> *"The school cannot withhold the transfer certificate on the ground of **any monetary
> claim**…"*

Writ allowed; TC to be handed over at a **named date and time** (17.02.2025, 11:00 a.m.); the
school free to pursue its claim in accordance with law; whether the money was owed is *"a
disputed question of fact, which this Court declines to interfere with."*

**Limits.** The basis is **property + trusteeship + non-attribution of a parent's default to the
child** — not RTE, not Art. 21-A — which makes it *broader*, since it does not turn on the
child's age or class. But it is an **oral order at preliminary hearing**, not a fully argued
reasoned precedent, and the court expressly declined to decide whether the fees were owed.

**No Karnataka rule permitting withholding was found**, and **no Karnataka circular prohibiting
it** either — the department's general-circulars index
(https://schooleducation.karnataka.gov.in/166/general/kn) carries TC-countersignature,
unauthorised-schools, recognition-renewal, fee-structure and anti-donation circulars, **but
nothing on TC withholding**. The Karnataka position rests on the High Court alone.

## 2 · Kerala

Kerala runs **two parallel recognition tracks**, and conflating them is the main trap.

1. **Kerala Education Act 1958 + KER Chapter V** — the detailed, still-operative state machinery.
2. **Kerala RTE Rules 2011, rule 14** — the "Order of Prior Recognition", which is what a **CBSE
   school needs for affiliation renewal**.

### 2.1 KER Chapter V — recognition is per STANDARD, and it says so

Source read in full: https://education.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Chapter_5.pdf
**[A throughout this subsection]**

> **r.1(5).** *"The opening of schools by an Educational Agency and their recognition shall be
> governed by the procedure laid down in the rules below and they shall apply mutatis mutandis to
> the opening and recognition of new standards. **Permission to open and for recognition for each
> standard shall be separately obtained.**"*

> **r.19.** *"The Order granting recognition to a school shall specify: (i) **the Educational
> Agency in whose favour it is issued**; (ii) the grade of the school…; (iii) **the Standard or
> Standards to which recognition is granted**; (iv) **the location of the school**; and (v) the
> date from which recognition will take effect. **The recognition so granted shall not apply to
> any other Educational Agency or to any other school or standard or to any other location.**"*

r.20 repeats this for a single standard. **Form 2** (the recognition application) carries items
**"Standards already recognized"** and **"Standards for which recognition is sought"** —
the form itself confirms the per-standard model.
https://education.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Forms.pdf

**Kerala is thus the clearest statutory statement in the region of the exact pattern seen in the
Telangana order: bound to the agency, to named standards, and to a named location.**

**Sections ("divisions") are sanctioned separately, by a different officer than recognition** —
r.3:

- **DEO** — *"to grant recognition to Primary, Secondary and Training schools and … Standards and
  withdraw recognition of schools of all types"* [r.3(b)(vi)]; and *"to permit the opening and
  closing down of **divisions of standards in Secondary Schools**"* [r.3(b)(vii)]
- **AEO** — *"to sanction the opening and closing of **division in existing Standards in Primary
  Schools**"* [r.3(c)(i)]
- **Director** — temporary recognition beyond one year [r.3(a)(v)]; appeals against DEO
  recognition orders [r.3(a)(ix)]

**So for Kerala, question 4 is an unambiguous yes on both axes: per standard for recognition,
and per section/division as a separate sanction.**

### 2.2 Validity and renewal — Kerala never had a fixed term to extend

> **r.21(1).** *"If any of the conditions prescribed in rule 17 is not satisfied a **temporary
> recognition for one year at a time** may be granted by the District Educational Officer.
> **Previous approval of the Director shall be obtained to accord temporary recognition beyond
> three years. Permanent recognition shall be granted to the schools satisfying all the
> prescribed conditions.**"*

> **r.16(a).** Applications for recognition go to the **DEO in Form 2 within three months** of
> opening. Applications *"for the continuance of temporary recognition … shall be made to the
> District Educational Officer **not later than three months before the expiry** of the temporary
> recognition."*

**That three-month window is Kerala's counterpart to the Telangana/AP 90-day rule.** r.16(b):
delay condonable by the DEO; beyond three months needs **Deputy Director (Education)** approval.
r.16(c): the application must carry a scaled **site plan** and a scaled **ground plan showing the
standards accommodated in each room**. r.16(d): recognition may be **permanent or temporary**.

r.17 lists six conditions (opened with r.11 permission; sound finances with no default on the
r.7 financial guarantee; teachers appointed per the Act; written undertaking to have accounts
annually audited; the school must be *"necessary to meet educational needs of the locality"*;
conducted per the Act). r.18 permits refusal for prior *"improper competition with other
institutions"*. **r.13A** allows withdrawal of both recognition and permission for a **false
declaration**.

**Consequence: a Kerala school on permanent recognition has no expiry date at all.** An
eligibility model that requires every school to carry a validity end-date will have nothing
truthful to put there for Kerala.

### 2.3 The RTE track — rule 14 and the Deputy Director

**Rule 14** of the Kerala RTE Rules 2011 governs recognition or upgradation; application in
**Form No. I**, and **sub-rule (6) mandates that recognition be given in Form No. II**.
**[A (via judgment)]** · https://indiankanoon.org/doc/42004374/

In *The Manager v. State of Kerala*, WP(C) 22812/2020, **22.01.2021**, the court declared that
an "Order of Prior Recognition" under rule 14 *"shall be issued by the concerned **Deputy
Directors of Education only in Form No. II of the Rules and in no other manner**, after following
due procedure and after making such necessary inspections as are warranted"*, and directed the
Director of General Education to circulate the judgment to all DDEs. **[A (via judgment)]** ·
https://indiankanoon.org/doc/99063321/

The dispute is instructive: DDEs were issuing these orders in ad-hoc formats, and some refused
outright on the ground that a CBSE-affiliated school needs no state recognition. **The court
rejected that** — **CBSE Affiliation Bye-law 2.3.4** makes the state recognition certificate a
**prerequisite** to processing affiliation. **A Kerala CBSE school needs both.**

**Kerala RTE recognition validity period: NOT FOUND.** No term-of-years sub-rule surfaced.

### 2.4 The prescribed TC — KER Form 5, read in full — **[A]**

*"[See rule VI-17(1)] TRANSFER CERTIFICATE"*, bilingual English/Malayalam.
https://education.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Forms.pdf

Mandated fields, in order: Name of School · **Whether the School is a Government, Aided or
Recognised School** · Name of Pupil · Name of Parent/Guardian and relationship · Identification
marks · Nationality · Religion · Whether SC/ST/OBC or a convert from SC/ST · **Date of birth per
the Admission Register (in words)** · **Standard in which last enrolled (in words)** · Date of
admission or promotion to that standard · Whether qualified for promotion · **"Whether the pupil
has paid all the fees due to the school"** · Whether in receipt of fee concession · Date of last
attendance · Date name removed from rolls · Date of application · Date of issue · Reason for
leaving · School intended to proceed to · Date of last successful vaccination · Number of school
days to date · Number of school days attended. Signed: **Principal / Headmaster / Headmistress**.

**Two things matter for a TC feature.**

1. **The no-dues field is baked into the statutory form itself** — which is exactly the mechanism
   the Karnataka trust in §1.7 said it was protecting. The courts' answer is that the certificate
   must still issue; the field is a **statement of fact, not a gate**.
2. **Form 5 has no countersignature block.** Headmaster's signature only.

**Other KER Chapter VI TC rules** (https://education.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Chapter_6.pdf) **[A]**:

- **r.17(1)** — TC in Form 5, issued by the Headmaster during summer vacation *"and for sufficient
  reasons at other times"*, but **at any time** for a pupil who has appeared for a public exam.
- **r.17(3)** — for a removed pupil over 20, no TC without prior sanction under r.5(2); a
  **leaving certificate in Form 5A** may issue instead.
- **r.18(2)** — TCs of pupils removed under r.15(iii)/(iv) issue *"on payment of all dues"*.
- **r.19(2)** — where a TC issues with the Educational Officer's or Director's sanction, the
  sanction number and date must be entered on the TC over the Headmaster's signature.
- **r.20** — refusal or delay in issuing a TC carries a **right of appeal to the Educational
  Officer**, whose decision is final unless referred upward. *This is the statutory remedy a
  parent has before going to the High Court, and no other southern state was found to have one.*
- **r.21** — the Director may group neighbouring schools so that no TC issues between schools of
  the same type within a group without the Educational Officer's sanction — an anti-poaching
  provision.
- **r.22** — duplicate TC on a ₹1 fee plus a loss certificate from a Gazetted Officer / local
  authority President / MLA / MP; must be marked **"Duplicate"**.

**Countersignature in Kerala — the answer is conditional.** **Ch.VI r.10**: pupils migrating from
schools **in other States or outside India** may be admitted on a TC *"or other equivalent
document **countersigned by the Inspecting Officer**"*, provided the school is recognised by the
respective Government and **not more than two months** have elapsed since issue (later admission
needs the Educational Officer's sanction). **For ordinary intra-Kerala TCs no countersignature is
required.** **[A]**

### 2.5 No dues — Kerala: a **live, unresolved conflict**

**The rule text — read, and the brief's premise is CONFIRMED here.** KER Chapter VI **r.17(2)**:

> *"No transfer certificate shall be issued to a pupil from whom there are any dues to the
> school."* **[A]** · https://education.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Chapter_6.pdf

The rule does not merely *permit* withholding — it **mandates** it. (r.17(2) also carries a Note
apportioning the monthly fee instalment between the old and new school by transfer date.)

**The judgment — and a correction to the premise.** **2025:KER:69076** is
***Amit K. Togi (Minor) v. The Director of Education (CBSE)***, WP(C) No. 31794 of 2025,
**17.09.2025**, Justice N. Nagaresh, Kerala HC at Ernakulam. **[A]** ·
https://indiankanoon.org/doc/147026647/

**It says nothing about KER Ch.VI r.17(2).** The rule is not mentioned, cited or discussed
anywhere in it. The respondent was a **CBSE** school (The Choice School, Tripunithura) — KER did
not apply — and the rule actually argued was **CBSE Examination Bye-laws Ch.3 r.8(vi)** (a
leaving student *"shall on payment of all dues, receive an authenticated copy of the Transfer
Certificate"*). The court rejected that argument.

Facts: class-10 student, ₹80,400 claimed. The school argued the family had left without notice,
admitted the dues in writing, then sought readmission, and that an issued **PEN number**
*"amounts to a provisional Transfer Certificate."* Held:

> *"This Court in the judgment in **Fathima Zahara (Minor) v. CBSE Regional Office [2023 KHC
> 9278]** has held that the School is not entitled to withhold Transfer Certificate for
> non-payment of fees… Since this Court … as well as in **a number of judgments** has held that
> Educational Institutions are not entitled to withhold Transfer Certificate for non-payment of
> dues, I am of the view that the omission of respondents in issuing Transfer Certificate cannot
> be justified. **The factual backgrounds under which such Transfer Certificate is withheld are
> of no consequence.** The Right to Education is a fundamental right now guaranteed by the
> Constitution of India. By withholding Transfer Certificate to a minor child, the Institution is
> causing violation of the fundamental rights."*

> *"The writ petition is therefore allowed. The 2nd respondent is directed to issue Transfer
> Certificate to the petitioner. **This will be without prejudice to the right of the 2nd
> respondent to realise / recover any dues as per law.**"*

**Limits: essentially none on the TC duty.** *"The factual backgrounds … are of no consequence"*
is deliberately unbounded — not confined to RTE-covered children, not to elementary classes, not
to a school type. The petitioner was 16, moving class 10→12, i.e. **outside** the RTE 6–14 band,
and the court still granted relief — so the holding does not depend on RTE coverage. The only
carve-out preserved is the school's separate right to recover the money lawfully.

Precedent relied on: ***Fathima Zahara (Minor) v. CBSE Regional Office*** [2023 KHC 9278],
WP(C) 21991/2023, 14.10.2023, Justice Basant Balaji — 13-year-old, ₹39,055 arrears; *"The
Transfer Certificate of a child cannot be withheld by a School because the fees are due to the
School."* **[C]** ·
https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/kerala-high-court/kerala-high-court-transfer-certificate-cannot-be-withheld-school-fees-arrears-240140

**The conflict, stated plainly.** The Kerala High Court has repeatedly held that schools may not
withhold a TC for fees — but it has done so **without ever striking down or reading down KER
Ch.VI r.17(2)**, and both judgments read involved **CBSE** schools where KER does not apply.
**No judgment and no gazette amendment removing r.17(2) was found.** A Kerala *state-syllabus*
school therefore faces an unamended subordinate rule telling it to withhold and a line of
constitutional writ decisions telling it not to. The constitutional holding prevails; the rule
stays on the books. **This is flagged, not resolved.**

### 3.2 TNER rules 38–46 — the TC rules, read verbatim — **[A]**

**Tamil Nadu Educational Rules** (1968 compilation), https://indiankanoon.org/doc/24617366/

> **r.40.** *"When a pupil is allowed to continue his studies in an institution during any term on
> the assumption that there are no arrears of fees for previous terms, a transfer certificate
> **shall not be refused** on the plea that such arrears exist."*

> **r.41.** *"When a proper application for a transfer certificate is received at the end of a
> term, or in the interval between two terms, or in first five working days of a term, the
> headmaster **shall forthwith issue** the certificate, provided that his claims for special fees
> admissible under rule 38 have been satisfied."*

> **r.42.** *"When proper application is received at any other time and when good and sufficient
> reasons are shown, the headmaster shall issue the certificate provided that his claims for fees
> admissible under rule 38 have been satisfied. If good and sufficient reasons are not shown, the
> headmaster may refuse to grant the transfer certificate."*

> **r.38.** *"Before granting a transfer certificate, the headmaster is entitled to claim the
> **special fee for one term only** and that the term in which the last attendance of the pupil is
> registered."*

> **r.43.** An application rejected under r.42 *"may be renewed at the end of a term … and if the
> application is so renewed the headmaster **shall issue the transfer certificate forthwith**,
> provided his claims for special fees admissible under rule 38 have been satisfied."*

> **r.44.** After more than a year since the pupil left, the headmaster *"may besides enforcing the
> provision of rule 38, charge such **search fee** … as reasonable by the District Educational
> Officer. A fee of fifty paise may be levied for the issue of a **duplicate** transfer certificate
> which should be clearly marked 'duplicate'."*

> **r.52(i)(1).** Schools must maintain *"A **register of admissions and withdrawals** (Appendix
> 6)"*, producible to the District Educational Officer.

### 3.3 **CORRECTION to the research brief's premise**

The brief states that TNER rules 40–42 *"contain rule text permitting a school to withhold a TC
for unpaid fees."* **The primary text says close to the opposite.**

- **r.40 is a prohibition, not a permission.**
- r.41–43 condition issuance only on **"special fees admissible under rule 38"**, and r.38 caps
  that at **one term's special fee** for the term of last attendance. That is a narrow, capped,
  current-term charge — **not general arrears**.
- The only refusal power in r.42 is for a **mid-term application where "good and sufficient
  reasons are not shown"** — a *timing* ground, not a *fees* ground — and r.43 lets the applicant
  cure it by re-applying at term end.

**Withdraw the premise for Tamil Nadu as stated.** A *different* TN instrument may permit
withholding — the Recognised Private Schools (Regulation) Act 1973/Rules 1974, or the Private
Schools (Regulation) Act 2018 — **that has not been checked.**

of absence in TN law generally.

**What was found instead is materially weaker:** ***Miss. J. Roshini v. The Principal***,
W.P.No.20026 of 2024, **2024:MHC:2730**, **18 July 2024**, **single judge**, Dr Justice Anita
Sumanth. **[A]** · https://indiankanoon.org/doc/194225027/

> *"withholding of certificates in any way by the school cannot be countenanced as the school can

But **the petition was dismissed** (*"This Writ Petition and the connected Miscellaneous Petition

> **s.21(2).** On six grounds (a)–(f) — failure to fulfil conditions, denial of admission on

**This settles question 4 for the AP/Telangana track at Level A.** Recognition is **statutorily

per-section scoping was found** for AP/TG.

**s.19(c)** is the statutory root of according recognition **to the registered society**.
**s.21(2)** is why the order says *"liable to be withdrawn at any time"* — withdrawal is real and
must be modelled as an immediate state.

**ss.22 onwards were not read** — the fee, records and register provisions, if any, are
unchecked.

### 9.2 What the pattern does and does not establish

**Establishes**, at Level A, in **Kerala, Karnataka and Telangana**: a school may not withhold a
Transfer Certificate as leverage for unpaid fees; the remedy is a recovery action. Three courts,
three years, three *independent* lines of reasoning — which is stronger than three courts
following one another.

**Does not establish:**

- That any is a **Supreme Court** ruling or a binding **Division Bench** precedent. All three are
  **single-judge writ decisions**; the Karnataka one is an **oral order at preliminary hearing**.
  They bind their respondents and are strongly persuasive. They are not a nationwide rule.
- Anything for **Andhra Pradesh**, **Puducherry**, **Lakshadweep** or **A&N** — **NOT FOUND** in
  each. In AP the silence is notable, not reassuring: it is the one large southern state with no
  located authority either way.
- That the **enabling rule text has been struck down**. It has not. **Kerala's KER Ch.VI r.17(2)
  — *"No transfer certificate shall be issued to a pupil from whom there are any dues to the
  school"* — is still on the books, unamended**, and the 2025 judgment never touched it (it was a
  CBSE case). Tamil Nadu's TNER never permitted general withholding in the first place. In every
  verified case the court directed issuance **"without prejudice"** to recovery.

**What has gone is the self-help remedy of withholding, not the debt and not the rule.**

### 9.3 Product consequence — stated as engineering, not as law

A no-dues **block** on TC issuance is legally unsafe in at least Kerala, Karnataka and Telangana,
and in Tamil Nadu is contrary to TNER r.40 on the rule text itself.

A no-dues **flag** — surfaced to the issuing officer, recorded against the register entry, never
blocking issuance — is consistent with every judgment read: each preserved the school's right to
recover and denied it the right to withhold.

**But note the Kerala complication, which a flag alone does not solve.** KER **Form 5** contains
the mandatory field *"Whether the pupil has paid all the fees due to the school"*. It is a
**statement of fact on a statutory form**, not a gate — and it is precisely the field the
Karnataka trust in §1.7 said it was protecting when it withheld the TC. So the correct behaviour
is: **print the field truthfully, and issue anyway.** Suppressing the field would falsify a
statutory form; letting it block issuance would repeat the conduct three High Courts have
condemned. This matches the corpus's existing rule that the no-dues gate must never default on
and must be per-state configurable.

## 10 · Patterns that hold across the region

**1 · Recognition is per class range everywhere it could be checked, and the statutory language
is explicit in three states.**
Kerala KER V-1(5): *"Permission to open and for recognition for each standard shall be separately
obtained."* AP Education Act s.21(1): recognition *"for a higher class in any such institution"*.
TN RTE Form II: *"for class … to class …"*. Karnataka, evidenced from litigation: *"first
recognition for Standard VI to VIII"*. Central RTE Rules Form: *"for Class ___ to Class ___"*.
**A school recognised for I–VII is not recognised for VIII.**

**2 · Per-SECTION scoping is confirmed in only two of the eight, and by different mechanisms.**
Telangana grants recognition itself *"with one section"* (§6.1, Level A). Kerala treats sections
as **"divisions" sanctioned separately** from recognition — AEO for primary, DEO for secondary
(§2.1, Level A). **Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and AP: NOT FOUND.** The AP/Telangana one-section pattern
should **not** be generalised.

**3 · Recognition attaches to the society/management, not the school** — Kerala KER V-19 names
*"the Educational Agency in whose favour it is issued"*; the Telangana order names the society;
AP Education Act s.19(c) defines a private institution by its **registered body of persons**.

**4 · Recognition is premises-bound in every state where the point arose** — Kerala V-19 (*"nor to
any other location"*), the Telangana order cond. 5, Karnataka's refused post-facto shift
permission.

**5 · Validity is the least generalisable field in this document.** Within one region:
Kerala **permanent, with a 1-year temporary track**; Tamil Nadu **no term stated at all**;
AP **8 years** since 2023; Karnataka **10 years then permanent** since 2024; Telangana **10 years**
in the order read; the Central fallback **3 years**. **Never infer a validity period from the
state — read it off the order.**

**6 · The renewal warning window, where one exists, is ~90 days.** Telangana **90 days** (order
cond. 18); Kerala **3 months** (KER V-16(a)). Karnataka, TN, AP: **NOT FOUND**.

**7 · Countersignature is receding, and its replacement is a machine-checkable identifier.**
Karnataka imposed BEO countersignature on 25.05.2016 and **withdrew it on 01.06.2016**, replacing
it with a **mandatory DISE code on every TC** plus online verification. Kerala requires
countersignature **only for inbound out-of-state TCs**. TN, AP, Telangana: **NOT FOUND**. This is
independent support for the corpus's suspicion that the CBSE countersignature requirement
`r.8(vii)` may be stale — and for designing around **identifier verification** rather than wet
countersignature.

**8 · The register is the source of truth, confirmed in two states.** TN **r.52(i)(1)** — *"a
register of admissions and withdrawals (Appendix 6)"*, producible to the DEO. Kerala **Form 5**
requires the date of birth *"as entered in the Admission Register"*. This supports the design rule
that a certificate transcribes a register entry rather than being composed freely.

**9 · Inbound interstate transfers are gated in at least two states.** Telangana order cond. 14
(no admission from an out-of-state recognised school **without DEO/Director permission**); Kerala
Ch.VI r.10 (out-of-state TC must be **countersigned by the Inspecting Officer** and used within
**two months**). A product that treats an inbound TC as self-executing will be wrong in both.

**10 · "Recognition" can mean two different licences in the same state.** Telangana has
departmental recognition (right to run and issue) *and* BSE Telangana's SSC-exam recognition.
Kerala requires a state RTE **Form II** order *in addition to* CBSE affiliation (Bye-law 2.3.4).
Karnataka fuses them — KEA s.39(3) makes losing recognition also block exam entry.
