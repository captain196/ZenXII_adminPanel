# Gap closure — north & north-east: five named authorities

**Run date: 2026-09-12.** Scope: the **Arunachal Pradesh Education Rules 2010**, **Sikkim**'s operating-recognition
and TC regime, **UBSE** (Uttarakhand), **BSEM** (Manipur) and the **MBOSE Examination Regulations** — five gaps
each already partly located by earlier streams.

> **Three headline results.**
>
> 1. **MBOSE's Examination Regulations are PUBLISHED.** The corpus recorded them as *"exist but appear
>    unpublished online"* and flagged that as *"the single most important caveat in this file."*
>    **That is refuted.** The **Regulations for the Conduct of the SSLC Examination, 2026** — 43 pages,
>    framed under the MBOSE Act 1973 — are on mbose.in. They were not in the statutory-documents section
>    (which lists only Acts); they were **rows 25–26 of page 2 of the paginated notice index**, dated
>    29-04-2026. See §2.1.
> 2. **BSEM is reachable.** The `ECONNREFUSED` that stopped the earlier pass was an **HTTPS-only failure**.
>    `http://bsem.nic.in/` (plain HTTP) returns 200, and the alternate domain `bosem.in` is fully live.
>    BSEM publishes a Recognition Rules page, its constitutive Act, and three numbered recognition forms.
>    See §2.2.
> 3. **The Manipur RTE Rules 2010 — recorded as NOT FOUND — were found**, on BSEM's own server,
>    mislabelled as an "Act". They carry a **printed-identifier mandate**. See §2.2.5.
>
> **And one methodological warning.** Every single countersignature provision recovered in this run is
> **inbound** — a requirement on a certificate *presented* to the authority, never one the issuing school
> must obtain. Two of them sit in documents that a careless reading would file as issuance gates.

#### 2.1.1 · MBOSE *does* define a Transfer Certificate — and the definition is issuer-neutral

**r.1.2(xxiii)**, verbatim: [A]
> "**'Transfer Certificate' means a Certificate issued to a student by an Institution on seeking transfer to
> another Institution.**"

This is more than a drafting detail, and it sharpens the "who signs" answer rather than leaving it blank:

- **The issuer is "an Institution"** — not an officer. **No designation is named**, so *"who signs, as the source
  words it"* has an answer for Meghalaya and the answer is **the institution, unspecified**. That is a
  **positive** finding of indeterminacy, not a NOT FOUND.
- **The definition contains no countersignature element whatsoever.** A TC is constituted, on MBOSE's own
  definition, by an institution issuing it — full stop. ⇒ **strong internal evidence that the countersignature
  is not part of what makes a TC valid, but an admission-side condition the receiving authority adds.** That is
  the direction finding in §2.1.2 (below) corroborated from the definitions chapter rather than from the operative rules.
- **And it is silent on content.** The Board defines the instrument, regulates its receipt, will lodge an FIR
  over a tampered one — and never says what it must contain. See C-16c.

#### 2.1.2 · Countersignature — inbound, three times, and never on issuance

**This is the most important finding in the file, and its direction is unambiguous.** All three provisions
attach to a certificate **presented to a Meghalaya school or to the Board**. None requires a Meghalaya school
to obtain a countersignature on a TC it issues.

**r.2.1(i)(b) — admission to Class IX. Trigger: inter-State/UT.** [A, verbatim]
> "**Verification:** All supporting documents shall be verified by the Head of the Institution where admission
> is sought. Students transferring from other States/Union Territories shall submit a **Transfer Certificate
> duly countersigned by the competent authority of the respective State/Union Territory**."

⇒ the countersigning authority is the **ORIGIN** jurisdiction's — the Delhi r.139(2) shape.

**r.3.14(c) — change of institution/examination centre. Trigger: cross-*board*.** [A, verbatim]
> "Self-attested copies of Registration Card, Transfer Certificate and Mark-sheet. **Transfer Certificate is
> compulsory for students from other recognised Boards which should be countersigned by the respective DSEO**"

Also required: a photocopy of the parents'/guardian's transfer order (if any); a **no-objection certificate from
the officer-in-charge of the Examination Centre/Institution where the candidate intends to be transferred**; and
prior Board permission before appearing from another centre. Prescribed form at **Annexure-2**.

**r.3.15 — candidates from other States/Boards.** [A, verbatim]
> "(i) **Unsuccessful Candidates of Other Boards:** … shall be required to submit a **Transfer Certificate
> and/or Migration Certificate issued by the concerned Board and duly countersigned by the District School
> Education Officer of the concerned district.** Such candidates shall be permitted to appear only as Regular
> Candidates by obtaining regular admission to Class X in an affiliated school. Admission of such candidates as
> Private Candidates shall not be permitted.
> (ii) **Candidates Not Appearing in Board Examination:** Candidates from other Boards who have not appeared …
> shall be required, at the time of admission, to submit a **Transfer Certificate duly countersigned by the
> District School Education Officer of the concerned district.** Such candidates may be admitted to either
> affiliated or **recognised non-affiliated schools**, in accordance with the applicable admission rules."

**A fourth countersignature, and it is a different object entirely** — r.8.7 requires that an *application* for a
duplicate document be *"countersigned by the Head of the Institution where the candidate last studied or by a
Gazetted Officer"* (Pass Certificate: also an affidavit before an officer not below First Class Magistrate, **or**
a police report). That is a countersignature on **a form, not on a certificate** — a distinct third shape, and it
must not be counted as a TC countersignature.

**And a fifth that is not certificate-related at all:** r.6.12 has an Invigilator countersign a candidate's
confessional statement in an Expulsion Report.

#### 2.1.3 · Format, dues, and the two-kinds-of-recognition point

- **TC format — NOT FOUND, and now a Level-A negative from the Regulations themselves** rather than from the
  absence of a document. `transfer certificate` appears 0 times as a proforma; `leaving certificate` 0 times as a
  school document. **No annexure is a TC.** This is the strongest form the C-16 negative has yet taken.
- ⚠️ **The Annexures are referenced but NOT PUBLISHED.** The table of contents lists **ANNEXURE-1 (p.40),
  ANNEXURE-2 (p.42), ANNEXURE-3 (p.43), ANNEXURE-4 (p.44)**, but the published PDF **ends at printed page 37**
  with the Executive Chairman's signature. Annexure-1 = duplicate-document application; Annexure-2 = change of
  institution/centre; Annexure-4 = undertaking against unfair means; **Annexure-3 is unidentified.**
- **Dues — no provision.** `dues` returns zero. **r.8.2 "Withholding of Results"** withholds *results*, and only
  for non-receipt of internal-assessment data, pending complaints, or mass-copying reports. **It is not a fees
  lever and it is not about certificates.** Do not read it across.
- **Recognition vs affiliation, stated by the Board itself** — r.1.2(iii): *"**'Affiliation' means Conditional
  Affiliation** of Secondary Schools to the Board **after being granted the recognition by the concerned
  authority**."* [A] With r.3.2(ii) (a Regular-1 candidate studies *"in a recognised Secondary School affiliated
  with the Board"*) and r.3.15(ii)'s *"recognised non-affiliated schools"*, MBOSE establishes **two independent
  statuses** — and a live category of school that is recognised but not affiliated.
- **Migration Certificate — the corpus's recorded conflict RESOLVES.** r.1.2(xvi): *"**'Migration Certificate':
  means a Certificate issued by MBOSE** for pursuing further studies after passing the SSLC Examination or its
  equivalent."* r.8.6: *"A Migration Certificate shall be issued to a candidate who has passed the SSLC
  Examination on submission of an application along with the original Registration Card, attested photocopy of
  mark sheet and admit card and the prescribed fee."* [A] **MBOSE does issue them. No TC and no countersignature
  is required to obtain one.** The e-application portal's omission of the service was the misleading signal.
- **UDISE+ / APAAR / DigiLocker — zero occurrences in the Regulations.** (DigiLocker is linked from the mbose.in
  chrome, but no regulation references it.) **MBOSE does not use a verifiable identifier as a substitute for the
  human countersignature — it still requires the human.**
- **A forgery provision worth carrying into the product** — r.9.1: tampering with *"the Admit Card, Mark Sheet,
  Certificate, **Transfer Certificate**, Migration Certificate, or Registration Card"* is deemed furnishing false
  information; the Board rejects the document and may lodge an **FIR**; and forgery *"detected at any time,
  including after the lapse of several years"* remains actionable. ⇒ **MBOSE asserts jurisdiction over a tampered
  TC even though it does not prescribe the TC's format.**
- **HSSLC (Class XII) Regulations — NOT FOUND.** The **entire 253-row notice index (13 pages) and the
  latest-news index were enumerated** and matched against `regulat|transfer|migrat|bye.?law|countersign|
  recogni|affiliat|udise|apaar|admission`. **Exactly two rows matched, both the SSLC instrument above.** A
  reproducible negative.

#### 2.2.1 · The Act

**THE MANIPUR ELEMENTARY AND SECONDARY EDUCATION ACT, 1972 (Manipur Act No. 7 of 1972), incorporated up to the
Fifth Amendment Act, 2004** — 18-page scan, OCR'd in full.
<http://bsem.nic.in/documents/act&rule.pdf> · **Evidence A**

Published by Government of Manipur, Secretariat — Law Department, **Notification No. 3/41/72-Act/L**, Imphal
the **11th August 1972**, the Act having received the Governor's assent on **2nd August 1972**, as *"The Manipur
Secondary Education Act, 1972"* — the word *"Elementary"* enters through later amendment. ⇒ **both short titles
in circulation are correct, at different dates.**

- **s.13(vi)** — the only certificate power: *"to grant certificates to candidates passing the examinations"*.
  **Identical in shape to MBOSE s.12(vi).**
- **s.13(ix)** *"to lay down conditions of recognition of schools for the Elementary Education"*; **s.13(x)**
  *"to recognize schools for the Elementary Education and the Secondary Education"* — both substituted by the
  Fifth Amendment Act 2004. ⇒ **BSEM recognises for elementary as well as secondary.**
- **s.2(k)** — *"**'Recognised' means recognised by the Board for the purpose of admission to the privileges of
  the Board** or prior to recognition by the Board, by any University established by Law in India or by any
  Board recognised by the State Government."* ⇒ **conflict C-2 again: examination recognition, not a licence to
  operate.**
- **s.24(2) — the regulation-making list, enumerated in full and containing NO certificate head:**
  (a) committees; (b) courses of study; (c) marks for passing; (d) examiners and paper-setters;
  (e) conducting examinations and publishing results; **(f) conditions of recognition of schools for imparting
  the Elementary Education and the Secondary Education**; (g) conditions for admission to the Board's
  examinations; (h) malpractice; (i) fees and charges; (j) provident fund; (ja) meeting procedure;
  (k) travelling allowances; (l) delegation; (m) residual. Closing proviso: all regulations *"shall be subject
  to approval by the State Government and published by notification."*
  ⇒ **No transfer-certificate or migration-certificate head exists. Same structural negative as MBOSE s.23(2).**
- **s.28** — the **State Government**, not the Board, makes Rules under the Act.
- **Verified Level-A negatives across the whole extracted Act:** `countersign`, `counter-sign`, `counter sign`,
  `migration`, `transfer certificate` — **all zero**. *"Leaving certificate"* occurs only as the examination
  names in **s.2(g)** (High School Leaving Certificate Examination) and **s.2(h)** (Higher Secondary School
  Leaving Certificate Examination) — **not a document a school issues.** Precisely the MBOSE pattern.
- ⚠️ **The file is named `act&rule.pdf` and the menu calls it "Acts & Rules", but it contains only the ACT** —
  it ends at s.28. **No Rules are published there.** And `/actsandrules/` publishes **exactly one document** —
  that same PDF. [B] ⇒ **BSEM publishes no Rules and no Regulations at all.**

  **The MBOSE theory was tested against BSEM and does not transfer.** Because MBOSE's Regulations turned out to
  be hidden in a notice index rather than a statutory section, `bsem.nic.in` was probed for the same:
  `/notification/`, `/notifications/`, `/notice/`, `/notices/`, `/circular/`, `/circulars/` and
  `/regulations/` **all return 404**. The site is seven static pages (Home, About Us, Recognition Rules, Acts
  and Rules, Right to Education Act, Administration, Downloads, Textbooks, Curriculum) with no notice channel
  at all. ⇒ **there is nowhere else on BSEM's own domain for regulations to be hiding** — which makes this a
  reasonably strong negative rather than an un-searched one, and points the request in §4 row 14 at the Board
  office rather than at the website.

**Corroboration from BSEM's own profile page** [B — <http://bsem.nic.in/about-us/>]: *"By the amendment of the
Act in 2004, besides the academic activities, **the prescription of conditions for granting recognition of
standard I and VIII was also entrusted to the Board of Secondary Education, Manipur**"* — independently
confirming the s.13(ix)/(x) substitutions above. It also records that *"at present **786 high and higher
secondary schools (226 Government, 103 Government aided and 457 Private) are affiliated to the Board** for
certification at end of secondary stage i.e. class X"*, and that the Board *"does not conduct any other public
examination"* beyond Class X.

## 3 · Conflicts to register

### C-26 · UBSE contradicts itself on WHICH document is countersigned, and by WHOM

Both limbs are **Evidence A**, both are UBSE, and both concern the **same transaction** — a name /
father's-name correction in a Board certificate.

| | instrument | document to be countersigned | countersigning officer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **limb 1** | *Procedure (Duplicate Copy, Migration, Correction and Scrutiny)*, p.4 | **the Class 10 T.C.** | **Block Education Officer or Chief Education Officer** |
| **limb 2** | *Correction FORM-A* — the Secretary's circular to Principals, टिप्पणी(2) | **the छात्र-पंजिका (Students' Register)** | **District Education Officer / Regional Girls' Education Officer** |

**Not resolved.** The two may be cumulative (the TC *and* a register extract), or successive editions of the same
requirement, or addressed to different applicant routes (candidate-initiated vs school-initiated). **Nothing read
establishes which**, and the documents carry no dates on their faces. The circular is undated in its printed form
(the letterhead date field reads "201_"), so **it cannot even be established which is later.**

**Consequence for us:** do not encode a single "countersigning officer" for Uttarakhand. **Four designations are
live** — BEO, CEO, DEO and Regional Girls' Education Officer — and the required *document* is itself disputed.
Surface all of it as guidance; enforce none of it.

### C-27 · MBOSE's countersigning officer is ambiguous in a way that matters

**r.2.1(i)(b)** puts the countersignature on *"the competent authority of the **respective State/Union
Territory**"* — unmistakably the **origin** jurisdiction. But **r.3.14(c)** and **r.3.15(i)–(ii)** name *"the
respective **DSEO**"* / *"the **District School Education Officer of the concerned district**"*.

**DSEO is Meghalaya's own district designation** — under the Meghalaya RTE Rules r.11(4) it is the DSEO who
grants recognition. So *"of the concerned district"* reads two ways: the **origin** district's equivalent
officer, or **Meghalaya's own destination-side** DSEO.

**Not resolved, and not resolvable from the text.** Note carefully what is *not* in doubt: **the direction is
inbound either way.** The ambiguity is only about *whose* officer signs, never about whether a Meghalaya school
must obtain a countersignature on a TC it issues — it need not.

**Consequence for us:** the corpus's C-15 model ("countersignature is a property of the destination") survives,
but **the countersigning officer may belong to the origin** even when the *rule* belongs to the destination.
Those are two different fields and a single one will hold the wrong value.

### C-28 · BSEM's recognition stages: the rules page says two, the forms say three

The Recognition Rules page, cl.1(iii), enumerates *"(a) Permission to open class IX. (b) Provisional recognition
of High Schools."* [B] But **FORM No. 3, "Application for Permanent Recognition of High School"**, exists and
requires the notification numbers of *both* prior stages. [A]

**The forms are better evidence than the web page** — they are the Board's own operative instruments, whereas the
page is a web rendering that is **demonstrably truncated** (it stops mid-document at clause 10). Recorded as an
artefact of the truncation rather than a substantive disagreement, **but not resolved**, because the clause that
would establish the third stage is in the unpublished portion.

### C-16c · The "no board prescribes a TC format" negative is CONFIRMED — and for MBOSE it is now a strong negative, not an absent document

The corpus's C-16 family rests in several places on *not having found* a board's regulations. **For MBOSE that
premise is now removed**: the Regulations have been read in full, and they **regulate the TC extensively as an
inbound evidentiary document while prescribing no proforma for it whatsoever**. BSEM's Act likewise carries
**zero** occurrences of `transfer certificate`, and its s.24(2) regulation-making list has no certificate head at
all.

**This strengthens rather than disturbs C-16.** A board that demands a countersigned TC at admission, and that
will lodge an FIR over a tampered one, still **does not say what a TC must contain.** That is now an
evidenced-positive finding about the shape of Indian practice, not an artefact of missing sources.

### C-12a · A sixth printed-identifier mandate — and it is NOT a countersignature substitute

**Manipur RTE Rules 2010, r.23(1) proviso** requires a private school to *"stamp prominently on the certificate
the number of recognition certificate issued by the competent authority."* [A] This joins Karnataka's DISE code,
Tamil Nadu's disputed rule, MP r.19 proviso and CG cl.16(4) as a **printed verifiable identifier**, and it is a
**state** rule — consistent with C-20.

**Two limits, stated so it is not over-read.** First, **it attaches to the certificate of completion of
elementary education, not to a TC** — the corpus's pattern is about TCs, and this is a different document.
Second, **it replaces nothing**: Manipur had no countersignature requirement to begin with, so this is not an
instance of an identifier *displacing* a human check. **The identifier is the recognition-certificate number, not
UDISE+.**

### On UDISE+ specifically — a negative across all three authorities I closed

`UDISE`, `APAAR` and `DigiLocker` return **zero** occurrences in the MBOSE Regulations, in the BSEM Act, and in
the Manipur RTE Rules 2010; nothing in the UBSE instruments references any of them either. **In these three
jurisdictions UDISE+ is NOT used as a substitute for a human verification step.** MBOSE in particular still
requires a live human countersignature on an inbound TC while carrying DigiLocker branding on its website —
**the portal chrome and the regulations have not converged.** The corpus's "five jurisdictions replace
countersignature with a verifiable identifier" pattern **gains no new member here**, and Manipur's r.23
identifier (C-12a) is not one.

### Method limitations that shaped this run — disclosed so the gaps are reproducible

- **`WebSearch` was exhausted (200/200) before this run's searches could be issued.** Every finding above
  therefore comes from **direct `curl` retrieval and document reading**, with no search engine involved. The
  consequence is specific and worth naming: the **case-law and circular limb of the dues question could not be
  attempted at all** for Uttarakhand, Manipur or Meghalaya. The Level-A negatives in row 8 are negatives *within
  the primary instruments* — which is the stronger claim — **but "no court has limited withholding in these
  states" is NOT established and must not be inferred.**
- **macOS Vision OCR has no Devanagari support** (30 languages, none Indic). Hindi documents were therefore
  **read directly as rendered page images** rather than OCR'd. English scans (the BSEM Act, the Manipur RTE
  Rules, the MBOSE notification) were OCR'd with a purpose-built Vision harness.
- **The legacy-font cipher was never transliterated.** The corpus's earlier refusal to guess at the Kruti-Dev-
  style substitution in the UBSE recognition PDF was correct; reading the page images made the question moot.
  **No claim in §2.3 rests on a guessed character mapping.**
- **`https://mbose.in/public/media_file/` returns 403** (no directory listing), and the filenames are Unix
  timestamps. Documents were therefore reached **only through MBOSE's own paginated indexes**, never by guessing
  filenames. This is why row 7's negative is trustworthy and also why it is bounded: **a document never listed in
  an index would not have been found.**

## 5 · What this file should change in the product

1. **Re-mark the MBOSE entry.** The corpus's *"MBOSE regulations exist but appear unpublished — do not encode
   'MBOSE prescribes nothing' as a settled fact"* has done its job and should now be replaced: the Regulations
   are read, and **MBOSE prescribes no TC format but does impose an inbound countersignature.** Those are two
   different facts and both are now Level A.
2. **Countersignature needs a direction field, not a boolean — and a second officer field.** C-27 shows the
   *rule* can belong to the destination while the *officer* belongs to the origin. C-26 shows a single
   jurisdiction can have four live officer designations and a disputed target document.
3. **Never gate issuance on countersignature in any of these five jurisdictions.** Nothing found in this run
   requires an issuing school to obtain one. Two of the provisions recovered would read as issuance gates to a
   careless eye and are not.
4. **Recognition cannot be one record.** UBSE is per examination × per stream × per subject; BSEM is per stage
   by notification number with no expiry; Manipur defers from its RTE rule to a separate state statute. **A
   `recognitionOrder` with a `validUntil` models none of the three.**
5. **Validity must stay absent, not zero.** For all three authorities I closed, the validity term is either
   absent from the rule body or sits in an unpublished form. Per C-6 that is the one place it hides.
