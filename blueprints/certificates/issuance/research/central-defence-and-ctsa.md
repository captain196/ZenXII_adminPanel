### The two things to carry away from this table

> **1. The layer that actually prescribes TC formats in India is absent or contested for these
> systems.** `C-16c` established that prescribed formats are *"overwhelmingly made by STATE
> governments"*. Three of these four systems are **CBSE-affiliated, classified by CBSE as government
> schools, and assert they sit outside state education-department jurisdiction** — so what fills the
> gap is **not** a published format: it is an **unpublished Society circular** (Sainik), **nothing at
> all** (RMS), or a **locally-extended CBSE skeleton** (APS). **A verification system must treat
> "prescribed format" for these tenants as *unknown*, not as *CBSE Annexure-I*.**
>
> **2. "Principal" is safe across every system.** No system, in any document retrieved, names an
> Officiating Principal, Registrar, Commandant, Manager or Headmaster as a certificate signatory. The
> **military rank that prefixes the name** (`Lt Col`, `Gp Capt`, `Brig`) **is not part of the
> designation** and must not be parsed as one.

### 2.1.5 Countersignature

#### OUTBOUND — an exhaustive enumerated negative, not merely "not found"

The phrase `transfer certificate | leaving certificate | school leaving` occurs **exactly four times
in all 178 pages**, and I read every one:

| rule | what it is | direction |
|---|---|---|
| **3.05** | mid-session admission of staff/defence children *"on the basis of transfer certificates"* from *"some recognized school"* | **inbound** |
| **3.23(c)(ii)** | admission scrutiny list: *"Transfer Certificate from the school last attended."* | **inbound** |
| **3.23(e)(i)** | date-of-birth verification (below) | **inbound** |
| **3.34** | the dues/scholarship withholding condition (§2.1.6) | outbound **condition**, not a countersignature |

And `countersign` occurs **11 times — not one concerning a certificate issued to a student**: TA/DA
bills, log books, expense vouchers, monthly accounts, stock statements, condemnation vouchers, a
displayed notice, the **Income Certificate**, and the 3.23(e)(i) rule.

> **Stated as strongly as the evidence allows: there is NO outbound countersignature requirement for a
> Sainik School transfer / school leaving certificate anywhere in the Sainik Schools Society Rules.
> [A — exhaustive grep of the primary text.]**
>
> **This remains an absence in the Society's rules, not a clause abolishing countersignature.** What
> reaches a Sainik School from outside is **CBSE Annexure-I's footnote** — and CBSE has abolished that
> requirement comprehensively and by name (`central-and-boards.md` §C).

**A near-miss that must not be misread.** The Society's minutes show Sainik Schools sit under a
**Chairman, LBA** (Local Board of Administration) — 47th AISSPC §(h): *"the Schools should utilise the
expertise available under the jurisdiction of **Chairman, LBA** and District Administration"* [A]; 51st
AISSPC Agenda No 25: *"JAG representatives are present at the **Chairman LBA office**"* [A]; and
**Rule 1.08** makes the arrangement constitutional (§2.1.8). **A Chairman LBA is the structurally
obvious countersigner — and no document found gives him any role on a certificate.** Recorded so a
later reader does not supply the missing link by inference. *(Contrast AWES, where a Chairman
countersignature **is** prescribed — §2.3.3.)*

#### INBOUND — narrower than it first looks

**Rule 3.23(e)**, verbatim, under *"Verification of dates of births will be carried out as under:-"*:

> **"(i) Boys who have previously attended schools recognised by the State Board of Secondary
> Education** — Date of Birth indicated in the Transfer Certificate issued by the previous School
> **duly countersigned by the District Education Officer** should be accepted."

Two points, both important:
1. It is **purely inbound** — a rule about a TC *presented to* a Sainik School. **It must never be
   cited as an outbound rule.**
2. **It is conditioned on the feeder school being state-board-recognised.** It is *not* a general

### 2.1.7 Withdrawal on disciplinary grounds — the rule, an unpublished endorsement duty, and a child-welfare collision

**Rule 3.32, *"WITHDRAWAL ON DISCIPLINARY GROUNDS"*, verbatim [A]:**

> "3.32 **The Principal may, in the interest of the school order withdrawal of a boy from the school,
> should the boy's conduct, behaviour or influence, in the opinion of the Principal, be detrimental to
> the general discipline/interest of the school.** In such a case, refund of scholarship may not be
> insisted. The Principal should, however, report such withdrawals to the **Local Board of
> Administration** at its next meeting and to the **Honorary Secretary** immediately, who on the merits
> of the case will decide as to whether the scholarship money with damages, if any, need be recovered
> from the parent."

**And the 49th AISSPC, SS Chittorgarh, 26–27 Sep 19, Agenda Point No 4** (SS Kodagu) [A]:

> "**Discussion:** Principal Sainik School Kodagu apprised that **as per Rule 3.32, a cadet can be
> withdrawn on disciplinary grounds by the Principal** if the boy's behaviour is detrimental to the
> general discipline of the school. Also, **as per instructions on the matter, Transfer Certificate is
> to be issued with an endorsement "on discipline grounds"**. However, in the light of JJ Act, RTE,
> POCSO and other relevant rules, children are to be counselled and referred to Child rehabilitation
> centre… Accordingly, **withdrawal on Disciplinary Grounds by the Sainik Schools has not been
> accepted by CWC stating that education cannot be denied.** They conveyed that 'corrective
> punishments' are to be given to such cadets.
> **Decision:** … the cadets must be counselled, warned and an undertaking from parents could be
> taken… A committee of Principals of Sainik School Gopalganj, Tilaiya and Ghorakhal has been
> constituted…"

Three findings:
- **The TC-issuing act is the Principal's**, confirmed from the rule side.
- **There is a Society instruction on TC CONTENT** — a mandatory endorsement *"on discipline
  grounds"*. It is referred to but **not numbered or quoted**, and **Rule 3.32 itself says nothing
  about it**. The instruction was **NOT FOUND** as a document.
- **A Child Welfare Committee has refused to accept disciplinary withdrawal by Sainik Schools** —
  *"education cannot be denied."* A product must not treat the endorsement as routinely available.

**On academic withdrawal**, MoD/SSS letter **No.30(1)2003/D(SSC) dated 17.05.05** directs Principals
that *"action for withdrawal is taken in extreme situations when all other efforts have failed"*,

### 2.3.7 Governance chain — three tiers, and "LBA" is the wrong word for APS

Named in terms by the Allahabad High Court, ***Ashok Kumar Upadhyaya v. Union of India***, 24 September
2004, 2005(1)AWC196, para 9 [A]:

> "A **three-tier command and control system** is provided to run the schools/institutions. The **Board
> of Governance, and its Executive Committee at Army Headquarters is the first tier**. The **Board of
> Administration at Headquarters Command is the second tier** and the **Managing Committee, at the
> station where the school is located, is the third tier**."

- **Tier 1 — Board of Governors + Executive Committee, Army HQ.** Red Book **7th Edition (25.03.2019),
  Chapter 2**, recited verbatim by the Rajasthan High Court, **[2026:RJ-JP:1557]**, 15 January 2026,
  para 21 [A]: **President — Chief of the Army Staff**; **Vice Presidents** — Vice Chief of the Army
  Staff and the seven GOCs-in-C; **Members** — Adjutant General, Quartermaster General,
  Engineer-in-Chief, DG Discipline Ceremonials & Welfare; **Member Secretary — Managing Director,
  AWES**.
  ⚠️ **Correction to a common description.** AWES's **website alone** says *"the Board of Governors
  (BOG) with the Chief of Army Staff as the **Patron-in-Chief** and Army Commanders as **Patrons**"*
  [B]; **the rulebook and the Memorandum say President / Vice President** [A]. AWES's site also says
  the Executive Committee is *"headed by the Adjutant General"* [B], but Chapter 2 lists the AG merely
  as a **Member** [A, 2019] while the 2004 recital called him *"Chairman of the Governing Body"* [A,
  2004]. **Do not assert AG chairmanship as current.**
- **Tier 2 — Board of Administration, HQ Command**: *"the **GOC-in-Command and the Chief of Staff**"*
  (Rajasthan HC para 22) [A]. At APS Dagshai: *"Patron Bd Of Adm – GOC-in-C, Western Comd"*, *"Chairman
  Bd Of Adm – COS, Western Comd"* [B].
- **Tier 3 — Managing Committee / SAMC, at the station, chaired by the local Station Commander, a
  Brigadier.** Central Information Commission, ***Amrita Ganga v. Genl. B.C. Joshi Army Public
  School***, CIC/WB/C/2008/000365, 12 January 2010, para 6, verbatim [A]: *"Needless to say, **the
  Chairman of the Managing Committee of the School is the Station Commander, Pithoragarh. He is a
  serving Brigadier of the Indian Army.**"* Corroborated by the Supreme Court in ***D.S. Grewal v. Vimmi
  Joshi***, 17 December 2008, para 4 [A]: *"Appellant (Brig. D.S. Grewal …) is the **Chairman of the
  School Managing Committee** while Col. Hitendra Bahadur … is the **Vice Chairman**"*. Current usage is
  **SAMC**: AWES's site [B] — *"administered by local formation commanders through **School
  Administration and Management Committees (SAMC)**"* — and Rajasthan HC para 6 [A].

> ⚠️ **"LBA" is folklore for APS — and the distinction is legally load-bearing.** Indian Kanoon
> exact-phrase counts [A]: `"Local Board of Administration"` = **143** documents; `+ "Sainik School"` =
> **142**; `+ "Army Public School"` = **no matching results**; `+ "Army Welfare Education Society"` =
> **no matching results**. **LBA is Sainik Schools Society vocabulary** — confirmed independently at
> §2.1.8 by **Rule 1.08**. **APS use Managing Committee / SAMC.**
>
> **And the systems' legal characters differ at the root:** the **Sainik Schools Society is "State"**
> (*All India Sainik Schools Employees' Assn. v. Defence Minister-cum-Chairman BoG*, AIR 1989 SC 88),
> whereas **AWES is NOT "State" under Article 12** — Supreme Court, ***Army Welfare Education Society v.
> Sunil Kumar Sharma***, Civil Appeal Nos. 7256–7259 of 2024, **2024 INSC 501, 9 July 2024**, para 42
> [A]: *"the High Court committed an egregious error in entertaining the writ petition … holding that
> the appellant society is a "State" within Article 12"*.

## 2.4 · CTSA — CENTRAL TIBETAN SCHOOLS ADMINISTRATION, AND THE SAMBHOTA TRANSFER

> ### The framing question, answered — and the answer is not the one the brief anticipated
>
> The brief asked whether **the issuing authority moved with the schools**. It did not — **because
> CTSA never held one.**
>
> **CTSA's own registered objects (1961), clause IV**, as reproduced in MoE Annual Report 2018-19
> (p. 120) and 2020-21 (p. 288) [A]:
>
> > "To control and supervise education discipline, boarding and lodging, health and hygiene and
> > general progress of the students and the staff in the CTSA schools **and to get affiliation of the
> > schools from any association, society or body that conducts public examinations for the purpose of
> > preparing the students for such examinations;**"
>
> The architecture was always: **CBSE = the examining and certifying authority; CTSA = the society
> that obtained affiliation and managed the schools.** The 2013–2021 transfers moved the **managing
> society**. They did not, and could not, transfer a certificate-issuing power, **because CTSA held
> none to transfer.**
>
> **That is why every transfer instrument is silent on certificates — there was nothing to say.** The
> silence is structurally correct drafting, not an omission.
>
> ⇒ **A TC from one of these schools is, and always was, issued at school level under CBSE's
> bye-laws, by the head of the institution.** What changed is **which society employs that head** —
> not the authority under which the certificate is issued. **[A on the objects, the closure and the
> bye-laws; the synthesis is a reading, not a quoted determination — see the residue at §2.4.7.]**

### 2.4.1 CTSA no longer exists — and KVS is the surviving Indian authority

# §3 · WHAT THIS MEANS FOR AN ISSUER-VERIFICATION SYSTEM

## 3.1 These systems break the state→school model the corpus was built on

Every state stream in this corpus resolved the same way: **the state prescribes the certificate, the
board supplies an identifier** (`boards-west-south.md` headline; `C-16c`). All four systems here are
**CBSE-affiliated and classified by CBSE as government schools**, and three of them **assert they sit
outside state education-department jurisdiction**. So the layer that actually prescribes formats is
absent or contested for them, and what replaces it is **not a published format**:

| system | what actually fills the format gap |
|---|---|
| **Sainik Schools** | an **unpublished Society circular** (formats created by a Board of Officers and circulated 2025-26) |
| **New Sainik Schools** | the **host school's own board**, which varies school to school |
| **RMS** | **nothing** — each school drafts its own; three schools, three titles, 21/22/15 fields |
| **AWES / APS** | **CBSE Annexure-I as a skeleton, locally extended to 25 fields** |
| **CTSA / Sambhota** | **CBSE Annexure-I**, by virtue of affiliation and nothing else |

> **Design rule.** A tenant in one of these systems must resolve to a profile that **enforces no
> format**, exactly as Maharashtra, Karnataka and Uttar Pradesh already do for want of a verified
> authority (`README.md` §1). **Do not default them to CBSE Annexure-I as a *requirement*:** RMS's
> three schools demonstrably do not use it, Sainik Schools now have a Society format of unknown
> content, and APS use an extended variant. Annexure-I is the right *skeleton* to offer and the wrong
> thing to *enforce*.

## 3.2 Signatory: `Principal` is safe across all five — and that is a real result

No system, in any document retrieved, names an **Officiating Principal, Registrar, Commandant, Manager
or Headmaster** as a certificate signatory.

- **Sainik**: Society correspondence addressed to *"Principal, All Sainik Schools"* (1997→2026); Rule
  3.32 vests withdrawal in the Principal. [A]
- **RMS**: printed on eight certificates; the Principal also signs *"for and on behalf of the president
  of India"*. [A]
- **AWES**: nine published certificates across four schools, all **Principal**; the governing rule is
  CBSE's *"regular Principal/Vice Principal"*. [A]
- **CTSA / Sambhota**: no Tibetan-side rule exists; **CBSE's Annexure-I supplies the authority**. [A]

> **An allow-list of `Principal` (plus `Vice Principal`, which Annexure-I's note expressly permits) is
> safe for these systems.** Two cautions:
> 1. **The military rank that prefixes the name is NOT part of the designation.** `Lt Col Principal`,
>    `Gp Capt`, `Brig` — do not parse these as offices, and **do not reject a certificate because its
>    signatory reads "Lt Col Principal"**.
> 2. **Do not read a Sainik "Registrar" or "Headmaster" as a signatory.** They are real offices
>    (= Administrative Officer and Vice Principal respectively, per SS Bhubaneswar's RTI disclosure),
>    and **no rule lets either sign a TC**.

## 3.3 Countersignature: model DIRECTION as a first-class field, or the data is unusable

This scope produced **six distinct shapes** of countersignature rule. They are not interchangeable:

| shape | instance | direction |
|---|---|---|
| Issuer **disclaims** it, printed on the instrument | RMS Dholpur's N.B.; all nine APS certificates | **outbound** |
| **Internal governance** countersignature **required**, scoped by class and destination | AWES letter B/45751/CBSE/AWES — **classes I–VIII, APS→non-APS, by the school Chairman** | **outbound** |
| Receiving school demands it **unconditionally** | RMS Bengaluru document list item 4 | **inbound** |
| Receiving school demands it **with a CBSE carve-out** | RMS Bengaluru Admission Requisites 3(b); APS *"except for Army School/APSs/KVs"* | **inbound** |
| Receiving body demands only the **issuing Principal's own attestation** | BSEH Reg. 13(I)(B)(iii); Sainik Rule 3.23(e)(i) (state-board feeders only) | **inbound** |
| Board countersigns **outbound** on request | TSBIE — already in the register as **C-15b** | **outbound** |

> **This vindicates and extends the C-15/C-15b taxonomy.** A model with a single boolean
> `countersignature_required` **has nowhere to put any of these six**, and would record whichever it
> read last. The field must carry at minimum:
> **direction** (outbound / inbound) · **who** (designation **and whose organisation** — an education
> department? the school's own governing board?) · **trigger** (board boundary? state boundary? class
> range? destination type? unconditional?) · **source** (is the rule the issuer's or the receiver's?).
>
> **AWES is the case that proves the point.** Its certificates print *"need not be counter signed by
> the **State Education Authorities**"* **while** an AWES letter requires a **Chairman**
> countersignature for classes I–VIII leaving for a non-APS school. **Those are not contradictory** —
> one denies an *external* countersignature, the other imposes an *internal governance* one. A boolean
> collapses them into a falsehood either way.

### The conflation trap, three times in this scope
1. **BSEH Reg. 13(I)(B)(iii)** requires a Sainik School SLC *"duly attested by the Principal, of the
   School concerned"* and separately that the pupil be *"adjudged on merit to be a fit student … **by
   the District Education Officer**"*. The DEO performs a **merit adjudication of the pupil**, **not**
   a countersignature of the document. A skim-read yields *"Haryana requires DEO countersignature on a
   Sainik School TC"* — **which is false.** *(The corpus read this correctly at
   `boards-north-central.md` §2.4; this stream confirms it and names the trap.)*
2. **The only countersignature the Sainik intake pack actually enforces is on the Income
   Certificate** — *"COUNTERSIGNED / Signature of the DC/SDO/Ist Class Magistrate"*, rule book
   Appendix 'E'. **Easy to misfile as a TC countersignature. It is not one.** [A]
3. **Both defence systems have an LBA Chairman**, and **RMS's LBA *is* the CBSE-facing School Managing
   Committee** — and **CBSE's 2014 circular step 6 does permit a Managing-Committee countersignature**
   (`central-and-boards.md` §C.3). **But neither system's rules give the LBA Chairman any role on a
   certificate.** The missing link must not be supplied by inference. *(In AWES, where a Chairman
   countersignature genuinely is prescribed, the body is a **Managing Committee / SAMC**, not an
   "LBA" — see 3.7.)*

## 3.4 Recognition: the instrument is usually not the one the label says

**Every system here files, under a CBSE disclosure slot, a document of a different legal nature than
the label:**

| slot label | what is actually in it |
|---|---|
| *"NOC issued by the State Govt./UT"* | MoD **2007 renaming letter** (RMS Bengaluru) · **CBSE** affiliation-fee letter (RMS Chail) · a **State Gazette notification under an MoA** (SS Kalikiri) |
| *"Trust/Society Registration Certificate"* | 2003 **LBA composition** letter (RMS Bengaluru) · **Appendix A of the SSS Manual** incl. the 1961 registration certificate (SS Kalikiri) |
| *"Recognition Certificate under RTE Act 2009"* | RTE **exemption** correspondence (both RMS) · the **Jan-2013 CBSE recategorisation letter** (SS Satara, SS Kunjpura) · **"NA"** (SS Kalikiri, SS Chittorgarh, APS Devlali) · *"RTE Act is not applicable to Sainik Schools"* (SS Goalpara) |
| *"DEO Certificate"* | **HTTP 404** (RMS Bengaluru) |

[A] **Anyone assessing these schools by slot *label* would conclude RMS holds a state NOC, a trust
registration and an RTE recognition certificate. It holds none of the three.** **Slot contents must be
read, never trusted.**

### What this does to `Issuer_identity`

`Issuer_identity` models recognition as *"validity in academic years, classes and sections covered,
granting society, premises, and the 90-day renewal window"* (`README.md` §2). **None of that fits.**

- **There is often no renewal at all.** **CBSE Affiliation Bye-Law 2.2.6**, read directly: *"**One time
  regular Affiliation** will be granted to the categories of schools under clauses 2.1.1, 2.1.2,
  **2.1.3** and 2.1.4"* — which covers the **Sainik Schools Society (2.1.3)**, **CTSO (2.1.3)** and
  **RMS (2.1.4)**. Confirmed in the instruments: *"one time Regular Affiliation, **without any need
  for extension/ renewal**"* (RMS, 2017) and *"schools run by Govt./Govt. Aided are granted regular
  affiliation"* (SS Kalikiri, 2016). **A 90-day renewal prompt would fire forever against a school
  that has nothing to renew.**
- **"Granting society" can be nothing.** For RMS, CBSE's own record reads society registration number
  **"NA"**, body **"MOD GOVT OF INDIA"**, validity **"PERMANENT REGISTRATION"**. **The school is the
  Union.**
- **The state instrument, where one exists, has no expiry and no scope.** SS Kalikiri's is a **Gazette
  notification under a bilateral MoA** between the President of India and the Governor.
- **And one system's issuer is defunct with a named successor**: CTSA, closed 31.03.2022, residuary
  work to **KVS**.

> **The model therefore needs a recognition *kind*** — at least
> `state_recognition | cbse_regular_affiliation | moa_plus_gazette_notification | affiliation_only | none_asserted`
> — **and must tolerate *no expiry date at all* without treating that as missing data.** It also needs
> an **issuer lifecycle**: `live | closed_with_successor | transferred_to_other_society`.

## 3.5 The recognition question is NOT settled — and CBSE's own rules cut against the schools

Three CBSE instruments, all read directly on this pass, point the opposite way from the schools'
assertions:

1. **Affiliation Bye-Law 2.3.4**: *"**The Schools** seeking affiliation with the Board shall submit
   formal prior **Recognition Certificate from concerned State Education Department as per extant
   rules and provisions contained in RTE Act-2009**."* — **unqualified, no category list.** Contrast
   **2.3.5** (state NOC), which *is* limited to categories **2.1.5–2.1.8** and therefore **does not
   reach** Sainik (2.1.3) or RMS (2.1.4). **The drafting distinction is deliberate.**
2. **The Jan-2013 recategorisation letter**: the managing society *"shall now be responsible to develop
   and adopt well defined rules … **in conformity to State / U.T / Centre Education Act** / Affiliation
   Bye Laws"*, **and must notify them publicly**.
3. **Examination Bye-Laws 8(i) and 8(viii)**: the **Admission Register form** and the **disciplinary
   power** default to *"the **State Government concerned**"* / *"the **Education Act of the
   State/Union Territory**"* — naming **KVS and NVS** as the only central alternatives.

> ⇒ **Two consequences, and they cut in opposite directions.**
> **(a)** Schools answering *"NOT APPLICABLE"* on the **NOC** row (Chittorgarh, Kapurthala, Kunjpura)
> are **correct** — 2.3.5 does not reach a 2.1.3 school. What looked like inconsistency is, on that
> row, **correct compliance**.
> **(b)** Schools answering *"NA"* or *"RTE Act is not applicable to Sainik Schools"* on the
> **RECOGNITION** row, and **RMS Dholpur's printed claim to sit outside state jurisdiction**, are
> **not supported by CBSE's own current bye-laws.**
>
> **But CBSE is a board, not the State**, and a bye-law condition is not a determination of RTE s.18's
> reach. `boards-central-completion.md` §2.3 establishes that **s.18 turns on whether the school is
> "established, owned or controlled by the appropriate Government"** — and for a Sainik School or an
> RMS the appropriate Government is the **Centre** (s.2(a)(i)), which may well make an exemption good.
> **Nobody official has said so.** ⇒ **Code no RTE exemption for any of these systems, in either
> direction.** See §4 C-47, C-51.

## 3.6 Two-layer affiliation: the New Sainik Schools case

A **New Sainik School** holds **two affiliations of different kinds simultaneously** [A]:
1. **affiliation to the Sainik Schools Society** — provisional, then regular, and revocable; and
2. **affiliation to its own education board** — CBSE **or a state board or ICSE or IB or IGCSE or
   GCSE** (R&R 2.2), **exactly one** (2.6.1), varying school to school.

**Neither substitutes for the other, and only the second bears on the certificate.**

> **A `school_type = "sainik"` flag is actively misleading for the 86 NSS.** The certificate regime
> for an NSS follows its **host board and host state** — which for the Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu
> and Kerala NSS means the **state-prescribed formats this corpus has already researched**
> (Maharashtra SS Code r.32.1 + Appendix Four; Gujarat નમૂનો-૯ reg 12(14); TNER Appendix-5/5-A; Kerala
> Form 5). **The distinguishing field is who owns the school, not what it is called.** See §4 C-48.

## 3.8 The trend worth building toward: identifiers, not signatures

**Every system in this file has independently substituted a machine-checkable identifier, or
publication, for a human countersignature** — see §4 **C-55** for the evidence table. **APS print the
UDISE code on the certificate itself**; **RMS demand PEN + UDISE on TCs presented to them**; **NVS
waives countersignature outright *because* the school is on UDISE**; **CBSE's Annexure-I header mandates
the Affiliation No. and School Code on the face of the certificate.**

> ⚠️ **With one caveat this pass proved empirically.** The **publication** limb **expires**. APS
> Gopalpur's register still lists 14+ certificates for 2026-27 by serial, name and class, and **every
> file returns HTTP 404** — the AWES letter's *"available on the school website for a period of **4
> months**"* is actually enforced. **A verification route that depends on the issuer continuing to host
> the document has a four-month half-life, after which the index survives and the evidence does not.**
> An identifier printed on the certificate does not decay that way. **Prefer the identifier.**

## 3.9 What must NOT be built

1. **Do not offer a countersignature field to a CBSE tenant as a requirement.** CBSE abolished it
   comprehensively and by name, through 31.10.2025 (`central-and-boards.md` §C). All systems here are
   CBSE-affiliated. The most that survives is the **permissive** Managing-Committee countersignature of
   the 2014 circular step 6 — plus, for APS only, the **narrow, unpublished** AWES Chairman rule.
2. **Do not enforce a dues gate — except note that Sainik Schools genuinely have one, and it is
   contestable.** **Rule 3.34 is real, central and operated.** But Sainik Schools admit at **Class VI**,
   so Classes VI–VIII sit inside RTE's elementary stage where **s.5(3) requires the TC "immediately"**;
   and a **Child Welfare Committee has already refused** a Sainik School's withdrawal on the ground that
   *"education cannot be denied"*. ⇒ **Record Rule 3.34 as a published rule in conflict with a statute.
   Do not code it as an enforceable gate, and do not code it as void.** For RMS, CTSA and the NSS **no
   dues rule was found at all.** See §4 C-46.
3. **Do not invent a form number, a field list, or a clause reference.** A Society-prescribed Sainik TC
   format **exists** as of May 2026 and is **not published**. The correct behaviour is *"a prescribed
   format exists; its content is unknown to us"*.
4. **Do not clear `fieldListVerified` on CBSE Annexure-I until the field list is fixed.** The corpus's
   current list is the **pre-2012** form and is wrong in six ways — §4 **C-45** sets out the repairs.
5. **Do not match issuers on organisation name alone.** One Tibetan lineage carries **four** names —
   *CTSA*, CBSE's *"CTSO"*, *STSS*, and the *CTA Department of Education* — plus **KVS** as statutory
   successor. Sainik Schools carry **two layers** of affiliation. **Name matching will fail.**
6. **Do not treat a published "TC sample" as a blank proforma.** In **all three** systems that publish
   them, they are **real completed certificates naming minors** — full name, parents' names, date of
   birth, caste category, **PEN and UDISE numbers**. Anything that ingests them ingests children's
   personal data.

## C-46 · Sainik Rule 3.34 withholds the TC for dues — and Sainik Schools admit at Class VI, inside RTE's elementary stage

**Both limbs are Level A. The collision is real and unresolved.**

- **Sainik Schools Society Rules, Rule 3.34:** *"**The school leaving / transfer certificate will,
  however, not be issued until all dues are cleared to the school and the scholarship money, if
  enjoyed by the student, is refunded.**"* [A — read from the primary rule book]
- **Rule 1.10:** *"Sainik Schools admit boys in **Classes VI & IX**."* [A]
- **RTE Act 2009, s.5(3):** the head-teacher *"shall **immediately** issue the transfer certificate"*,
  with no condition. **s.2(f):** *"elementary education" means the education from first class to eighth
  class.* [A — both quoted in the corpus]

**⇒ Classes VI, VII and VIII at every Sainik School sit squarely inside RTE's elementary stage, and
Rule 3.34 on its face authorises exactly what s.5(3) forbids.**

**This is the same shape as the conflict already registered at `central-and-boards.md` §B1.6** (CBSE
bye-law r.8(vi) *"on a payment of all dues"* vs RTE s.5(3)) — **but it is worse here in three ways:**
1. r.8(vi) is a **board bye-law**; **Rule 3.34 is the rule of the body that owns and runs the
   schools.**
2. **It is actively operated** — the 47th AISSPC (2017) records recovery *"before issuing them
   transfer certificate"*; **SS Kunjpura makes parents pre-sign the undertaking** on the TC application
   form; **SS Chittorgarh and SS Tilaiya restate it publicly.**
3. **Rule 3.34's second limb — scholarship refund — has no analogue in r.8(vi) at all**, and is the
   limb actually enforced in practice. **Rule 3.35** reinforces it: scholarship holders *"can withdraw
   them **only after repayment of the total amount of scholarship enjoyed**"*.

**A third party has already pushed back on the adjacent rule.** On disciplinary withdrawal under Rule
3.32, the 49th AISSPC records: *"withdrawal on Disciplinary Grounds by the Sainik Schools **has not
been accepted by CWC stating that education cannot be denied**."* [A] **A Child Welfare Committee
refusing a Sainik School's withdrawal is evidence these rules are contestable in practice, not merely
in theory.**

**Resolution on the law is not genuinely in doubt** — a statute overrides a society's internal rule,
and RTE s.5 covers I–VIII. **But Rule 3.34 is not thereby void for Classes IX–XII**, where s.5 does not
reach and where the rule still speaks — and the 2017 minutes show the practice is concentrated
**post-Class X**, precisely where RTE does not bite. Note also that **s.5(1)/(2) excludes transfers
*into* specified-category schools** (which s.2(p) names Sainik Schools as being), but the corpus's own
reading is that **s.5(3)'s duty is drafted on the school the child last attended**, and it adopts the
safe construction: **never block a TC below Class IX, whatever the destination**
(`boards-central-completion.md` §2.2). **That standing rule is correct and this finding reinforces
it.**

> **Recorded, not resolved. Do not code Rule 3.34 as an enforceable gate. Do not code it as void.**
