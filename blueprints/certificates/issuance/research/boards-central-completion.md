# §2 · CROSS-CUTTING — the RTE recognition question, and a NOT FOUND now CLOSED

**Evidence A.** RTE Act 2009 statutory text read directly at
<https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Right_of_Children_to_Free_and_Compulsory_Education_Act,_2009>,
corroborated independently against India Code's official section record (`s18.json`, Ministry of
Education) and `indiankanoon.org/doc/186552640/` + `/doc/125645684/`. Three independent renderings of
s.18 agree verbatim.

## 2.1 This CLOSES the NOT FOUND left open at `central-and-boards.md` §A1.1 point 6

That file said of the s.5 carve-out:

> "**The precise practical effect of that carve-out is NOT FOUND** — I did not verify s.2(n)
> sub-clause text on this pass, and it should be read before any rule is coded on it."

**It is now read.**

**s.2(n) — "school"**, verbatim:

> "school" means **any recognised school imparting elementary education** and includes— **(i)** a school
> established, owned or controlled by the appropriate Government or a local authority; **(ii)** an aided
> school receiving aid or grants to meet whole or part of its expenses from the appropriate Government
> or the local authority; **(iii) a school belonging to specified category**; and **(iv) an unaided
> school** not receiving any kind of aid or grants to meet its expenses from the appropriate Government
> or the local authority.

**s.2(p) — "specified category"**, verbatim:

> "specified category", in relation to a school, means a school known as **Kendriya Vidyalaya, Navodaya
> Vidyalaya, Sainik School** or any other school having a distinct character.

**s.2(a) — "appropriate Government"**, verbatim:

> **(i)** in relation to a school established, owned or controlled by the **Central Government**, or the
> administrator of the Union territory having no legislature, **the Central Government**; **(ii)** in
> relation to a school other than the school referred to in sub-clause (i), established within the
> territory of— (A) a State, **the State Government**; (B) a Union territory having legislature, the
> Government of that Union territory.

**s.2(f)**: *"elementary education" means the education from first class to eighth class.*
**s.2(c)**: *"child" means a male or female child of the age of six to fourteen years.*

## 2.2 What the carve-out actually does

RTE **s.5(1) and 5(2)** grant the right to seek transfer *"to any other school, **excluding the school
specified in sub-clauses (iii) and (iv) of clause (n) of section 2**"*. Substituting the definitions
just read, the excluded **destinations** are:

- **(iii) specified-category schools — Kendriya Vidyalayas, Navodaya Vidyalayas and Sainik Schools *by
  name***, plus any other school "having a distinct character"; and
- **(iv) unaided private schools** — the category almost every IB and Cambridge school in India occupies.

> **The statutory transfer right runs only *to* government and aided schools.** It creates **no** right
> to transfer *into* a KV, a JNV, a Sainik School, or an unaided private school. Those keep their own
> admission processes — KV's priority categories, the JNVST, the AISSEE, a private school's own
> admission process subject to s.13.

**The limit of that reading — INFERENCE, flagged.** The carve-out is drafted on the **destination** in
5(1)/(2). **s.5(3)'s duty — "the Head-teacher … shall immediately issue the transfer certificate" — is
drafted on the school the child was *last admitted to*, and opens "For seeking admission in such other
school"**, referring back to the carved-out-destination right. A literal chain therefore suggests the
s.5(3) duty may not be engaged where the destination is excluded. **I do not assert this and the
product must not act on it.** The safe construction is the one `central-and-boards.md` already adopts:
**never block a TC below Class IX, whatever the destination.** Recorded only so a later reader is not
surprised by the text.

## 2.3 Recognition — specified-category schools are NOT expressly exempt

**s.18(1) exempts exactly one class of school and no other:**

> "No school, **other than a school established, owned or controlled by the appropriate Government or
> the local authority**, shall … be established or function, without obtaining a certificate of
> recognition…"

**Verified negative: there is no exemption anywhere in the Act for "specified category" schools.** The
only occurrences of the phrase are **s.2(n)(iii)** and **s.2(p)**; neither s.18 nor s.19 mentions them.
**There is likewise no curriculum-based carve-out** — nothing in s.18 makes any allowance for a foreign
curriculum.

**So whether a KV / JNV / Sainik School needs a state recognition certificate turns entirely on one
question: is it "established, owned or controlled by the appropriate Government"?** Note that s.18(1)
keys off the **ownership/control facts**, not off which sub-clause of s.2(n) the school sits in — so
the s.2(n)(iii) classification does not by itself settle s.18.

**⚠️ A correction to the intuitive answer, and it cuts against exemption.** The obvious reading is
that each body is wholly government-funded with its governing body chaired by the Union Minister —
i.e. **"controlled by"** the Central Government, the easiest of s.18(1)'s three limbs to satisfy — so
the appropriate Government is the Central Government (s.2(a)(i)) and the school sits outside s.18(1).
**But the Act's own structure shows it does NOT treat KVs and JNVs as s.2(n)(i) schools:**

- **s.12(1)(a)** imposes on **s.2(n)(i)** schools the duty to educate **all** admitted children free.
- **s.12(1)(c)** imposes on **s.2(n)(iii) and (iv)** schools the **25% weaker-section intake** duty:
  > "(c) specified in sub-clauses **(iii) and (iv)** of clause (n) of section 2 shall admit in class I,
  > to the extent of at least **twenty-five per cent.** of the strength of that class, children
  > belonging to weaker section and disadvantaged group in the neighbourhood…"
- **KVS openly performs the 25% duty on itself** — Admission Guidelines FAQ,
  <https://kvsangathan.nic.in/en/admission-guidelines/>, Q1 *"Whether KVS follows provisions of
  admission under RTE Act?"*: **"10 seats (out of 40 seats) in class I per section are to be filled as
  per RTE provisions (25% seats)…"** **Evidence A.**

> **A s.2(n)(i) school would owe s.12(1)(a), not a 25% quota.** KVS performing s.12(1)(c) is direct
> evidence that the Act places KVs in the **specified-category** box, *not* in the
> government-school box — which weakens, though does not defeat, the argument that they are
> "established, owned or controlled by the appropriate Government" for s.18(1) purposes.

> **OPEN — NOT RESOLVED, and now more genuinely open than it first appeared.** **No MoE, MHRD, KVS,
> NVS or NCPCR clarification, circular, FAQ or judgment on whether a KV or JNV must hold a State
> recognition certificate was found** (see §9). Recorded as **INFERENCE**. **Do not encode a
> recognition rule for KV/JNV/Sainik tenants on this research, in either direction.**

**⚠️ One drafting point recorded because a confident wrong quotation is exactly the failure mode this
file exists to prevent:** s.18(1) does **not** open with "save as otherwise provided in clause (n) of
section 2" or any similar cross-reference. Its carve-out is a **direct substantive exclusion** of *"a
school established, owned or controlled by the appropriate Government or the local authority"*. **Do
not cite the cross-reference form; it is not the law.**

## 2.4 The other side — s.2(n)'s opening words

Note *"'school' means **any recognised school** imparting elementary education"*. An **unrecognised**
institution is not a "school" for the Act's purposes at all. That is the textual root of why an
unrecognised institution's certificates have no standing, and it corroborates from the definitional
side the point `central-and-boards.md` §A1.3 makes from the receiving side (CBSE bye-law 6.1
Explanation (a) refuses admission on an unrecognised institution's certificates).

**⚠️ Scope caveat that must not be skipped.** RTE is an **elementary education** statute (Art. 21A;
s.2(f) fixes classes I–VIII). Whether s.18 recognition reaches **classes IX–XII**, and how it interacts
with state secondary-education Acts, is a genuinely separate question this pass did not close. The
per-state instruments are covered in the sibling files (`north-india.md`, `south-india.md`, etc.).

## 3.3 A NIOS learner moving to a conventional school

**After passing:** the **Migration / Migration-cum-Transfer Certificate** (§14.3, D3 §4.5) plus the
**Marks Statement-cum-Certificate** (§14.1).

**Mid-stream — this is the hard case, and it is a gap, not a rule.**

- **No NIOS document exists for a non-completing learner.** §14.1 gives a physical certificate only on
  an overall pass; §14.3 gives a Migration Certificate only to one who "has passed". A learner who
  simply stops holds only the **Identity Card** and whatever subject-wise digital Marks Statement they
  can download. Evidence A.
- **TOC is inbound-only.** §3.9.2 carries credits *into* NIOS. **There is no reverse TOC** — NIOS nowhere
  provides a mechanism to carry credits *out* to a conventional board mid-stream. **NOT FOUND rather
  than a prohibition**, but the asymmetry is real and decision-relevant.

## 3.6 Recognition an NIOS centre must hold — answered, and it is PRIOR

> **NIOS accreditation is an additional layer on top of an existing affiliation — never a substitute for
> it.** The state-recognition question for an AI is answered by the **host school's** own affiliation.

**Is an AI a "school" under RTE s.18? — INFERENCE, flagged, do not ship.** s.2(n) confines "school" to one
"imparting elementary education"; s.2(f) fixes that at classes I–VIII. An AI for academic courses
delivers **Secondary and Senior Secondary** — above the elementary stage — so on the text it falls
**outside s.2(n)**, and therefore outside s.18, **not by exemption but because the definition never

## 4.2 What each body actually issues

**IB — the award is the body's exclusive prerogative, stated as a rule the school must communicate:**

> "3.1 … Schools must inform the relevant authorities and legal guardians that: … c. **The award of the
> following are the sole prerogative of the IB and not of the school:** i. the International

**Cambridge draws a precise three-way distinction an ERP should mirror:**

| Document | Who issues | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **Statement of results** | Cambridge → centre | **Provisional** — results may still change |
| **Certificate** | Cambridge → centre → candidate | **Permanent** record of the award |
| **Certifying statement** | Cambridge → candidate/institution | Official **replacement-proof** of grades |

> "**A Cambridge certificate records and confirms a candidate's final results. A certificate is separate
> from a statement of results. Until certificates are issued, we reserve the right to change or amend a
> candidate's results.**"

> "**6.4.5 Lost certificates — We will not replace lost certificates.** … If a candidate loses their

> "**6.5** … Certifying statements are **fully endorsed by the Vice Chancellor of the University of
> Cambridge**. Educational institutions and employers accept them as a legitimate record of results."

**§6.4.3 Name changes — directly relevant to any ERP that lets a school edit a student's name:**

> "**A certificate is a permanent record of the candidate's achievement. It shows their name, as you
> entered it, at the time we make the award. We cannot provide replacement certificates to: show any
> changes that take place after the exam**, for example, a candidate changing their name because they get
> married or adopt a parent's name…"

Also: **§6.4.4** — "**We cannot combine grades achieved in different series or at different centres onto
one certificate**"; **§6.4.6** damaged certificates replaceable on return of the original plus fee;
replacement window roughly **two years** (June series: 30 April 2028; November: 30 September 2028), after
which only certifying statements; certifying statement turnaround "**within 28 days**".

## 4.3 ⭐ Branding — the one rule that directly constrains a certificate module

> **Cambridge EXPRESSLY FORBIDS its branding on certificates the school itself issues.** Evidence A.
>
> "Q5 You must: … **Q5.5 not use the Cambridge Identity or Cambridge Marks in certification or financial
> documentation that you issue, for example, attendance certificates, receipts or invoices**…"

A school-issued leaving certificate is "certification … that you issue". **Cambridge branding must not
appear on it.** Supporting rules: **Q3** — "**You must email logoapproval@cambridgeinternational.org with
proof copies of all your planned uses … and gain approval before use**"; **Q4.3** — place the identity
"**at the bottom of any document**"; **Q5.1** — no more than **75% of the size** of the centre's own logo
and **lower** than it; **Q11** — permission "**is dependent upon your continued status as an approved
centre**"; **Q1** — misuse means "**your centre status may be withdrawn and the Agreement terminated**".

**The IB has no equivalent express prohibition — but no licence either.** Its licence is a **closed list**:

> "10.4 … a non-exclusive licence to: a. **use the 'IB World School' trilingual logo on its stationery,
> publications, website and non-commercial promotional material** in connection with the IB programme it
> is authorized to offer…"
> "10.5 **Schools must not otherwise reproduce any materials from the IB or use its logos in any form
> without prior written consent from the IB.**"
> "10.2 … **a school is prohibited from using the above-mentioned trademarks to identify or reference its
> own non-IB courses.**"
> "3.2 … **schools are never permitted to use the IB corporate logo.**"

**Honest about the ambiguity:** a school-issued TC is not named in the 10.4 list. Whether it is
"stationery" or a "publication" is **genuinely ambiguous and I will not resolve it by preference** —
10.4(a) qualifies the licence with "in connection with the IB programme", and a leaving certificate
certifies *enrolment and conduct*, not the programme. **What IS unambiguous: using an IB mark on a
certificate for a NON-IB stream is prohibited outright by 10.2** — a live risk for the many Indian
schools running both an IB and an Indian-board stream.

**Both bodies also restrict the school's NAME**, which appears on certificate letterhead: **IB Art. 15.1**
— "**No authorized school may have a legal or preferred name which includes the words 'International
Baccalaureate', 'IB' or 'World School'**" (a school may *describe itself as* an IB World School under
3.2, but may not *be named* one); **Cambridge Q9/Q10** — no new registration from a school using
"Cambridge", "Cambridge International" or "IGCSE" in its name or brand, and an existing school "**may not
change its name to include the word 'Cambridge'**". ⚠️ **Many Indian schools market themselves exactly
this way** — the rule is Evidence A; the marketing practice is observational and not verified here.

## 4.6 ⭐ THE CRITICAL QUESTION — what Indian recognition an IB / Cambridge school must still hold

### IB — YES, expressly, as a CONTINUING obligation. Article 15. Evidence A.

> **"Article 15: Name and status of schools**
> 15.1 No authorized school may have a legal or preferred name which includes the words "International
> Baccalaureate", "IB" or "World School" …
> **15.2 A school must be and remain duly registered as a legal entity under local law** — either for
> profit or not-for-profit, privately or publicly funded.
> **15.3 A school must have and maintain the required licensure or accreditation by the local authorities**
> and/or, if applicable, independent recognized accreditation agencies, **indicating that it is
> licensed/accredited to provide educational services to students of the ages served by the programme(s)
> for which the school is authorized.**
> 15.4 A school must inform the IB in writing of any changes in its legal status … licensure, or
> structure."

**Read carefully:**

- "**must be and remain**" (15.2) and "**must have and maintain**" (15.3) are **continuing** obligations,
  not one-off entry checks.
- 15.3 is scoped to "**students of the ages served by the programme(s)**" — **a recognition covering only
  some grades does not cover the rest.** A school running the PYP must be licensed for primary-age
  children.
- Applied to India, "required licensure or accreditation by the local authorities" **is the state
  recognition certificate** — the RTE s.18 instrument and its state-law equivalents. **The IB does not
  define what Indian licensure means; it defers to Indian authorities.**

**Restated as a withdrawal ground, which gives it teeth:**

> "13.1 … the IB may withdraw a school's authorization include … b. **a school ceases to be registered as
> a legal entity under local law** c. **a school no longer holds the required licensure or accreditation
> by the local authorities** …"

Reinforced at **Art. 2.3** ("**Authorized schools must operate in accordance with all applicable laws,
regulations and policies**… must reconfirm the same to the IB at the time of evaluation"), **Art. 5.1**,
and **Art. 9.5** (online DP). **The requirement appears four times independently in the governing text —
well past the "one source is not verification" bar within the document.**

⚠️ **Remaining limit:** the *authorisation application form* and the "Guide to school authorization" are
**separate documents I could not retrieve** (ibo.org 403). So the **standing rule** (Art. 15) is verified;
the **application-form wording at initial application** is not. Given 15.2's "must **be and remain**" the
distinction is largely academic, but it is recorded rather than glossed.

### Cambridge — YES, as an ENTRY criterion. Evidence B (official portal; it is the stated approval criteria, but a webpage rather than the Handbook).

> Fire certificate.

### ⚠️ A notable NEGATIVE that matters commercially

Cambridge's own **"Become a Cambridge International school in India"** page is **pure marketing** —

**⚠️ And the conflation this file exists to prevent:** Cambridge's India page claims "**Recognised by

### What Indian law independently requires

unambiguous on this point — but whether and how states actually enforce s.18 against

# §5 · ⭐ THE COUNTERSIGNATURE QUESTION FOR KVS AND NVS — the premise was wrong

**The brief asked whether the KVS Asstt. Commissioner / NVS Deputy Director countersignature survived
CBSE's abolition. The question rests on a false premise, and correcting it answers it.**

That countersignature was **CBSE's rule about accepting an *inbound* TC** — never a KVS or NVS rule
about *issuing* one. CBSE's Annexure-I footnote named several officers whose countersignature **CBSE**
would accept on a TC presented to one of its schools. The Asstt. Commissioner (KVS) and Deputy
Director (NVS) appear there as officers CBSE would accept a signature **from**, not as officers KVS or
NVS required a signature **by**.

**Once separated, there are three different countersignatures, running in different directions, with
different fates:**

| # | Countersignature | Whose rule | Direction | Status as of 2026-09-12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Asstt. Commissioner, KVS / Deputy Director, NVS on a CBSE-to-CBSE TC | **CBSE** (Annexure-I footnote) | **Inbound** — so the receiving school may accept it | **ABOLISHED** by CBSE (26.11.2014, 2019, 04.02.2020, 31.10.2025) — already established in `central-and-boards.md` §C |
| **2** | **Chairman, VMC** on a **KV-issued** TC | **KVS** (Education Code, Appendix XXXIII) | **Outbound** — on the TC the KV itself issues | **STILL ON THE BOOKS**, but **conditional**: required **only** when the TC is signed by an **Officiating/Incharge** Principal. Not required for a regular Principal's TC. **Evidence A** |
| **3** | Countersignature on a TC **presented to a JNV** at admission | **NVS** (PAP 2026-27 §34) | **Inbound** | **WAIVED** — "No need to countersign the same if the school is registered in UDISE portal." **Evidence A** |

> **The verdict: CBSE did not "abolish KVS's or NVS's internal countersignature", because on the
> evidence found, no KVS or NVS rule ever required an Asstt. Commissioner or Deputy Director to
> countersign a KV-issued or JNV-issued TC in the first place.** CBSE's abolition and KVS's
> Appendix-XXXIII rule are different requirements, on different documents, borne by different
> officers, running in opposite directions. **CBSE's abolition has no effect on the Chairman-VMC rule.**

**How the negative was established (Evidence A).** All 285 pages of the KVS Education Code were
extracted and grepped for `countersign|counter sign|assistant commissioner|asstt`. "Countersign"
occurs on pages **246, 257, 258, 262** only. Page 246 is **Appendix XXXIII — the TC form — and its
countersignature is Chairman, VMC**. Pages 257/258/262 are **Appendices XXXVIII/XXXIX, the National
Award for Teachers proformas** — verbatim: *"Counter signature of Asstt. Commissioner of the Region
(with seal)"*, under "Recommendations of the Chairman, VMC", on a **teacher's award nomination**.

> **In the KVS Education Code, the Asstt. Commissioner countersigns teacher-award nominations, not
> student Transfer Certificates.**

For NVS: the PAP 2026-27 (380 pages) contains **exactly one** occurrence of "countersign" — para
34(i) — and it is about **inbound** TCs.

**⚠️ Residual uncertainty, stated honestly — CONFLICT K-1.** The KVS Education Code is a ~1980s second
edition, and **no KVS circular post-dating CBSE's 2014/2019/2020/2025 abolitions addressing TC
countersignature was found, in either direction.** So the correct confidence statement is: *the only
KVS countersignature rule locatable is Chairman-VMC-if-officiating; an unpublished or intranet KVS
instruction could not be positively ruled out.* Treat "Asstt. Commissioner countersignature required"
as **UNSUPPORTED, not DISPROVED.**

**⚠️ An independent second reason CBSE's footnote is dead letter for NVS — CONFLICT N-1.** Across PAP
2026-27: **"Deputy Commissioner" ×21**, "Assistant Commissioner" ×16, **"Deputy Director" ×1** — and

Deputy Commissioner.** CBSE's footnote naming *"the Deputy Director, Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti"* refers

## 6.4 Who signs — Principal, plus a conditional Chairman VMC

**Evidence A**, Appendix XXXIII signature block, verbatim:

> `तैयारकर्त्ता / Prepared by` · `जांचकर्ता / Checked by` · `* प्रतिहस्ताक्षरित / Countersigned — अध्यक्ष वि.प्र.स. /
> Chairman, VMC` · `प्राचार्य / Principal` — each with Signature, Name, Designation; **Seal** under both
> the Chairman-VMC and Principal columns.

**The footnote, verbatim:**

> "\* If this T.C. is issued by the **Officiating/Incharge Principal**, it should be **invariably counter
> signed by the Chairman, VMC**, otherwise **it will not be valid**."

> **ERP rule:** signatory = **Principal**. Two clerical roles are named on the form (*Prepared by*,
> *Checked by*) and should be modelled — they are part of the prescribed form, not optional. The
> Chairman-VMC countersignature is a **conditional validation rule keyed on signer type**, not a display
> field: absent it, an Officiating/Incharge Principal's TC is **invalid on the face of the Code**. **No
> Vice-Principal and no Sangathan-officer signature appears anywhere on Appendix XXXIII.**

## 6.5 Priority admission on transfer — and a rule that TIGHTENED

**Two editions read in full (Evidence A).** ⚠️ **The KVS HQ page still links only the 2023-24 edition;
the current one is on a Regional Office site** — RO sites are fresher than HQ.

- **2023-24 §7(i):** "Admission of children with KV TC will be **automatic (over and above Class
  Strength)** if the parent has been transferred from one station to another."
- **2026-27 §7(A)(i)** — *"automatic" is gone*: KV-TC admissions "from class I to XII, would be
  **beyond 50, only with the approval of the Deputy Commissioner**, concerned RO KVS, provided the
  option of redistribution/re-allotment to nearby KVs is exhausted." Where no parental transfer is
  involved: **class strength of 45** (not applicable to sibling cases). Re-allotted KVs should be
  **within 8 KM** of the child's residence; **"Sibling cases of KV TC should be allotted in the same
  KV."**

**CONFLICT K-4: this is a substantive tightening, not a restatement. 2026-27 governs.**

**An inbound endorsement that is ALIVE, and is not a CBSE countersignature (Evidence A).** 2026-27
Part-B §2(iii): "**TCs issued by the schools of Defence Ministry/ Departments/ Authority will be
endorsed by the concerned Deputy Commissioner of the Region where admission is sought.**"

> This is a **KVS-internal admission control on an inbound TC**, wholly outside CBSE's countersignature
> regime and unaffected by its abolition. **CONFLICT K-2:** the 2023-24 text read "*TCs issued by the
> **CBSE affiliated** schools of Defence Ministry…*"; **2026-27 dropped "CBSE affiliated"**, widening
> the requirement to Defence-run schools generally. Widening, or drafting slip? **Unresolved.**

## 6.6 Dues, duplicates, timeline — three NOT FOUNDs

- **Withholding for dues — NOT FOUND as a power.** The full Education Code and both Admission
  Guidelines were grepped for `dues|withhold|arrear`. The Code's only "withhold" hit is about a Service
  Association's foreign correspondence. **The Admission Guidelines contain no occurrence of "dues" or
  "withhold" at all.** What exists is Appendix XXXIII **field 13 — "Whether the pupil has paid all dues
  to the Vidyalaya?"**, which is a **declaration printed on the TC, not a rule permitting the TC to be
  withheld.** Related but distinct: **Art. 117(b)** contemplates a name being *struck off the rolls* for
  "non-payment of fee", restored on a Rs.50 re-admission fee. **Do not enforce a dues-block for KVs on
  KVS authority.**
- **Duplicate TCs — NOT FOUND.** "Duplicate" appears in the Code only in the Service Associations
  regulations. Note Art. 102's "third copy" implies a **multi-copy original issue**, which is a
  different thing from a duplicate.
- **Timeline for issuing a TC — NOT FOUND.** The only deadlines in either Admission Guidelines edition
  are admission-process deadlines. **KVS prescribes no TC turnaround.** *(Contrast NVS, which does —
  §7.4. The asymmetry is real and worth surfacing in the product.)*

## 6.7 Post-issuance duty — a PUBLIC "TC issued" register

**Evidence A, corroborated across schools.** Every KV school site on the s3waas platform carries a
standard **`/en/tc-issued/`** page — a **publicly searchable register of every TC issued**, with an

- <https://no1bhopal.kvs.ac.in/en/tc-issued/> — HTTP 200, content read (**A**)
- <https://no1kanchrapara.kvs.ac.in/en/tc-issued/> — HTTP 200 (**B**, corroborating that it is a

**Columns published, verbatim:** `Name | T.C. No. | Admission No. | Class | Father Name | Mother Name |
Date of Birth`.

**Observations that matter for the module:**

- **TC No. format is `Book/Serial`** (`6479/253699`), matching Appendix XXXIII's `Book No. / No.`
  header. The book number is stable across a batch; the serial runs continuously.
- The **Class** column carries **free text including detention status** ("ONCE DETAINED CLASS IN IX") —
  Appendix XXXIII field 11 leaking into a public register.
- Admission-No. formats are **inconsistent within a single school** (`302057` vs `30/1249`).
- **KVS publishes a data table, not scanned TC images.** CBSE requires the school to upload the *scanned
  TC*; what KVS operationalises school-side is this **structured register**. **The two duties are
  different and a KV owes both.**

## 7.2 No NVS TC proforma — NOT FOUND

## 7.3 Who signs, and the approval that matters more than any signature

**Signature: the JNV Principal** (Evidence A) — "TC may be issued to the concerned student **by the JNV
Principal**". No co-signature block is prescribed, because no NVS proforma exists.

**Authorisation ≠ signature — and this is the important distinction.** For a **pre-mature** (mid-course)
TC the Principal cannot act alone. Two routes appear, both Evidence A:

- **Disciplinary route** — "In all cases of gross indiscipline TC may be issued … by the JNV Principal
  **on recommendations of the Chairman VMC and approval from Deputy Commissioner of the concerned NVS
  Regional Office**" *(F.No. 7-10/2017-NVS (SA)/5803 dated 22.04.2022)*.
- **Long-absence route** — permission of the **Regional Office**, with a **10-day RO decision SLA**.

**Outbound countersignature: NOT FOUND at all.** Instead NVS imposes a **prior-approval** requirement —
a stronger control applied *earlier* in the workflow than a countersignature applied after.

**"Pre-Mature Transfer Certificate" is an AUDITED category.** The PAP's Panel Inspection proforma
(p.326) requires each JNV to report: "**3. Pre-Mature Transfer Certificate issued since last Panel
Inspection:** `S.No. | Session | Name of the student | Class | Date of TC | Reason of issue of TC |
Official granting permission for issue of TC (with letter no. & Date)`".

> **This is the most product-relevant NVS finding after the countersignature one: a JNV TC record is
> incomplete without `{reason, approving official, permission letter no., permission date}`.**

## 7.4 The inbound waiver — and how it differs from CBSE's

**PAP 2026-27 §34, verbatim (Evidence A):**

> "**34) ADMISSION IN JNVs: (Session 2026-27)** … following guidelines regarding submission of documents
> (at the time of admission) are made:-
> **i) Transfer Certificate - No need to countersign the same if the school is registered in UDISE
> portal.**
> ii) Rural/Urban certificate … iii) **Income Certificate - Not mandatory.**
> iv) Birth Certificate - If birth certificate is not submitted by the candidate, **verify the same with

**⚠️ CONFLICT N-2, and it is practically important.** NVS's waiver is **conditional on UDISE

## 7.5 The long-absence timeline — NVS's only TC clock

**Evidence A**, *(ref. F.No.12-8/2023-24/NVS(Acad)/382 dated 09.10.2025)*:

| Total days absent | Action (verbatim, abridged) |
|---|---|
| **10 days** | JNV issues 1st written communication by registered post to parents, to report within a week or submit documents |
| **20 days** | 2nd written communication, report within 10 days, "**with warning that name of his ward may be strike off**" |
| **30 days** | "**Principal JNV shall seek permission from Regional Office to issue TC** … and **within 10 days RO should communicate decision**" |
| **40 days** | "After getting approval from Regional Office, **name of student must be removed from rolls**" |

## 7.6 ⚠️ MIGRATION — three different things that must not be conflated

**The brief asked about "the JNV Class-IX migration scheme" as one thing. NVS runs two distinct
schemes, and neither is a board Migration Certificate.**

| | **Migration (§40)** | **Mini-Migration (§39)** |
|---|---|---|
| Class | **IX only** | **XI only** |
| Trigger | National integration, **30% quota** | Chosen **stream unavailable** at parent JNV |
| Duration | **One academic year, then returns** | **Permanent** (rest of XI–XII) |
| Rolls | Stays on **parent** JNV rolls | **Admission confirmed at host** JNV |
| Records | — | "All documents including personal file" forwarded parent → host |
| Document produced | **Participation certificate + group photo**, from host Principal | Admission at host; **TC deliberately avoided** |
| Who decides | Parent JNV per NVS norms, April | **Regional Office** decides movements |

**Migration (§40)** — corroborated from two independent official sources (an archived NVS page and PAP
§40), Evidence A: "**30% of children from one JNV are migrated to another JNV at Class-IX level for one
academic year**", between Hindi and non-Hindi regions, implementing the Three Language Formula. From
"**1988-89 with only 2 JNVs and 31 migrated students**" to **650 JNVs / 39,530 Class-IX students /
11,638 migrated** in 2023-24. PAP §40: "**After completion of migration period successfully, a
certificate to be issued to each migrated student by Principal, JNV where the migrated student studied
for one year with Group Photograph.**"

> **No TC is issued and the student is not struck off the parent JNV's rolls.** The scheme is **Class IX
> ONLY** — neither official source mentions Class XI under it.

**Mini-Migration (§39)** is a real transfer, and the PAP ties it explicitly to TC-avoidance: the aim is
to accommodate students at their own JNV "…**minimizing the mini migration as well as reducing the
number of TC issued due to the non-availability of the choice stream at the parent JNV**" *(ref. F.No.
1-2/2021-NVS (Acad)/ dated 13.05.2022)*.

> **Product naming rule: do NOT let "Migration" in a JNV context resolve to a board Migration
> Certificate.** Three distinct concepts share the word. **Nothing in any NVS source touches a board
> Migration Certificate at all.**
