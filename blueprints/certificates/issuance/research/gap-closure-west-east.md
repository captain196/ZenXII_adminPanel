**Method limits, stated up front because they shape every negative below.**

1. **The `WebSearch` budget for this session was exhausted (200/200) before this leg began.** Every
   source here was reached by *direct fetch of a known or guessed URL*, by enumerating a page's own
   links, or through the Wayback CDX index. No keyword discovery was possible. A negative in this
   file therefore means "not found by URL enumeration from the authority's own site", which is
   weaker than "not found by search" for anything the authority does not link to itself.
2. **`indiankanoon.org` worked for four queries and then became blocked** ("unable to verify if
   domain is safe to fetch"). Judgment-based leads are consequently partial.
3. **No CAPTCHA was encountered in this leg.** The Maharashtra-style `HUMAN-ONLY` category is used
   below only for host failures and unpublished text, not for a gate anyone was asked not to solve.

### The direction question, answered precisely

The four authorities in this file produce **four different shapes**, and three of them are new to the
corpus:

| shape | direction | instance |

| Countersignature by the **managing organisation** | **INBOUND**, triggered by institution type | **BSE Odisha admission form — new** |
| Counter-**authentication** by the affiliating Board's Secretary | **OUTBOUND** (on a certificate the school issues, before it is valid) | **WB RTE r.18 + Schedule II — second instance in the corpus after TSBIE (C-15b), and the first that is mandatory on the face of a prescribed form** |

#### 2.1.2 The 1986 Rules — Chapter XII read verbatim at Level A

Retrieved from the Directorate's own domain (a **different, cleaner copy** than the goa.gov.in one
the corpus used) and fully text-extractable: **[A]**
https://education.goa.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Goa-Education-Rules-1986.pdf

- **r.127, verbatim — the signer:** *"No leaving certificate is valid unless it is in the form
  prescribed by the Director of Education and is signed by the Head of the school himself."*
- **r.125, verbatim — the dues rule:** *"Either refusal to issue a Leaving Certificate without
  adequate justification or delay of over one week in issuance of the certificate or in giving a
  reply explaining why it cannot be issued, may entail penal action against the school, considering
  of cut in grant or withdrawal of recognition. The only ground on which a leaving certificate may be
  refused is (i) non-payment of fees due, if any or (ii) rustication by the Director. In the case of
  rustication, no leaving certificate shall be issued until the expiry of the period for which the
  Director has debarred the pupil from admission to a recognised school."*
- **r.116(2), verbatim — the countersignature, and its direction:** *"Where a student seeks admission
  to an aided school on the basis of a School leaving certificate granted by a school in any State or
  Union territory, other than Goa, such certificate (except where such certificate has already been
  verified) be sent for verification and countersignature, by the head of the school in which
  admission is sought, to the education authority of the district in which the school from which the
  transfer certificate was obtained is situated, in case the certificate is not already got
  countersigned by the guardian."*
  ⚠️ **The trailing clause is corrupt.** *"…got countersigned by the guardian"* is incoherent — a
  guardian cannot countersign for a district education authority. It is probably a printing or
  transcription error for *"by the said authority"*. **Recorded as a textual defect; deliberately not
  repaired.** The operative direction (receiving head sends it to the originating district's
  authority) is unambiguous regardless.
- **r.124:** application in person or in writing by parent/guardian/candidate-if-major; *"Leaving
  certificate shall be issued without unnecessary delay, within a week at the latest."*
- **r.126:** free within two years of leaving (or of the SSC/Technical Education result); ₹1
  thereafter, and ₹1 for a duplicate, a birth-date certificate or a register extract.
- **r.128:** date of birth (Christian Era) and standard entered in **figures and words**, *"in
  manuscript and not typewritten."* **r.129:** promoted/detained noted in the **Remarks** column.
- **r.27(1)(A):** the pupil records every school must produce at inspection include the **General
  Register "in the form prescribed"**, **Leaving Certificates received from other schools**, and
  **Counterfoils of Leaving Certificates issued to Pupils**. **r.27(3):** *"Standard forms and
  registers as applicable to Government Schools shall be used for the recognised schools as well."*
  → **this is the hook by which the Director's unpublished LC form binds private recognised schools.**

**Prescribed format — the honest statement.** Goa is **not** a "no prescribed format" state. r.127
makes the form **legally mandatory and constitutive of validity**, and delegates its content to the
Director of Education; r.27(3) extends the Government-school standard forms to recognised schools.
The form itself is simply **not published in the Rules**. That is a *publication* gap, not a *legal*
absence — a distinction that matters for C-16/C-16b (see §3.6).

**Two corrections to the corpus's §5.5 table.**

1. **The annexure list is incomplete.** The corpus records "Form I, a Proforma, Form III and Form IV
   (School Inspection Proforma)". Read directly, the 1986 Rules annex **six** items: **Form I**
   (r.36, application for grant of recognition) · **Proforma** (school particulars) · **Form II**
   (r.49, application for registration for grant) · **Appendix A** (r.69, building grant/loan) ·
   **Form III** (r.71, statement of movable and immovable properties) · **Form IV** (**r.161**,
   *"Data for Inspection of Secondary Schools"*). Form IV sits under r.161, not as a generic
   inspection proforma. **The conclusion is unchanged and now firmer: no leaving-certificate form is
   annexed.** **[A]**
2. **r.131 is narrower than the corpus renders it.** The corpus row reads "The whole chapter applies
   to recognised unaided schools 'so far as may be'". The text is two-part: **r.131(1)** first grants
   unaided schools *autonomy over admission method* (*"either on the basis of admission test or on
   the basis of result in a particular class or school"*), and **r.131(2)** then applies the chapter
   *"**Subject to the provisions of sub-rule (1)** … so far as may be."* So the chapter reaches
   unaided schools **subordinated to their admission autonomy**. The substance of the corpus claim
   survives; the subordination is load-bearing for anything that gates admission. **[A]**

⚠️ **Chapter XII is drafted for AIDED schools.** rr.112 and 116(1) both say *"an aided school"* /
*"any aided school"*. Unaided recognised schools are reached only through r.131(2). Note also that
r.125 and r.127 sit in the same chapter — so **the Goa dues rule and the Goa signer rule reach an
unaided school by extension, not directly.**

#### 2.1.3 GBSHSE — the gap the corpus recorded as unreachable is now closed at Level A

`west-central-india.md` §5.9 records both `gbshse.in` (DNS failure) and `gbshse.gov.in` (connection

**The Board's constituting Act and Rules are published on the Directorate of Education's domain,
not the Board's** — which is why site-based searching missed them: **[A]**
https://education.goa.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/EducationBoardActandRules1975.pdf

It is a 344-page official compilation of **The Goa, Daman and Diu Secondary and Higher Secondary
Education Board Act, 1975 (Act No. 13 of 1975)**, assented 27 May 1975, published by Law and
Judiciary Department Notification **LD/Bill/2/75**, Panaji, 29 May 1975 — plus the Rules made under
it (amendments traced in footnotes to O.G. Series I No. 26 of 25-9-1980 and No. 33 of 25-11-1990),

- **s.35(1) — recognition is per-examination and time-boxed to the exam cycle:** a school *"desiring
  to be recognised for the purposes of admission to the privileges of the Board"* shall apply *"not
  later then fifteenth day of the month of July of the year which precedes by two years, the year in
  which it proposes to present candidates for the final examination"*; the Chairman may condone delay
  up to six months. **s.36:** a recognised school may send up candidates *"for final examination for
  which such recognition has been granted to it."* **[A]**
- **Rules r.5(1) — Form I for recognition, Form II for an additional subject or medium**, submitted
  to the Secretary **in triplicate**, setting out 13 particulars including *"whether the School has
  been administratively recognized by the Education Department"* → **the State grant is a declared
  input to the Board grant. C-18, from a fourth board.** **[A]**
- **r.5(1) Note, verbatim — the per-section answer:** *"A separate application be made by the
  institution in respect of a Higher Secondary Section."* **[A]**
- **r.5(2) — different inspectors per stage:** a Secondary application goes to the **Director of
  Education**; a Higher Secondary application to *"a Committee of two members of the Academic
  Council appointed by the Recognition Committee."* **[A]**
- **r.5(3):** the recommendation states *"whether and in what subjects or streams, on what condition
  and for what period the recognition should be granted"* → **no fixed term; the term is an output of
  each grant.** **[A]**
- **r.5(5):** an unrecognised school *"shall not be permitted to present candidates."* **[A]**
- **r.5(6)(x) — a recognition condition about certificates themselves:** a school may not be
  recognised or continue to be recognised unless *"the records, statistical, returns and certificates
  given by the school or the management are trustworthy."* **[A]**
  **This is the cleanest statutory link in the whole corpus between certificate integrity and the
  licence to issue.** It makes certificate reliability a standing condition of recognition, not
  merely a consequence of misconduct.
- **Definitions, Rules Ch. II vi–vii:** *"A Secondary School" means a school recognized as such by
  the board…*; *"Higher Secondary school" means a Higher Secondary School recognized as such by the

- **r.42 — Migration Certificate:** *"A Migration Certificate may, on application and payment of fee
  of Rs. 10/- be granted to a candidate who has passed the examination conducted by the Board."*
  Application to the **Secretary of the Board**. **[A]**
- **Later regulation set — the Eligibility Certificate, verbatim:** *"(1) The Board shall issue
  Eligibility to a student seeking admission in any recognized higher secondary school of this Board

### 2.2 · RBSE — the TC layer, and the font diagnosis corrected

#### 2.2.1 The extraction problem is not what the corpus recorded

`boards-north-central.md` §7.2 states that `hand_book16.pdf` *"embeds a legacy Devanagari font while
declaring itself Helvetica, so both pdftotext and PyMuPDF return Latin garbage … It is a substitution
cipher that could be guessed at."*

**That diagnosis is wrong, and the remedy differs.** Inspected directly:

- The file is **PDF 1.3, creator `Canon`**, created 2017-02-13.
- **Every one of its 38 pages contains exactly one image**, and the only font in the entire document
  is `('Helvetica', 'F3', 'WinAnsiEncoding')`.

So it is a **scanned paper document with a bad Latin OCR layer laid over Devanagari page images** —
not a text document in a mis-declared Indic font. **There is no substitution cipher and no font
mapping that could recover it**; the garbage (*"RTeTr qid, {\'Nen=T / ftftFSkdT / Hand-Book /
alwfl{--13,13-A,13-B"*) is an OCR engine's Latin guess at Devanagari glyph shapes, which is
information-destroying rather than reversible.

**What actually worked:** render each page with PyMuPDF at 140 dpi and read the page images. The
document is clean, high-contrast and completely legible that way. **All RBSE findings below were read
from rendered page images**, not from any text layer.

⚠️ **A related but distinct lead the corpus should keep separate.** RBSE **publishes the legacy font
itself** — `MFDEV010.TTF` on its Downloads page
(https://rajeduboard.rajasthan.gov.in/downloads/MFDEV010.TTF). That is the correct remedy for the
*other* class of RBSE/UBSE documents — genuine text PDFs in a mis-declared legacy Indic font. It is
**not** the remedy for `hand_book16.pdf`, which has no such font. **Two different defects were
conflated under one label; they need different tools.**

- **Intra-Board transfer → return-to-issuer verification.** The receiving school must send the TC and

  से प्रवर्जित छात्रों को राजस्थान बोर्ड के मान्यता प्राप्त विद्यालयों की कक्षा 11 व 12 में प्रवेश लेने

  बोर्ड/विश्वविद्यालय की कक्षा-10 माध्यमिक स्तर की उत्तीर्ण मूल अंकतालिका, **प्रव्रजन प्रमाण पत्र अथवा

#### 2.3.2 Chapter X, regs 9 and 39 — via a judgment **[C]**

*Procedure vs Board Of Secondary Education, Orissa*, Orissa High Court, Cuttack, **10 November 2023**,
**Justice A.C. Behera** — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/174755571/

- **Reg. 9, Ch. X** is cited for the proposition that *"every candidate shall fill up his form for
  appearing the High School Certificate examination before the Board by his own best handwriting"*
  and that **the form must be verified against the school admission register before submission.**
- **Reg. 39, Section-VI, Ch. X:** *"the date of birth entered in the Board's record cannot be changed,
  unless it is in the nature of clerical error or printing mistake"*, and a correction application is
  *"entertainable only within three years after passing of High School Certificate examination."*
- **On the TC's evidentiary weight — relevant to how much a product should trust one:** the Court held
  that transfer certificates recording a date of birth as a pupil moved between schools had **limited
  evidentiary value** absent proof of the original entry in the *first* school's admission register
  and examination of the teacher who made it. **The admission register, not the TC, is the source of
  truth.**

⇒ This aligns Odisha with West Bengal's Schedule II (*"As per Admission register of the school"*) and
with RBSE §1.8's return-to-issuer verification: **three of the four authorities in this file treat the
issuing school's admission register as the authoritative record and the TC as a derived document.**

### 3.5 · The countersignature trigger is NOT always a State line — a refinement to C-8/C-12/C-15

C-12 and `boards-east-northeast.md` §2.2 state the pattern as: *"Countersignature in India is
condition-triggered by **the pupil crossing a State line**, never a standing requirement of the
document."* Three of the four authorities here show the trigger is more varied:

| trigger | instance | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **State line** | Goa r.116(2); Kerala, Puducherry, MH, GJ, DNHDD, Bihar s.272, JAC, NBSE | A |
| **BOARD line** — *"बोर्ड के कार्य क्षेत्र से बाहर अर्थात अन्य बोर्ड/विश्वविद्यालयों से"* | **RBSE Anudeshika §1.7** | **A** |
| **Document language** — vernacular certificates need a DEO-countersigned English attested copy | **RBSE Anudeshika §1.8(2)** | **A** |
| **Institution type** — school managed by a Social Welfare Organisation | **BSE Odisha admission form field 07** | **A** |
| **Unrecognised sending school** | Bihar Education Code s.280 | A |

**Consequence, and it is a product consequence.** A rule engine keyed on `sendingState != receivingState`
gets Rajasthan wrong in a common case: **a CBSE school in Jaipur to an RBSE school in Jaipur is an
intra-State but inter-Board move, and it triggers the full inbound gate** (eligibility certificate +
DEO-countersigned TC). **The correct key is the sending *board/jurisdiction*, with language and
institution type as additional triggers — not the sending state.**

### 3.6 · C-16b needs a third correction — "no prescribed format" conflates three different things

C-16b already corrected C-16 from *"CBSE's Annexure-I is the only prescribed TC format"* to
*"look to the state, not the board"*. This file shows the negative is still too coarse, because
**"no prescribed format" covers three legally distinct situations**:

| situation | meaning | instance |
|---|---|---|
| **No format exists** | nothing prescribes the document's fields | MP, Chhattisgarh (per corpus) |
| **A format exists and is published** | a citable form with mandatory fields | CBSE Annexure-I · Gujarat નમૂનો-૯ · MH Appendix Four · Kerala Form 5 · TN Appendix-5/5-A · **WB Schedule II** *(completion certificate)* |
| **A format is legally mandatory but NOT published** | validity turns on a form whose text the researcher cannot obtain | **Goa r.127** — *"No leaving certificate is valid unless it is in the form prescribed by the Director of Education"*, with no form annexed |

**Goa is the clean example of the third category and the corpus currently files it as a plain
NOT FOUND.** That understates it: a Goa LC issued in a free-text layout is, on the face of r.127,
**invalid**. Any generic template offered to a Goa school is therefore a compliance risk, whereas the
same template in MP is merely unregulated. **Action: add a `formatPrescribedButUnpublished` state to
the format model, and add WB Schedule II to the prescribed-format table with its instrument scoped to
the completion certificate, not the TC.**

### 3.7 · The identifier-substitution pattern — two additions and one exclusion

C-12/C-17 track "printed verifiable identifiers replacing a human countersignature" across six
jurisdictions. From this file:

- **ADD, RBSE [A]:** the Board issues an **अस्थायी/स्थायी सम्बद्धता क्रमांक + विद्यालय कोड** and
  **publishes both on its website** (cl.4(i), cl.8(iv), cl.9(iii) — *"बोर्ड की वेब साईट पर भी
  प्रदर्शित किया जाएगा"*). Board-published and therefore **externally verifiable** — the closest
  structural match in the corpus to Karnataka's DISE-code substitution.
- **ADD, West Bengal [A]:** **Schedule II cannot be completed without the school's Index number** — a
  prescribed certificate with a mandatory identifier field. Note it **coexists with** an outbound
  counter-authentication rather than replacing it, which is evidence **against** reading the pattern
  as a clean substitution everywhere.
- **EXCLUDE, BSE Odisha:** the WhatsApp/portal digitised-certificate service is **delivery of the
  Board's own certificates**, gated by roll number + DOB + OTP. It verifies **a candidate's identity to
  obtain their own certificate**; it does **not** let a receiving school verify a TC. **Counting it as
  an identifier-substitution instance would overstate the pattern.**

### 3.8 · Two mechanisms the corpus's taxonomy has no slot for

C-12's taxonomy is *human countersignature* → *printed verifiable identifier*. Two further mechanisms
are established here at Level A, and a model with only those two categories has nowhere to put them:

1. **The board-issued eligibility certificate, as a hard inbound gate.** **GBSHSE** — *"shall be
   admitted to the institutions recognized **only on production of eligibility certificate issued only
   by this Board**"*; **RBSE** — *"पात्रता प्रमाण पत्र के अभाव में प्रवेश नहीं दिया जायेगा"*, with a
   ₹1,000 per-pupil penalty on the school and personal liability on the head who admits without one.
   **Two independent jurisdictions, same instrument, same stage (+2 entry).** This is not a
   countersignature (nothing is endorsed on the pupil's TC) and not an identifier (nothing is printed
   on it) — it is **a separate document the receiving board issues before admission may occur.**
2. **Return-to-issuer verification.** **RBSE §1.8(1)** — for an **intra-Board** transfer the receiving
   school must send the TC and marksheet **back to the issuing head**, obtain verification, and only
   then grant permanent admission. **No prior instance in the corpus**, and it is the only mechanism
   found anywhere that polices **intra-jurisdiction** transfers, where every other authority requires
   nothing at all.

**Both are receiving-side**, so neither disturbs C-15. But both mean a school's TC can be functionally
unusable at the destination without anything being wrong with the TC — a materially different failure
mode from a missing countersignature.

### 3.9 · WB r.18 vs Schedule II — an internal mismatch, flagged not resolved

r.18(2) requires the completion certificate to be *"signed by the Head Master/Head of the Institution
… **and one assistant teacher**"*. **Schedule II provides only two signature blocks: the Headmaster,
and the Secretary of the affiliating Board — there is no assistant-teacher block.** Separately,
**Schedule II's own cross-reference reads "(See rule 19)"**, but r.19 is *Allowances … of members of the
State Advisory Council*; the certificate rule is **r.18**.

**Two candidate explanations, and I cannot choose between them on this copy:** genuine drafting
defects in the notification, or artifacts of the Centre for Civil Society transcription (which
demonstrably contains OCR errors — `Definiti0ns`, `class VH1`, `sea]`). **Do not implement a
three-signature WB completion certificate, and do not cite Schedule II as "rule 19", until the Kolkata
Gazette copy is read.** **[A with transcription caveat]**

### 3.10 · Odisha — the corpus's caution can be narrowed but not lifted

`boards-east-northeast.md` §3.5: *"This is an access failure. **Do not record BSE Odisha as 'has no TC
rule.'**"* — correct when written. The chapter index (§2.3.1) now supports a narrower, stronger

## 5 · What this file should and should not change in the product

**Should change:**

1. **Goa gets a real authority profile at Level A** — signer = *head of the school himself* (r.127);
   dues refusal permitted on exactly two grounds (r.125); one-week issuance deadline (r.124);
   DOB in figures **and** words, **in manuscript** (r.128). And a **`formatPrescribedButUnpublished`**
   flag, because a free-text Goa LC is invalid on the face of r.127.
2. **Per-section recognition is now confirmed at Level A for a third jurisdiction** — GBSHSE
   r.5(1) Note. `Issuer_identity` should not carry a single school-wide recognition record for Goa,
   Rajasthan or Odisha.
3. **The inbound-gate model needs two new mechanism types** — board-issued eligibility certificate,
   and return-to-issuer verification (§3.8).
4. **Any cross-jurisdiction rule must key on the sending BOARD, not the sending STATE** (§3.5).
   Keying on state gets the common CBSE-to-RBSE-within-Rajasthan case wrong.
5. **`boards-north-central.md` §7 needs rewriting** — §7.1's blanket negative is overturned, §7.2's
   font diagnosis is wrong, and §7.5's Q3/Q4/Q5 are answered (§3.1–3.3).

**Should NOT change:**

1. **C-15 stands.** No authority here requires an issuing school to obtain a countersignature before
   handing over a local TC.
2. **Do not give West Bengal a TC countersignature rule.** r.18/Schedule II is an **outbound
   authentication on the s.30 completion certificate**, and Schedule 1's "Countersigned" block is a
   **school-governance** signature on a recognition application. Both are one inference away from a
   rule that does not exist.
3. **Do not assert that Odisha has no TC rule** (§3.10), and do not count Odisha's digitised-certificate
   service as identifier substitution (§3.7).
4. **Do not implement a dues-based TC block anywhere on the strength of Goa r.125** without legal
   sign-off. Corpus C-2 and C-11 both apply: for classes I–VIII the RTE Act prevails, and the honest
   framing remains *"the rule stands and is unenforceable"* — which, for Goa specifically, is
   **still untested** (G6).
