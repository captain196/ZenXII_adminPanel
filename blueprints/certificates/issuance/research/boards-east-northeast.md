# East & North-East India — THE BOARD LAYER

**Scope** The state examination boards of the twelve eastern and north-eastern jurisdictions:
WBBSE · WBCHSE · BSEB · JAC · BSE Odisha · CHSE Odisha · SEBA/AHSEC (now ASSEB) · **MBOSE** ·
TBSE · NBSE · MBSE · BSEM · COHSEM · Arunachal Pradesh · Sikkim.

**Verification date** 2026-09-12. Every claim carries a URL and an evidence level.

**This file answers eight questions and only those eight**, per board: (1) prescribed TC format or
mandated TC fields; (2) anything required *printed* on the certificate; (3) countersignature;
(4) post-issuance duty; (5) admission of a student arriving from another board or state;
(6) migration certificates; (7) duplicates; and, for Meghalaya and Assam, (8) whether an
**Autonomous District Council** has any role in school certificates.

**Companion file** `east-northeast-india.md` covers the *recognition* layer for the same region and
established the governing distinction this file assumes: **eligibility to ISSUE flows from state
RECOGNITION; boards govern EXAMINATIONS.** Nothing below disturbs that.

## Evidence levels

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | The primary text was read — the regulation, Act, bye-law or form itself |
| **B** | An official portal, circular or notification (not the underlying legal text) |
| **C** | Credible secondary — court reporting, a legal database, reputable news |
| **D** | Unverified or inferred. **Never enforce a level-D claim.** |

## 2 · The five findings that matter

### 2.1 · Not one board in this region prescribes a TC format. Fifteen searched.

This is the strongest negative in the file and it is now **corroborated at the board layer as well
as the state layer**. The companion recognition file found that no state in the region prescribes a
TC format; this file finds that **no board does either.**

Where a board touches the TC at all, it does so in one of three narrow ways — none of them a layout
or a field list:

| Board | What it actually requires |
|---|---|
| **JAC** | The TC must be *signed by the Head of the Institution last attended* and, for out-of-State candidates, **countersigned by the DEO** |
| **NBSE** | For inter-State migrants, the TC must be **countersigned by the Inspector of Schools / DEO of the State last studied** |
| **TBSE** | The TC must **state the class the pupil is eligible to enter** *(established in the prior wave)* |
| **ASSEB, MBSE, COHSEM** | A TC is a **document the board consumes** at transfer/registration — its existence is mandatory, its content is not specified |

**Product consequence, and it is unchanged from the prior wave: the software must not assert a
prescribed TC format for any board in this region.** It may assert that a TC must exist, must be
signed by the head of institution, and — in Jharkhand and Nagaland — must be countersigned when the
pupil comes from another State.

### 2.2 · The board layer is where countersignature actually lives — and it is inter-State only

This **materially revises `CONFLICTS.md` C-8 and C-12.** C-8 recorded that state-level
countersignature existed in *exactly one of twelve states* (Bihar), and concluded that "the board
layer is separate and its current status is still unread." It is now read.

Two more boards impose countersignature, and **both attach it to the same trigger Bihar used**:

> **JAC, Regulations for Examinations, Reg. 5(iv)(a)** — a student must produce *"the school leaving
> certificate/transfer certificate signed by the Head of the Institution last attended **and
> countersigned by the District Education Officer (in the case of candidates outside the State)**."*
> [A — https://jac.jharkhand.gov.in/assets/documents/ExamRegulations.pdf]

> **NBSE, Management of Examinations 2023, Ch.8 "Regular Candidate"** — an inter-State entrant to
> Classes VIII–X must produce *"Marksheet and transfer certificate in original issued by the school
> and **countersigned by the Inspector of Schools/District Education Officer of the State last
> studied**."* [A — https://nbsenl.edu.in/cms/document/16/general_infos]

**The pattern now holds across three research streams and three layers.** Countersignature in India
is **condition-triggered by the pupil crossing a State line**, never a standing requirement of the
document, and never keyed to school type:

- **Intra-State TC** — no countersignature found anywhere in this region, at either layer.
- **Inbound out-of-State TC** — Bihar Education Code s.272, **JAC Reg. 5(iv)(a)**, **NBSE Ch.8**;
  and, in the west/south streams, Kerala, Puducherry, Maharashtra, Gujarat, Goa, DNH&DD.
- **Unrecognised sending school** — Bihar Education Code s.280 only.

> **Model countersignature as a property of the RECEIVING school's admission check, not of the
> issuing school's document.** ZenXii's issuing school never needs to obtain a countersignature for
> an ordinary local transfer. The obligation lands on the *receiving* school, and only when the
> pupil arrives from another State.

**A trap to avoid.** ASSEB Div-I *does* require an Inspector of Schools countersignature — but on
the **Change-of-Institution application form**, not on the TC:

> *"Download the Application Form, fill the required details and get it **signed by Head of the New
> Institution and counter singed by Inspector of Schools**."*
> [A — https://sebaservices.in/change-of-institution-home]

Reading that as a TC countersignature rule would be wrong. It is a *board-process* countersignature
on a *board form*.

### 2.3 · Migration Certificates are a board document everywhere in this region — seven boards confirmed issuing them

The prior wave established this for CBSE, CISCE and NIOS. It now holds across the state boards too,
with **named regulation numbers in four cases**:

| Board | Instrument | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **BSEB** | **Regulation 23**, BSEB Regulations 1964 | *"Migration certificate shall be issued to a candidate who passes the examination of the Board and intends to prosecute further studies outside the State."* Applied for through the head of institution. [A — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/30801339/] |
| **JAC** | **Reg. 2(ix)**, JAC Exam Regulations | *"a certificate issued by the Jharkhand Academic Council on the request of the candidates passing any examination of the Council for seeking admission to any Institution elsewhere."* [A] |
| **MBSE** | **Bye-Law 43** (+ Appendices IV & V) | *"'Migration Certificate' means a certificate issued by the Board to a student who is willing to prosecute studies outside the jurisdiction of MBSE"* (cl. 2(q)). The cleanest codification found. [A] |
| **NBSE** | Mgmt. of Examinations 2023, **Ch.26 cl.11** | Issued to successful HSLC/HSSLC candidates seeking admission outside the State; **not issued after 5 years** of passing. ₹400. [A] |
| **CHSE Odisha** | **Form No. 19** | ₹120; applied through the Head of Institution under the institution's seal; registration receipts surrendered in original. [A] |
| **COHSEM** | Notification **No. 3(3)/58/2023-HSC**, 11 Jan 2024 | **e-Migration Certificates** online from 15 Jan 2024, valid 6 months. [A — cohsem.nic.in/docs/Notice/mig.pdf] |
| **ASSEB Div-I** | portal rule (regulation NOT FOUND) | Exam years **2022 onward: digital only** — *"No hard copy will be issued from ASSEB, DIV-I."* Pre-2022: hard copy, same day. [A — https://sebaservices.in/migration-home-new] |

**ASSEB Div-II** likewise publishes both a Migration Certificate form and a *Duplicate* Migration
Certificate form [B].

> **This confirms and strengthens the design rule already in the corpus: drop "Migration
> Certificate" from the school-issued template set entirely.** Not one of the fifteen boards lets a
> school issue one. Seven of them issue it themselves. A ZenXii school's only role is to *forward*
> the pupil's application.

### 2.4 · MBSE is the only board found anywhere that regulates how a duplicate is MARKED

Duplicate-certificate *process* is well regulated across the region — affidavits, FIRs, police
reports, tiered fees. But the question the certificate designer actually needs answered is *how the
duplicate document must be endorsed*, and that came back **NOT FOUND for every board except one**.

> **MBSE Examination Bye-Laws, Bye-Law 41(3) (certificates) and the parallel marksheet rule:** an
> ordinary duplicate is issued on proof of identity plus fee; separately, *"A duplicate Marksheet
> **without stamping duplicate seal** may be issued … on production of: (i) An affidavit sworn
> before 1st Class Magistrate (ii) Police report testifying loss or damage."* [A]

So MBSE runs **two tracks — duplicate *with* the duplicate seal, and duplicate *without* it** — the
no-seal track being the one gated behind an affidavit and a police report. This is the inverse of
the intuition that an affidavit produces a more heavily-stamped document.

Everywhere else the marking rule is absent even where the process is fully documented:

| Board | Duplicate process | Marking rule |
|---|---|---|
| **BSEB** | **Reg. 24** — duplicate *and triplicate* where the original is *"either lost or destroyed by insect or any other cause"*; First Class Magistrate's affidavit (self-affidavit if over 18). Fees re-set by **Memo No. 1051 of 16.09.2022**: ₹200 → ₹2,500 by age of record, +₹500 urgent. [A] | **NOT FOUND** |
| **JAC** | **Reg. 8(iv)** — duplicate TC on affidavit **+ a copy of the FIR**. [A] | **NOT FOUND** |
| **NBSE** | **Ch.25** — FIR for loss (apply after 10 days); Fire Service report if burnt; damaged original surrendered; issued in 3 days; **₹1,000 fine for tampered documents**; *"The Board reserves the right not to issue duplicate/triplicate documents."* [A] | **NOT FOUND** |
| **CHSE Odisha** | ₹1,000; **Police General Diary No.** endorsed by the officer-in-charge; **Tahsildar's certificate** for flood/fire; defaced originals surrendered; form asks whether this is the **1st or 2nd** duplicate application. [A] | **NOT FOUND** (though duplicates are evidently *numbered*) |
| **COHSEM** | Affidavit before a 1st Class Magistrate + **newspaper insertion (English/Manipuri)** + police report + gazetted-officer attestation. [A] | **NOT FOUND** |
| **MBOSE** | e-application; **₹600**; duplicate certificate requires a **missing report from the nearest police station**. [B] | **NOT FOUND** |

**One board refuses duplicates outright, for one document type.** CHSE Odisha, Form 19 rules:
*"The rules of the Council don't provide for issue of a duplicate copy of the Migration Certificate
once issued."* A lost migration certificate requires a **fresh application at full fee**, citing the
original's number and date. [A] Note the asymmetry: CHSE *does* duplicate pass certificates (₹1,000)
but *never* migration certificates.

### 2.5 · The "recognised" a board means is not the "recognised" a state means

**MBOSE Act 1973, s.2(j), verbatim** — *"'recognised' means **recognised by the Board** for the
purpose of admission to the privileges of the Board or, prior to recognition by the Board, by any
University established by law in India or by any Board recognised by the State Government"*
[A — https://www.mbose.in/public/media_file/1767777712.pdf]

and **s.23(2)(vi)** empowers the Board to make regulations on *"conditions of recognition of
Schools."* [A]

So a Meghalaya school sits under **two independent recognitions** — the state's, granted by the
District School Education Officer in Form No. II under the RTE Rules (prior wave, Level A), and the
**Board's**, under MBOSE's own s.23(2)(vi) regulations. ASSEB makes the same split visible from the
other side: its Div-I application form is titled *"APPLICATION FOR **PERMISSION/RECOGNITION/SCHOOL
CODE**"* [A — https://site.sebaonline.org/photo/upload/2023/1678701511_3228.pdf], and it publishes a
list of *"Affiliated Schools under ASSEB (Div-I)"* [B]. MBOSE prices registration differently for
**affiliated vs unaffiliated** institutions (Class XI 2026: ₹300 vs ₹400) [A], which means MBOSE
knowingly registers students from schools it has *not* affiliated.

> **This directly supports `CONFLICTS.md` C-2 ("recognition denotes two different things") with
> primary text from a board's own Act.** A single `recognised` boolean on a school record is wrong
> for this region. Store state recognition and board affiliation as separate grants with separate
> identifiers — and note that MBSE goes further still, charging a *surcharge* rather than refusing
> registration to pupils of schools lacking state permission (prior wave, Level A).

## 3 · Per-board detail

### 3.1 · WBBSE — West Bengal Board of Secondary Education

**No WBBSE regulations instrument could be located.** wbbse.wb.gov.in's home, circular archive and
notification archive are client-side rendered and returned no static content to a fetch; Indian
Kanoon returned **zero results** for `"West Bengal Board of Secondary Education Regulations"`.
[Search trail recorded; 2026-09-12]

1. **TC format / fields — NOT FOUND.** What WBBSE regulates is the *receiving* side: the conditions
   under which a school may **accept** an incoming TC. That is not a format rule.
2. **Printed fields — NOT FOUND.**
3. **Countersignature — NOT FOUND.**
   ⚠️ **A trap worth naming.** *Dr. Narottam Gharah v. State of West Bengal* discusses an NOC under
   **r.23(f)** of the West Bengal Secondary Education Act regime — that NOC governs **Headmaster/
   Headmistress (staff) transfer**, not student TCs. Do not mistake it for a student-TC rule.
   [C — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/23536961/]
4. **Post-issuance duty — NOT FOUND** (deprioritised, not exhausted).
5. **Admission from another board/state — the one solid WBBSE finding.** Two circulars, quoted
   verbatim inside a Calcutta High Court judgment [B — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/447442/]:
   - **Circular No. S/851, 2 December 1988** — Rule 1: *"No first admission in a recognised
     secondary school shall be allowed to any class higher than class IX."* Admission up to Class IX
     by **15 July** needs no Board approval; **16 July – 15 September** needs Board approval plus a
     ₹5 fee. **Rule 5:** the head of the receiving institution must find the pupil *"fit for
     admission to the class in which he has been reading"*, and admission must be sought **within one
     month** of discontinuing prior studies. **Rule 9 (Class X transfers):** a TC from Class X of a
     recognised High School permits admission to Class X elsewhere **only up to 15 July**, subject to
     no break exceeding **30 days**, the same subjects studied in the previous school's Class IX, and
     compatible Work Education / Physical Education programmes — *"No admission to class X on the
     basis of transfer certificate shall be made beyond 15th of July."*
   - **Circular No. S/343, 6 June 2000** — reiterates the no-first-admission-above-Class-IX rule.

   ⚠️ **These are 1988 and 2000 circulars read through a 2002 judgment.** Neither original PDF was
   obtained, and neither was confirmed to be current in 2026. **Evidence B, and stale-dated. Do not
   hard-block an admission on a 1988 date cut-off without re-verification.**
6. **Migration certificates — NOT FOUND as an issuing power.** The same 2002 judgment shows WBBSE
   treating migration certificates as documents *presented to* it in a fraud-screening context, not
   documents it issues. No regulation number found. [C]
7. **Duplicates — NOT FOUND.**

### 3.2 · WBCHSE — West Bengal Council of Higher Secondary Education

**The thinnest board in the file.** Its Download Centre carries only exam-administration formats
(venue supervisor format, seal-opening format, confidential format); its FAQ covers only the
semester system, subject combinations and passing criteria. Indian Kanoon **rate-limited (HTTP 429)**
partway through this leg. **Questions 1–7: NOT FOUND.**

⚠️ *Supriyo Dey v. Secretary, WBCHSE* (2018) looks on-point and is not — it concerns a

Excluded deliberately. [C — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/196926155/]

### 3.3 · BSEB — Bihar School Examination Board

**Primary text read in full: the Bihar School Examination Board Regulations, 1964.**
[A — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/103831715/]

> ⚠️ **Two distinct instruments, do not conflate.** The **Bihar Education Code 1961** (ss.261–282 —
> the TC rules, already covered in the prior wave) and the **BSEB Regulations 1964** (the board's own
> examination regulations, covered here) are different documents governing different layers.

1. **TC format — a searched negative, not a gap.** The full regulation text contains **no section on
   transfer or school-leaving certificates**, and a targeted search for
   `"Bihar School Examination Board Regulations" "transfer certificate"` returned **0 results**.
   Bihar's TC field requirements live in the 1961 Education Code s.282, not here. [A]
2. **Printed — the Board's own certificates carry a facsimile.** Ch.II (1980 substitution): *"The
   Secretary may … designate an officer of the Board to check and verify each and every certificate
   and to **affix Secretary's facsimile** on the certificates of successful candidates."* [A]
   No index-number or school-code print mandate found.
3. **Countersignature** — governed by the 1961 Education Code; the 1964 Regulations add nothing.
4. **Post-issuance duty — YES.** Ch.IV, Reg. 1(ii): recognised institutions must *"supply to the
   Board on or before such dates as may be fixed by the Board such **returns and information** as may
   be required by the Board from time to time"* and *"**maintain such registers and records** of the
   students sent up by the School as may be required by the Board."* [A]
   Separately, a BSEB corrections form references a school-held **"TC Guard File" (टीसी गार्ड फाइल)**
   as a record a school may be required to produce in a major-correction case.
   [A — https://biharboardonline.com/files/Documents%20required%20for%20correction.pdf]
5. **Admission from another State — Reg. 22(x):** *"Students who have migrated from other States and
   have got regular admission in class XI of any school of the State will have to get themselves
   **registered within a month** of their admission."* [A]
6. **Migration certificates — Reg. 23.** See §2.3.
7. **Duplicates — Reg. 24.** See §2.4.

### 3.4 · JAC — Jharkhand Academic Council

**Primary text read in full: JAC *Regulations for Examinations*, made under s.27(b) and (d) of the
Jharkhand Academic Council Act, 2002.**
[A — https://jac.jharkhand.gov.in/assets/documents/ExamRegulations.pdf]

> This closes the gap the prior wave left open. It recorded *"The s.27 regulations were NOT FOUND"*
> and correctly flagged that the common "JAC Act 2005" attribution is wrong (it is **Act 2 of 2003,
> commenced 4 March 2003**). **The s.27 regulations are now found and read.**

1. **TC format — NOT FOUND, but a TC is defined.** **Reg. 2(xvi):** *"'Transfer certificate' means a
   certificate issued to a student by the Institution on his seeking a transfer to any other
   Institution by termination of his studies in the previous Institution."* No form or annexure. [A]
2. **Printed fields — NOT FOUND.**
3. **Countersignature — YES, and it is the region's clearest board-layer rule.** **Reg. 5(iv)(a)** —
   quoted in full in §2.2. The trigger is *"in the case of candidates outside the State"*. [A]
4. **Post-issuance duty — partial.** **Reg. 8(i):** *"It is only on production of the transfer
   certificate, his name may be entered in the Admission Register"*, the register being defined at
   Reg. 2(ii). **The duty sits on the receiving school; no return is filed with JAC.** [A]
5. **Admission from another board/state — detailed.** Reg. 5 (general conditions); **Reg. 6(a)** — a
   student from an institution *not* recognised by JAC, the State Government, CBSE or COBSE cannot be
   admitted on that institution's certificate; **Reg. 7.2(b)** — Class XI open to holders of a
   Secondary certificate from any Board recognised by JAC or COBSE as equivalent; **Reg. 7.3(c)** — a
   student completing Class XI **outside Jharkhand** needs the **Council Chairman's prior
   permission**. [A]
   **Reg. 8(ii)–(iii):** a student may not be admitted **above the class shown on the TC or Migration
   Certificate**, and may not migrate mid-session once sent up for the Council's examination. [A]
   *(Note this mirrors TBSE Reg. 5(b) — two boards, independently, requiring the TC to fix the
   admissible class.)*
6. **Migration certificates — Reg. 2(ix).** See §2.3.
7. **Duplicates — Reg. 8(iv):** *"A duplicate copy of the transfer certificate may be issued if the
   student submits an **affidavit** to the effect that the original transfer certificate has been
   lost, **alongwith a copy of the F.I.R.** lodged."* No marking rule. [A]
   ⚠️ **This is a duplicate *TC* rule — one of only two found in the whole region** (the other being
   MBSE's duplicate-seal tracks, which govern board documents rather than TCs). It is directly
   actionable for the certificate designer.

⚠️ **Not verified:** the JAC Secondary/Intermediate **School Affiliation Regulations** were located
but their text was not read. Further TC rules could live there.

### 3.5 · BSE Odisha — Board of Secondary Education, Odisha

### 3.6 · CHSE Odisha — Council of Higher Secondary Education, Odisha

**The Orissa Higher Secondary Education Act, 1982 was read (ss.1–38); two Council forms were read in
full.** [A — Form 19 and the Duplicate Certificate form, both at chseodisha.nic.in]

1. **TC format — NOT FOUND.** CHSE governs +2 institutions, not school-issued TCs.
2. **Printed — established for the Council's own documents.** The Migration Certificate application
   requires the **"Council Registration Number"** and must be forwarded under the **"Seal of the
   Institution"**; the duplicate-certificate form requires the original's **"Regd. No."** and
   *"Signature of the Principal with seal."* [A]
3. **Countersignature — not a TC rule.** Regular students apply *"through the Head of the Institution
   they last studied under this Council"*; *"Non-Collegiate students … should submit their
   application … direct to the Council **with an attestation by a Gazetted Officer**."* [A]
4. **Post-issuance duty** — the forwarding-memo mechanism only (Principal forwards under a Memo
   No./date to the Secretary, CHSE). [A]
5. **Admission from another board/state — NOT FOUND.**
6. **Migration certificates — Form No. 19.** See §2.3, including the **no-duplicate-ever** rule.
7. **Duplicates — detailed.** See §2.4.

> ⚠️ **CONFLICT, recorded and deliberately unresolved.** A secondary web source
> (blinkvisa.com, a visa-services blog) carries a passage attributed to the Orissa Higher Secondary
> Education Act 1982: *"Transfer certificate to be counter-signed: – (1) The transfer certificate in
> respect of any student of a registered school shall be in such form and issued in such manner as
> may be prescribed…"*. **The Act's own 38 sections were then read and this clause does not appear in
> them.** The snippet may belong to a different Act, to subordinate Regulations, or may simply be
> wrong. **Evidence D, contradicted by a Level-A primary read. Do not rely on it.** If a TC
> countersignature rule genuinely exists in Odisha, this is the thread to pull — from a gazette copy
> of the Regulations, not from the blog.

### 3.7 · ASSEB — Assam State School Education Board (Div-I ex-SEBA, Div-II ex-AHSEC)

**Primary text read in full: the AHSEC *Academic Regulation for Secondary Courses (Class XI–XII),

## 4 · Autonomous District Councils and school certificates — question 8

> **Answer: Autonomous District Councils have a real, constitutionally-grounded role in PRIMARY
> schooling. No certificate, TC, recognition or countersignature role was found for any ADC, in
> either state. For Meghalaya this is now well corroborated. For Assam's three councils it remains
> largely unresearched on the board side.**

The prior wave recorded this as *"a genuine tension between the Constitution and the RTE Rules"* and
correctly declined to guess. **The board-side documents do substantially settle it — in the
direction of "no ADC certificate role" — through one decisive statutory carve-out.**

### 4.1 · The statutory carve-out that resolves the tension

**MBOSE Act 1973, s.12, closing proviso — verbatim, read directly from the Act PDF and independently
re-verified:**

> *"Provisions that **the power of the Board shall not extend to the Primary Schools established,
> constructed or managed by the District Councils**, unless the State Government, after consultation
> with the District Council concerned by notification empowers the Board to exercise the powers
> aforesaid in respect of the Primary Schools mentioned above."*
> [**A** — https://www.mbose.in/public/media_file/1767777712.pdf]

Two things follow, and they matter more than anything else in this section.

**First, this independently corroborates the Sixth Schedule.** The phrase *"established, constructed
or managed"* is **the Sixth Schedule paragraph 6 formula, word for word** — para 6 empowering a
District Council to *"establish, construct or manage primary schools…"*. The prior wave could only
reach para 6 at Level B (indiacode 403, mea.gov.in 404) and that remains true today; **but a State
Act read at Level A now reproduces its operative language**, which is strong corroboration of the
*scope* of ADC power even though the gazetted constitutional text is still unread at Level A.

**Second, it draws the boundary.** MBOSE's jurisdiction is carved **away from** ADC primary schools.
ADC schools are **primary**; board certificates and the TC rules that matter attach to **secondary
and higher-secondary examination pathways**. In Meghalaya the two domains are **mutually exclusive by
statute** — which is precisely why no ADC certificate rule exists to be found.

### 4.2 · Meghalaya — a well-corroborated negative

- **KHADC (Khasi Hills).** Its **complete legislation index — 71+ Acts, Rules, Regulations and Bills
  from 1951 to 2024** — was retrieved and parsed in full, then searched for
  `educat|school|student|certificat|primary`. **Zero hits.** The corpus covers land, forests,
  markets, chieftainship succession (Syiem/Sirdar/Headmen), Khasi lineage custom, traditional
  medicine, solid waste, town committees, professions tax and administration of justice. **There is
  no education legislation in it at all.**
  [A — https://khadc.nic.in/acts_regulations_rules_bills.html]
  ⚠️ This **qualifies the prior wave's finding** that "KHADC has created an Education Department"
  among its seven departments [B — khadc.nic.in/executive.html]. **A department exists; legislation
  does not.** A council may administer schools without regulating certificates — and on this evidence
  that is exactly what KHADC does.
- **Meghalaya DCA Department** confirms it coordinates *"the affairs of the 3 (three) Autonomous

- **GHADC (Garo Hills)** — `ghadc.gov.in` and `ghadc.meghalaya.gov.in` both **DNS-failed**;
  `ghadc.nic.in` resolved but shows no Education department and only a Land Record Management System;
  `/governance/departments.html` unreachable. **INCONCLUSIVE.**
- **JHADC (Jaintia Hills)** — `jhadc.nic.in` returned a title banner and no navigable content.
  **INCONCLUSIVE.**
- ⚠️ **An unresolved contrary signal.** An Indian Kanoon fragment notes that in Meghalaya *"the

This aligns with, and now explains, the prior wave's Level-A finding that **Meghalaya's RTE Rules
r.2(1)(h) define "local authority" as the concerned Joint Director — not the ADC.**

### 4.3 · Assam — one concrete data point, and it cuts the same way

**The ASSEB Div-I affiliation form contains a field at 1(c): *"(iii) Name of VC : (in case of
autonomous council)"*.** [A — https://site.sebaonline.org/photo/upload/2023/1678701511_3228.pdf]

So an Autonomous Council appears in the board's affiliation workflow **as a territorial/address
descriptor only**. The application is still routed *through the Inspector of Schools* and
countersigned by the **Chairman SMDC** — **never by a council officer.**

> **In ASSEB's own paperwork, an Autonomous Council is a *place*, not an *authority*.**

⚠️ **But the Assam councils were not researched on the council side at all in this pass.**
`bodoland.gov.in`, `kaac.assam.gov.in` and the Dima Hasao council site were **never fetched**. The
prior wave's Assam findings therefore stand unchanged and unchallenged:
- The **Assam RTE Rules 2011 name "Autonomous Council" ~20 times as co-equal with the State**, and
  its distribution list names the BTC Secretary (Education) and Director (Education) as recipients.
  [A, prior wave]
- **Gauhati High Court, *Sewa Ashram Girls M.E. School v. Bodoland Territorial Council* (31 Aug
  2016)** shows the **Secretary, BTC actually according recognition** to a school.
  [B — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/88051126/, prior wave]

**So for Assam the recognition question remains genuinely open — while the *certificate* question
has one data point pointing at "no council role."** Do not merge these.

### 4.4 · What the product should do

> **Do not model an Autonomous District Council as a certificate authority.** No ADC anywhere in
> this region was found to issue, countersign, prescribe or register a school certificate.
>
> **If an ADC field is ever needed, model it the way ASSEB itself does — as a location/jurisdiction
> attribute of the school, never as an issuing or countersigning authority.**
>
> **Leave the Assam *recognition* question open.** BTC demonstrably recognises schools (prior wave,
> Level B). That is a recognition-layer fact and belongs in the recognition model as
> "authority uncertain", exactly as the prior wave concluded. Nothing found here disturbs it.

## 7 · What this file should and should not change in the product

**Should change:**

1. **Add JAC and NBSE to the inter-State countersignature rule.** Both at Level A, both triggered by
   the pupil arriving from **another State**, both naming the **DEO / Inspector of Schools of the
   sending State**. This is now a three-source pattern in this region alone (with Bihar s.272) and
   corroborates `CONFLICTS.md` C-12 across a third layer.
2. **Model countersignature on the RECEIVING school's admission check, never on the issuing school's
   document.** A ZenXii school issuing an ordinary local TC needs no countersignature anywhere in
   this region.
3. **Add a `transfer_register` to the statutory-register set for Assam.** ASSEB inspects it as a
   condition of recognition (Level A). This is the only hard post-issuance duty found.
4. **Keep "Migration Certificate" out of the school-issued template set** — now confirmed across
   seven more boards. A school's only role is to forward the application.
5. **Store board affiliation and state recognition as separate grants.** MBOSE Act s.2(j) defines
   "recognised" as *recognised by the Board*; ASSEB's own form is titled "PERMISSION/RECOGNITION/
   SCHOOL CODE"; MBOSE prices affiliated and unaffiliated schools differently. One boolean is wrong.
6. **Fix the NBSE domain** wherever it appears — `nbsenagaland.com` is a squatted betting site; the
   board is at `nbsenl.edu.in`.
7. **Record MBSE's duplicate-seal rule** as the only marking rule found in the region — and do not
   generalise it to any other board.

**Should NOT change:**

8. **Do not assert a prescribed TC format for any board in this region.** Fifteen searched, none
   found. The generic profile that enforces nothing remains correct.
9. **Do not encode "MBOSE prescribes nothing"** — its Examination Regulations exist and are unread.
10. **Do not record BSEM or BSE Odisha as having no rules.** Both are access failures.
11. **Do not model an ADC as a certificate authority** — and do not close the Assam *recognition*
    question, which stays open exactly as the prior wave left it.
12. **Do not enforce the WBBSE 1988/2000 admission date cut-offs** without re-verifying them against
    the original circulars.
13. **Do not implement the blog-sourced Odisha "counter-signed" clause.** It failed a Level-A check.
