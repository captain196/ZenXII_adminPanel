### The countersignature answer, stated once

**In every one of the six jurisdictions, countersignature is a duty on the RECEIVING school when a
pupil arrives from OUTSIDE the state — never a precondition on the ISSUING school.** No state in
West & Central India requires a BEO/DEO to countersign a TC/LC that one of its own schools issues to
one of its own pupils. **Any product that gates local TC issuance on an education-officer
countersignature would be enforcing a rule that does not exist here.**

This materially corrects the `r.8(vii)` entry flagged in `HOW_ELIGIBILITY_ACTUALLY_WORKS.md` §3: the
countersignature rule is real, but it is an **inbound-admission** rule, not an **issuance** rule.

### Terminology: "TC" vs "LC"

| Jurisdiction | Statutory name |
|---|---|
| Maharashtra | **Leaving Certificate** (LC) — *शाळा सोडल्याचा दाखला*. "Transfer Certificate" is not the instrument's legal name |
| Gujarat | **School Leaving Certificate** — *શાળા છોડ્યાનું પ્રમાણપત્ર*, prescribed form **નમૂનો-૯**. "Transfer Certificate" does not appear as the name |
| Goa / DNHDD | **Leaving Certificate**, though the rules use "transfer or school leaving certificate" interchangeably (Goa r.116(1)) |
| MP | **Transfer Certificate** (स्थानांतरण प्रमाण पत्र) — but no MP rule prescribes it |
| Chhattisgarh | **स्थानांतरण प्रमाण पत्र** (Transfer Certificate) — named in CGBSE clause 16(4) |

**The LC/TC split is not cosmetic.** In Maharashtra and Gujarat the LC is a *statutorily formatted
instrument* whose validity depends on the form and the signature; in MP and CG the TC is an
unformatted document that merely has to carry the recognition number. **A single "TC" template
across the region would be wrong in four of the six.**

## 0 · THE CENTRAL BASELINE (applies in all six jurisdictions)

Before any state rule, three central provisions bind every school in scope. **All [A] — statutory
text read verbatim this session.** Source caveat: retrieved from a bare-act compilation of *The
Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 (35 of 2009)*, marked "Last Updated
31 December 2019". **indiacode.nic.in, legislative.gov.in and education.gov.in all refused
automated fetch (403 / HTML interstitial) on 2026-09-08**, so the gazette copy was not obtained.
The text below is self-evidently the Act, but a gazette confirmation is still owed.

### s.18 — recognition is the licence to function

> **"(1) No school, other than a school established, owned or controlled by the appropriate
> Government or the local authority, shall, after the commencement of this Act, be established or
> function, without obtaining a certificate of recognition from such authority, by making an
> application in such form and manner, as may be prescribed."**

- **s.18(2)** — the prescribed authority issues the certificate "in such form, within such period,
  in such manner, and subject to such conditions, as may be prescribed", and **"no such recognition
  shall be granted to a school unless it fulfils norms and standards specified under section 19."**
- **s.18(3)** — withdrawal must be **by an order in writing**, must **name the neighbourhood schools**
  that will absorb the children, and requires **an opportunity of being heard**.
- **s.18(4)** — from the date of withdrawal, no such school shall continue to function.

**The "form, period, manner and conditions" are expressly delegated to state rules (s.38(2)(g)-(h)).
This is precisely why validity periods differ state to state and why none may be assumed.**

### s.19 — norms, the three-year clock, and the money

- **s.19(1)** No school shall be established or recognised unless it fulfils the Schedule norms.
- **s.19(2)** Pre-existing schools had **three years** to comply, at their own expense.
- **s.19(3)-(4)** Failure → recognition withdrawn → **"no school shall continue to function."**
- **s.19(5)** — the enforcement teeth:
  > **"Any person who continues to run a school after the recognition is withdrawn, shall be liable
  > to fine which may extend to one lakh rupees and in case of continuing contraventions, to a fine
  > of ten thousand rupees for each day during which such contravention continues."**

### s.5 — the right of transfer, and the TC

> **"(2) Where a child is required to move from one school to another, either within a State or
> outside, for any reason whatsoever, such child shall have a right to seek transfer to any other
> school … for completing his or her elementary education.**
>
> **(3) For seeking admission in such other school, the Head-teacher or in-charge of the school
> where such child was last admitted, shall immediately issue the transfer certificate:**
>
> **Provided that delay in producing transfer certificate shall not be a ground for either delaying
> or denying admission in such other school:**
>
> **Provided further that the Head-teacher or in-charge of the school delaying issuance of transfer
> certificate shall be liable for disciplinary action under the service rules applicable to him or
> her."**

**Four hard consequences for the product, valid in every state in this report:**

1. **"shall immediately issue"** — there is no lawful queue, review step or approval gate between a
   request and a TC for an elementary-stage child. State-level "within a week" rules (Maharashtra
   SS Code r.29.1, Goa r.124, DNHDD r.106) are the *outer* limit, not the standard.
2. **A missing TC can never block an admission** for classes I–VIII. Any admission flow that makes
   the TC a required field for an elementary admission is unlawful on its face.
3. **Withholding is a disciplinary offence** against the head-teacher personally.
4. **s.5 covers only elementary education (classes I–VIII).** Above Class VIII the state codes
   revive — and they are markedly more restrictive (Maharashtra SS Code r.17 bars admission without
   an LC outright). **The scope boundary at Class VIII is the single most important branch in the
   whole certificate rule engine.**

⚠️ **Direct conflict, unresolved and important.** Maharashtra SS Code **r.29.1** and Goa **r.125**
and DNHDD **r.107** all permit refusing a leaving certificate for **non-payment of fees**. For
classes I–VIII this cannot stand against RTE s.3 (free education) and s.5(3). For classes IX–XII
it probably can. **We should not ship a fee-based TC block that applies to elementary classes**, and
the product should not present "unpaid dues" as a globally valid hold reason. Legal sign-off needed.

### 1.3 Validity period and renewal

- **Three years.** Form II grants **provisional** recognition: *"convey the grant for provisional
  recognition to the (name of the school) for Class __ to Class __ **for a period of three years
  w.e.f. __ to __**."* **[A]** (Maharashtra RTE Rules 2011, Form II)
- Form II condition 1: **"The grant for recognition is not extendable and does not in any way
  imply any obligation to recognize or affiliate beyond Class VIII."** **[A]**
- **Renewal lead time: NOT FOUND.** Neither Rule 11 nor Form II states a window for applying to
  renew (contrast Andhra Pradesh's "90 days before expiry"). Searched the full rules text for
  "renew"— the only hit is condition 18, which concerns **renewal of the society's registration**,
  not of recognition. **Do not assert a Maharashtra renewal lead time in the product.**
- ⚠️ **Conflict / open question.** Rule 11 describes a *one-time* post-commencement
  self-declaration exercise, yet Form II issues a *three-year* term, and real orders are issued
  fresh in 2022 for 2022-25. The rules text does not describe the steady-state renewal cycle at
  all. The three-year term is certain; the mechanism for obtaining the next one is **NOT FOUND**.

### 1.6 THE LEAVING CERTIFICATE — Maharashtra's distinctive instrument

**Maharashtra does not issue a "Transfer Certificate". Its statutory instrument is the *School
Leaving Certificate* (LC), Marathi *Shala Sodlyacha Dakhla*.** This is not merely a naming
difference — the Code prescribes the form, the grounds for refusal, the fee, and what makes it
*invalid*. All of the following is **[A]**, read from the Code text.

| Code rule | Subject | Substance |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule 17** | Production of LC | **"No recognised school shall admit a pupil without a leaving certificate from the last recognised school which he had attended."** Form prescribed in **Appendix Four**. If the pupil never attended such a school, a **parent/guardian declaration** to that effect is taken instead |
| **Rule 18** | Admission without an LC | If the applicant says the last school **refused** the LC, the new Head writes to the former Head; **if no satisfactory explanation within 10 days**, he "will be entitled to **admit the pupil provisionally**" and must report to the appropriate authority |
| **Rule 22.1** | **Countersignature** | Admission of a pupil **from another State or Union Territory** "shall be made only if the leaving certificate of the pupil is **countersigned by the Education Officer or an equivalent authority in that State/Union Territory**"; if not countersigned, the Head **may admit provisionally pending countersignature** and report the case |
| **Rule 22.3** | Foreign pupils | LC or similar "duly **countersigned by the Educational authorities of that country**", plus visa check and an entrance test |
| **Rule 23** | Class placement | "no pupil shall be placed in a standard **higher than that for which his leaving certificate shows him to be qualified**" without special sanction |
| **Rule 24.1** | Unrecognised schools | **"No admission shall be made on the strength of a leaving certificate from an unrecognised school."** |
| **Rule 24.2** | " | Pupil with no recognised-school history: **affidavit before a stipendiary Magistrate** + **written test in all subjects** → provisional admission to the standard he is found fit for, **subject to confirmation by the appropriate authority**. Affidavit must state why he did not join a recognised school, the unrecognised school's name, standards/subjects attended, exams passed |
| **Rule 24.3** | " | If he *did* previously attend a recognised school, he must produce that school's LC (or duplicate) plus an affidavit |
| **Rule 24.4 note** | " | Report on **Appendix Five** — "Form of report regarding admission of pupils from unrecognised schools" |
| **Rule 26.4** | Register corrections | Applications to change DOB/caste etc. in the General Register are entertained **only for a pupil still attending the school** |
| **Rule 27** | Scrutiny | Suspected "unauthorised addition or alteration" in an LC must be reported immediately; pupil not admitted pending reply; if already admitted, he may sit the annual exam provisionally but **his result must not be declared** without orders |
| **Rule 28** | Application | Application for an LC **must be in writing by the parent or guardian** (a major may apply personally). "School should issue leaving certificates without unnecessary delay" |
| **Rule 29.1** | **Refusal** | Refusal without adequate justification, **or delay of over one week** in issuing it or in replying with reasons, "may entail action against the school". **The only two lawful grounds for refusal are: (i) non-payment of fee and/or other dues; (ii) rustication under Rule 34** |
| **Rule 29.2** | " | After rustication, no LC until the debarment period expires |
| **Rule 30** | **Duplicate** | Parent must state in writing what became of the original and why a duplicate is needed; if unsatisfied the Head **may require an affidavit before a stipendiary Magistrate**; **every duplicate "shall be marked with the word 'Duplicate' in red ink at the top"** |
| **Rule 31** | **Fee** | **No fee** may be charged within **one year** of leaving (or of the public-exam result). After that, **Re. 1 per subsequent year, maximum Rs. 5**. **Rs. 3** for a duplicate LC, a date-of-birth extract, or any other General Register extract |
| **Rule 32.1** | **Validity** | **"No leaving certificate is valid unless it is in the form prescribed in this Code (vide appendix Four) and is signed personally by the Head of the school."** |
| **Rule 32.2** | " | In the Head's absence, a person authorised by the management signs **as in-charge Head** |
| **Rule 33** | Exam result | Promotion/detention must be noted in the **Remarks** column for pupils withdrawn at/around year end |
| **Rule 34** | Rustication | Grounds include securing admission by a **false or forged LC**, and **tampering with LC entries** |

**Rule 29.1 is the single most product-relevant rule in the Code: it converts "issue the LC" into a
one-week legal duty with an exhaustive two-item refusal whitelist.** Note it *does* permit
withholding for unpaid dues — the opposite of the position in some other states. (The
citizen-guide figure of "7 working days" **[C]** — https://mahadocguide.in/school-leaving-certificate-guide-maharashtra/
— is consistent with the Code's "over one week", which is the citable primary basis.)

#### Prescribed LC form — Appendix Four (*vide* Rule 17) **[A]**

Header: *"No change in any entry in this certificate shall be made except by the authority issuing
it and any infringement of this requirement is liable to involve the imposition of penalty such as
that of rustication."* Plus **Register No. of the Pupil** and **Name of School**.

The **12 mandated fields**:

1. Name of pupil in full
2. Caste and sub-caste — **only** for pupils of Backward Classes, and the category (SC/ST etc.)
3. Place of birth
4. **Date of birth, month and year according to the Christian era, both in words and figures**
5. Last school attended
6. Date of admission
7. Progress
8. Conduct
9. Date of leaving school
10. Standard in which studying, and since when
11. Reason of leaving school
12. Remarks

Closing attestation: **"Certified that above information is in accordance with the school
register."** Signed by **Class Master** *and* **Head of the School**.

Notes to the form (all **[A]**):
- **N.B. 1** — DOB (per the Christian era *and* the **New National Calendar**) and the standard, in
  **both figures and words**.
- **N.B. 2** — **"These entries shall be in manuscript and not typewritten."**
- **N.B. 3** — accelerated promotion must be specified in Remarks with standard, year and school.
- **N.B. 4** — named scholarships must be listed in Remarks with the Index Card attached
  (Govt. Circular, E. & Y.S.D. No. SCH. 1076/24969/XXXV of 30 September 1977).

⚠️ **N.B. 2 ("manuscript and not typewritten") is directly hostile to a printed/digital LC.** It
almost certainly no longer reflects practice — SARAL reportedly prints LCs — but **we have found no
notification superseding it.** Do not silently ignore it; record it as an open conflict.

**Two signatories.** Rule 32.1 requires the **Head's personal signature**; Appendix Four also
carries a **Class Master** signature line. A single-signature LC template would be non-conforming.

**Countersignature summary for Maharashtra:** **NOT required for an ordinary intra-Maharashtra
LC.** Countersignature is required only when a pupil arrives **from another State/UT** (by that
state's Education Officer, r.22.1) or **from another country** (r.22.3) — i.e. it is a duty on the
*receiving* school to check, not on the *issuing* Maharashtra school to obtain. **[A]**

#### Registers behind the LC **[A]**
The Code requires schools to preserve **"Leaving Certificates received from other schools"** and
**"Counterfoils of Leaving Certificates issued to pupils"** as school records. The **General
Register** is the source the LC attests to (Appendix Four's closing certification). This confirms
the register-is-authority model in `HOW_ELIGIBILITY_ACTUALLY_WORKS.md` §3, for Maharashtra
specifically: **a counterfoil must exist for every LC issued.**

#### Case law flag **[C]**
*Janabai d/o Himmatrao Thakur v. State of Maharashtra*, Writ Petition No. 8085 of 2017, Bombay High
Court (Aurangabad Bench), **Full Bench**, reported as decided 17 October 2019 — reported to hold

### 2.6 THE LEAVING CERTIFICATE — reg 12(14) and નમૂનો-૯

**Gujarat's instrument is the L.C. — "શાળા છોડ્યાનું પ્રમાણપત્ર", literally "school leaving
certificate". The prescribed form is titled "SCHOOL LEAVING CERTIFICATE / શાળા છોડ્યાનું પ્રમાણપત્ર".
The term "Transfer Certificate" does not appear as the name of the instrument in either the 2012 RTE
Rules or the 1974 Regulations. [A]**

**The operative rule: Regulation 12(14)**, heading **"શાળા છોડ્યા અંગેના પ્રમાણપત્ર કાઢી આપવા અંગે"**,
amended by Education Dept resolution **મશબ/૧૨૨૦/૮૪૩/છ dated 24-05-2021**. All **[A]**:

| Sub-rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **12(14)(1)** | Application **in writing by the guardian/parent**; an adult (પુખ્તવયનો) student may apply himself. The school **must issue the LC in નમૂના-૯ within at most SEVEN DAYS** of the application |
| **12(14)(2)** | The school **cannot refuse** an LC without proper reasons; if it does not issue one, **the reasons must be given to the applicant in writing within seven days** |
| **12(14)(3)** | A **duplicate** requires an application **plus an affidavit before an Executive Magistrate** stating where the original went and why a duplicate is needed |
| **12(14)(4)** | **FEE.** **No fee in any circumstances** if demanded **within two years** of leaving, or of the result date of the public examination (SSC/HSC/transfer exam) the school entered the student for. After that: **₹1.00 per year, maximum ₹5.00** |
| **12(14)(5)** | **FORM AND SIGNATURE.** Must be in the prescribed **નમૂના-૯**, **hand-written in ink** (શાહીથી હાથે લખેલું), **signed by the head of the school in his own ink**, and bear the **school's seal** — *"તો જ તે કાયદેસર ગણાશે"* (**"only then shall it be legally valid"**) |
| **12(14)(6)** | In the head's absence, signed by a person **authorised by the management (સંચાલક મંડળ) and approved by the District Education Officer** |

⚠️ **Note 12(14)(2) is materially stricter than Maharashtra.** Gujarat gives **no exhaustive list of
lawful refusal grounds** — it simply requires "proper reasons" plus **written reasons within seven
days**. Maharashtra by contrast whitelists exactly two grounds. **Do not port Maharashtra's
"unpaid dues" refusal ground into Gujarat; it has no Gujarat basis.**

**Related regulations [A]:**
- **Reg 12(4)** — a student who left another school must submit, **with the admission application,
  the last school's LC in નમૂના-૯**. Provisos: if the child never attended any school, take a
  **declaration**; if the previous head has not issued the LC, the head **may admit provisionally
  (કામચલાઉ)** after consulting the previous head, report to the officer, and act on the officer's
  order.
- **Reg 12(10)** — without special approval, no placement in a class higher than the LC shows the
  student eligible for; class-placement fallback where no LC exists.
- **Reg 12(13)** — after admission, enter the student in the **General Register in નમૂનો-૧૦**.
- **Reg 12(ક)** — *"શાળા રજિસ્ટરોમાં જન્મ તારીખ, નામો વગેરેમાં ફેરફારો"*: **no change to any
  school-register entry without the officer's prior written approval**; only the guardian of a
  genuinely-attending student may apply; DOB change needs a certified birth-register extract,
  certified vaccination certificate, certified baptism certificate (Christians), or an affidavit
  before an Executive Magistrate or Notary. Case law confirms **Regulation 12(A)** is the route and
  that **once a student has left, amendment of the LC is not permissible** — corrections then go to
  the jurisdictional Magistrate. **[C]**
  *(This matches Maharashtra's Full Bench position in §1.6 — a genuine cross-state pattern.)*

### 2.7 Countersignature — the precise answer **[A]**

- **Within Gujarat, on an LC issued by a Gujarat school: NO countersignature by BEO/DEO/DPEO is
  required.** The form's signature block is **Clerk + Class Teacher + Principal + school seal**.
  Nothing in reg 12(14) or નમૂનો-૯ requires an external counter-signature.
- **Two DEO-adjacent requirements exist and are different things:**
  - **Reg 12(14)(6)** — if the **head is absent**, the substitute signatory must be authorised by the
    management **and approved by the DEO**. This is **approval of the person, standing in advance —
    not countersignature of each certificate.**
  - **Reg 12(9)(ક)** — **inbound**: a student from a school in **another State or UT** may be admitted
    to a registered Gujarat school **only if the LC bears the countersignature (સામી સહી) of the
    education officer of that State/UT**. Proviso: if not countersigned, the head shall get it
    countersigned; **until then the student may be admitted provisionally** with a full report to the
    officer. **Reg 12(9)(ઘ)** applies the analogue to **other countries** (valid visa for the study
    period, a test before admission, and an LC countersigned by that country's education authorities).
- **Per school type (govt / grant-in-aid / self-financed / CBSE): NOT FOUND.** The Regulations draw
  the aided/non-aided distinction for **class permission** (reg 10(1)) — **not for LC signature.** No
  differentiated LC countersignature rule was located for any school type, and none for CBSE schools.

### 2.9 Admission without an LC; unrecognised schools

**(a) Elementary / RTE — NOT FOUND. [A], all rules 1–33 + both Appendices read.**
The Gujarat RTE Rules 2012 contain **no rule rendering RTE Act s.5(3)**. Closest provisions:
- **Rule 3(2)** — age proof: where a birth certificate is unavailable, (a) Hospital/ANM register
  record, (b) Anganwadi record, or (c) **declaration through an affidavit of age by the parent or
  guardian** shall be deemed proof.
- **Rule 3(3)** — extended admission period = **six months**; a child admitted after it completes
  studies with special training determined by the head.
- **Rule 8(2)** — *"No school shall take donations for admission, or shall take interview of parents
  and / or take test of the children."*
- **Form 2, Condition 6** — *"The School shall not deny admission to any child for lack of **age
  proof** or on the ground of religion, caste, creed or race, place of birth or any of them."* —
  **age proof, not LC.**

**Do not assert a Gujarat rule number for the no-TC rule.** For classes I–VIII the operative
provision is **central RTE Act s.5(2)-(3)** applying directly, plus s.14(2) on age proof.

**(b) Secondary — Gujarat DOES have an express rule, and it is a PROHIBITION. [A]**

**Reg 12(11)(ક):** *"માન્ય ન થયેલી શાળામાંથી શાળા છોડ્યાના પ્રમાણપત્રના આધારે પ્રવેશ આપી શકાશે નહિ."*
— **admission may NOT be granted on the basis of a leaving certificate from an unrecognised school.**
Such a student is regularised instead under **reg 12(10)(2)** (age-based class placement, 5+ years,
one class per year).

- **Reg 12(11)(ખ)** — from an unrecognised school, **never** having attended a recognised/approved
  primary school: parent must swear an **affidavit before an Executive Magistrate**; the student sits
  a **written test in all subjects**; the head admits **provisionally**, subject to the officer's
  approval. Affidavit must state (1) reasons for not attending a recognised school, (2) the name of
  the unrecognised school, (3) standards attended and subjects studied, (4) examinations passed.
- **Reg 12(11)(ગ)** — from an unrecognised school but **having previously** attended a recognised
  primary or secondary school: (1) produce **that recognised school's LC, or a duplicate**, (2)
  affidavit before an Executive Magistrate, (3) a **written test in all subjects of the standard he
  would normally have completed**, counting one standard per year from leaving the last recognised
  school; then **provisional admission** subject to the officer's approval.
- **Reg 12(4) proviso** — even for a *recognised* previous school, if the previous head has not issued
  the LC, the receiving head **may admit provisionally** after consulting the previous head and
  reporting to the officer. So Gujarat's secondary rules **do** provide a no-LC pathway — but it is
  **provisional admission plus an officer's order, never an unconditional right, and never on an
  unrecognised school's LC.**
- **Reg 12(6)** — admission normally **within 30 days** of the start of the academic year; late
  admission needs a reported justification (illness, bereavement, guardian transfer, change of
  residence).

**Note the striking convergence:** Gujarat reg 12(11) and Maharashtra SS Code r.24 are structurally
the same rule — affidavit before a magistrate, written test in all subjects, provisional admission
subject to the officer's confirmation. **Both derive from the same Bombay-Presidency-era code.** That
shared ancestry is why Maharashtra and Gujarat can share a rule template that MP and CG cannot.

### 3.4 Scope — per class-range

### 4.4 Scope — per class-range, medium and faculty

**Per class-range, decisively, on both routes. [A]**
- Form II grants recognition **"for Class ____ to Class ____"**, disclaiming obligation beyond
  Class VIII.
- **Clause 1(4):** *"एक ही शैक्षणिक सत्र में संस्था को हाईस्कूल एवं हायर सेकण्डरी की मान्यता **एक

- **Per medium** — **clause 15(1):** an institution adding English medium must treat it as **a

Gujarat's analogue, reg 35 (છૂટા થવા માટેનું પ્રમાણપત્ર), is the **staff relieving certificate** | **Do not present a student character certificate as legally mandated** |

formats, field lists or countersignature on them. **Migration must be blocked at school level
entirely.**

### What this changes in the eligibility model

1. **Recognition validity is not a single field.** Three of six jurisdictions grant it **per
   class-range**; MP and CG additionally scope it **per stream and per medium**; Goa and DNHDD have
   **no fixed term at all**. `HOW_ELIGIBILITY_ACTUALLY_WORKS.md` Phase 1 should model an **array** of
   `{regime, classFrom, classTo, medium, stream, authority, certNo, validFrom, validTo}`.
2. **Two jurisdictions de-recognise silently.** Maharashtra's Form-2 condition 19 (deficiencies
   uncured by the end date → *"recognition shall automatically stand cancelled"*) and CGBSE clause 20
   (two years' non-renewal → *स्वतः निरस्त*). **Expiry arithmetic is the legal state, not a warning.**
3. **The countersignature rule in the corpus is mis-scoped** — it is an inbound-admission rule, not an
   issuance rule (see the summary table note). `r.8(vii)` should be re-scoped, not merely re-dated.
4. **Print the recognition number.** MP r.19 proviso and CG clause 16(4) independently require it on
   the document; Gujarat achieves the same through the S.S.C./H.S.C. Index No. on નમૂનો-૯. This is
   the strongest cross-state pattern found and is safe to implement region-wide as a default.
5. **Block school-issued migration certificates outright.** In all six, migration is a **board**
   instrument.
6. **The Class VIII boundary is the master branch.** Below it, central RTE s.5 forbids gating
   admission on a TC and forbids delay; above it, the state codes revive and are markedly more
   restrictive. **Any fee-based TC hold must be disabled for classes I–VIII.**
