**Scope** West Bengal · Bihar · Jharkhand · Odisha · Assam · Sikkim · Meghalaya · Tripura ·
Mizoram · Manipur · Nagaland · Arunachal Pradesh

**Verification date** 2026-09-08. Every claim below carries a URL and an evidence level.

## Evidence levels used

| Level | Meaning |

## 2 · The central floor that sits above all twelve jurisdictions

**RTE Act 2009 s.5 — "Right of transfer to other school".** Read at level **A** ·
https://indiankanoon.org/doc/154784849/

- **s.5(1)**: where a school has no provision for completing elementary education, the child has a
  right to seek transfer to another school.
- **s.5(2)**: a child moving *"from one school to another, either within a State or outside, **for
  any reason whatsoever**"* has a right to seek transfer.
- **s.5(3)**: the head-teacher or school in-charge **"shall immediately issue the transfer
  certificate"**; and the operative proviso — **"delay in producing transfer certificate shall not
  be a ground for either delaying or denying admission in such other school."** A head-teacher who
  delays issuance faces **"liability for disciplinary action under the service rules applicable to
  him or her."**

**This is the single most important rule in this file.** It is the only prescribed TC obligation
that demonstrably binds every one of the twelve states, and it runs in the *opposite* direction to
most product intuitions: the TC must be issued immediately and on demand, and the *receiving*
school may not gate admission on it.

**Scope limit:** s.5 governs *elementary* education, i.e. through **class VIII**. No equivalent
central protection for classes IX–XII was found — and this matters acutely in this region, because
the RTE recognition forms in Assam, Meghalaya and Tripura all expressly disclaim any obligation
"beyond Class VIII".

**RTE Act s.14 — proof of age.** Age is determined from the birth certificate; *"No child shall be
denied admission in a school for lack of age proof."* [B — https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Right_of_Children_to_Free_and_Compulsory_Education_Act,_2009]

**RTE Act s.15 — no denial of admission.** Admission at the start of the academic year or within a
prescribed extended period; *"no child shall be denied admission if such admission is sought
subsequent to the extended period."* [B — same]

**RTE Act s.18 — the statutory hook for recognition.** No school other than one established, owned
or controlled by the appropriate Government or local authority *"shall … be established or
function, without obtaining a certificate of recognition."* [B — same]

**RTE Act s.30 — completion certificate.** No child shall be required to pass any Board examination
till completion of elementary education; every child completing elementary education *"shall be
awarded a certificate, in such form and in such manner, as may be prescribed."* [B — same]

## 3 · Cross-cutting patterns worth designing against

**3.1 · The MHRD model form propagated verbatim, and it is the only place the validity period
lives.** Assam, Meghalaya and Tripura all annex a recognition certificate ("Form II" / "Form No.
2") whose operative sentence is word-for-word identical:

> "I convey the grant for **provisional recognition** to the ______ (name of the school with
> address) **for Class ___ to Class ___ for a period of three years** w.e.f. ___ to ___."

and whose condition 1 is likewise identical:

> "**The grant for recognition is not extendable** and does not in any way imply any obligation to
> recognize/affiliate **beyond Class VIII**."

**The rule bodies of these states state no validity period at all** — the three-year term exists
only in the annexed form. Any product that reads the rule text and concludes "no expiry" will be
wrong for these three states; any product that generalises the three-year term to Odisha, Mizoram,
West Bengal or Jharkhand will also be wrong. **The form governs, and only where a form exists.**

**3.2 · Recognition is per class-range, not school-wide, wherever it could be checked.** Assam,
Meghalaya and Tripura scope it on the face of the certificate ("for Class ___ to Class ___");
Odisha scopes it by notification to "Elementary school (Class-I to VIII)"; Manipur bands its land
and building norms across seven class-range categories; Nagaland's registration notification is
scoped "Pre-Primary upto Elementary". Meghalaya's Form II goes further and forbids the workaround
directly — condition 11: *"**No unrecognized classes shall run** within the premises of the school
or outside in the same name of the school."*

**Product implication: a single school-wide "recognised" boolean is wrong for this region.** Model
recognition as one or more grants, each carrying a class range and its own expiry.

**3.3 · There is a recognition code number, and it is not the UDISE code or the affiliation
number.** Assam and Meghalaya Form II both carry: *"The **recognition Code Number** allotted to your
school is ……… This may please be noted and quoted for any correspondence with this office."*
Odisha's Form I carries a "Recognition number" field. Nagaland issues a separate **Registration
Number** from the Directorate. **[A]** This is a distinct identifier the ERP should store.

**3.4 · Recognition and board affiliation are two different grants, and several states say so
explicitly.** West Bengal's NOC is issued *"in favour of the applicant school seeking affiliation
under any Board located in West Bengal or outside"* — i.e. recognition is upstream of affiliation
[A]. Manipur's 2025 notification separates *"recognition"* (state) from *"affiliation to the BSEM/
COHSEM/ CBSE"* (board) [A]. Tripura's TBSE regulations require a school to be **both** *"duly
recognized by the Government of Tripura **and** affiliated to the Tripura Board of Secondary
Education"* [A]. This confirms the model already recorded in `HOW_ELIGIBILITY_ACTUALLY_WORKS.md`:
**authority to issue a TC flows from recognition, not affiliation.**

**3.5 · No state in this region prescribes a TC format — except Bihar, which prescribes fields.**
Twelve states searched; the only mandated TC content found anywhere is the **Bihar Education Code
1961 s.282** (reason for leaving; last terminal marks on the reverse, signed by the Head) and one
**TBSE** field requirement (the class the pupil is eligible to enter). Everything else is NOT FOUND.
**The software must not assert a prescribed TC format for any state in this region.**

**3.6 · Countersignature exists in exactly one state, and it is condition-based, not
school-type-based.** Only Bihar (and, presumptively, Jharkhand by carry-over) has a
countersignature rule. It attaches to the *circumstances of the transfer* — inter-State origin, or
an unrecognised sending school — **not** to whether the school is government, aided, unaided or
CBSE-affiliated. Everywhere else in the region: **NOT FOUND**.

## 4 · Per-state detail

### 4.1 · WEST BENGAL

**Rules.** West Bengal Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Rules, 2012, *Kolkata
Gazette, Extraordinary, Part I, 16 March 2012*.
[A — https://righttoeducation.in/sites/default/files/RTE%20Rules%20West%20Bengal.pdf ; cross-check
https://www.legitquest.com/act/west-bengal-right-of-children-to-free-and-compulsory-education-rules-2012/CEC3]

- **Rule 10** — authority, form and manner of applying for a certificate of recognition under
  s.18(1). r.10(1) verbatim: any unaffiliated and/or unrecognised unaided school *"shall apply **in
  the Form appended to the Schedule I** for a certificate of recognition … **within 3 months**"*. [A]
- **Self-declaration does not exist in West Bengal.** Unlike the Central Model Rules — and unlike
  Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram — WB replaced the
  self-declaration route with **application + fee + preliminary hearing + district inspection +
  State Level Committee**. This is a real divergence and must not be assumed away.
  [A — r.10(1)–(14), no self-declaration provision anywhere in the Rules]
- **The certificate is a "No Objection Certificate".** r.10(11) verbatim: the State Level Committee
  *"shall … recommend 'No Objection Certificate' in favour of the applicant school seeking
  affiliation under any Board located in West Bengal or outside … **This shall be construed as the
  Certificate of Recognition for all purposes.**"* r.10(17): the school *"shall display a copy of
  such certificate at a conspicuous place in the school all the time."* [A]
- **Process.** Application to the **District Inspector of Schools (Secondary Education)** or **DI
  (Primary Education)**, *"irrespective of the Board to which such school intends to get
  affiliated"*, or online to the State through **www.wbsed.gov.in** (r.10(2)). Preliminary hearing
  within **45 working days**. Fee (r.10(5), non-refundable): Primary rural ₹3,000; Primary urban
  ₹5,000; Upper Primary rural ₹7,000; Upper Primary urban ₹10,000. [A]
- **District Level Inspection Team** (r.10(7)): DI of Schools convenor + a WBBSE nominee + a nominee
  of the Chairman, District Primary School Council + **a nominee of the Madrasah Board**. Reports
  within 60 days. **State Level Committee** (r.10(9)–(11)) chaired by the **Director of School
  Education**, including the Secretaries of WBBPE, WBBSE, the Chairperson of Rabindra Mukta
  Vidyalaya and the Secretary of the Madrasah Board. [A]
- **Rule 11** — withdrawal: show-cause with **4 weeks** to reply; Committee of Inquiry of 3 or 5
  members; de-recognition operative from the following academic year, naming the neighbourhood
  schools. Appeal to the Secretary, School Education Department within 4 weeks. [A]
- **Rule 11(4) verbatim — the WB no-TC rule:** *"**No school shall deny admission to the affected
  children on the grounds of non-production of transfer certificates.**"* (Stated in the
  de-recognition context.) [A]
- **Rule 12** — the **Board concerned** must maintain a website publishing all schools granted,
  refused, pending and withdrawn recognition. [A]
- **Rule 18** — the completion certificate (s.30) is issued in **Schedule II**, by the school, and
  is **"duly authenticated by the Secretary of the affiliated Board of the school"**, signed by the
  Head and one assistant teacher within three months. **This is the one WB certificate requiring
  external counter-authentication — and it is by the Board Secretary, not the DI.** [A]
- **Rule 7** — age proof: where a birth certificate is unavailable, admission proceeds on an
  ICDS-centre record, a registered medical practitioner's certificate, or a declaration; the parent
  may furnish the birth certificate **within 6 months** after admission. [A]
- **Validity/renewal: NOT FOUND.** Full-text scan for "valid", "renew", "three years", "expiry"
  attaches no term to the NOC. **Do not implement a WB recognition expiry.** [A — negative]
- **CONFLICT to flag.** A **WBBSE notification dated 28.09.2016** declares a Birth Certificate and a
  **Primary School Leaving Certificate** *"mandatory for admission to Class V"*
  [B/C — https://wbxpress.com/mandatory-documents-admission-class-v/], while RTE s.14(2)/s.15 and WB
  r.11(4) forbid denial of admission for want of documents. **Do not implement "mandatory" as a hard
  block.**
- **Boards.** WBBME operates under the **WB Board of Madrasah Education Act 1994** as amended 2014
  [B — https://wbbme.org/act/], with High Madrasah (423), Junior High Madrasah (89), Senior Madrasah
  (102), plus **Alim & Fazil**, and a published list of **614 recognised madrasahs**
  [B — https://wbbme.org/recognition/ ; http://wbbme.dsscwtl.in/list-of-recognised-madrasahs/].
  **WBBSE, WBCHSE and WBBME publish no TC/migration regulation** — NOT FOUND across
  https://wbbse.wb.gov.in/ and https://wbchse.wb.gov.in/ (Download Centre carries only
  exam-administration formats). `wbbpe.org` did not resolve on 2026-09-08.
- **Prescribed TC format and countersignature: NOT FOUND.** Searched wbxpress for "transfer
  certificate" and "school leaving certificate"; indiankanoon for "transfer certificate
  countersigned District Inspector of Schools West Bengal" (97 hits, none on point).
- ⚠️ On 2026-09-08 neither `wbsed.gov.in` nor `banglarshiksha.gov.in` resolved from this environment
  (DNS failure, not a 404). Treat the live portal URL as unconfirmed today. [A — own probe]

### 4.2 · BIHAR

**Rules.** बिहार राज्य बच्चों की मुफ्त एवं अनिवार्य शिक्षा नियमावली, 2011. Hindi gazette read
page-by-page as images.
[A — https://bepcssa.in/en/pdf/Bihar%20State%20Free%20&%20Compulsory%20Education%20Rules%20-%202010.pdf]

> ⚠️ **Naming trap.** Patna High Court and the Bihar Education Project Council call these variously
> the "Bihar Right to Education Rules, 2011", the "Bihar State Free & Compulsory Education Rules
> 2011", and the file is named "…- 2010.pdf". Same instrument.

- **Rule 11(1)** — a **three-member district committee**: जिला शिक्षा पदाधिकारी (**DEO**) — convener;
  a Deputy-Collector-level officer nominated by the District Magistrate — member; जिला शिक्षा अधीक्षक
  (**District Superintendent of Education**) — member secretary. [A]
- **11(2)** self-declaration in **प्रपत्र-1 (Form-1)** within **six months**; **11(3)** made public
  within 15 days; **11(4)** on-site inspection within 3 months; **11(5)** recognition granted **in
  प्रपत्र-2 (Form-2) by the committee within 15 days**; **11(6)** a non-recognised school may
  re-apply within 3 years; **11(7)** schools not meeting norms within 3 years of the Act **shall
  cease to function**; **11(9)** schools running unrecognised are **liable to penalty**, the
  committee being the competent authority. [A]
- **Rule 12** — withdrawal: one month to explain; **prior approval of the Human Resource Development
  Department required**; **no appeal lies to the State Government** against an order under
  r.12(1)(ग); order effective from the succeeding academic year, naming the neighbourhood school. [A]
- **Rule 9** — age proof: hospital/ANM register record, anganwadi record, or a parent's declaration.
  **Rule 10** — extended admission period **six months**, then special training. [A]

**Recognition in practice — Patna High Court, *Rahul Kumar (Minor) v. Union of India*, 01.09.2026**
[A/C — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/129700447/]:
> "the school … had been granted formal provisional recognition (**Prasvikriti**) by the **District
> Education Officer, East Champaran**, under Section 18 … read with **Rule 11 of the Bihar Right to
> Education Rules, 2011**"

and — *"The aforesaid recognition was **valid for a period of three years**, w.e.f. 18.12.2021 to
18.12.2024"*, renewed *"through the **e-Sambandhan** portal"*.

- ⚠️ **CONFLICT — validity.** The **Rules prescribe no validity period** (pages 9–10 read in full),
  but administration treats recognition as **three years** with renewal via e-Sambandhan. The
  instrument imposing the term was **NOT FOUND**. Model this as *"3 years per current practice,
  source unverified"* — never as a rule citation.
- ⚠️ **The e-Sambandhan URL is NOT FOUND.** `esambandhan.bihar.gov.in`, `esambandhan.in`,
  `www.esambandhan.in`, `esambandhan.biharboardonline.com` and `education.bihar.gov.in` all failed
  to resolve on 2026-09-08. [A — own probe]
- **UDISE+ is load-bearing in practice.** In *Rahul Kumar* the school was wrongly shown as
  unrecognised because of *"a technical/data discrepancy in the UDISE portal"*, corrected only after
  the school filed **Form A01**; a Navodaya admission had been refused on that basis. **For the
  product: UDISE+ status, not the paper certificate, is what third parties actually check.** [C]

**Bihar Education Code, 1961 — the strongest TC finding in the whole region.**
Full code at https://indiankanoon.org/doc/99596033/

| Section | Holding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **s.261** | A pupil admitted to primary school from another school must produce a TC | C — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/10934001/ |
| **s.262** | Applications for a TC must be made by the parent or guardian | C — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/111461677/ |
| **s.267** | No admission above Class 7 of a recognised High School without a TC | C |
| **s.272** | **"A pupil from a school in another State … must produce a transfer certificate countersigned — (a) if the school is in another State, by the Inspector of Schools in charge of the area within which the school is situated; (b) if the school is in an Indian State, by the officer of the State authorised in this behalf."** | **A** — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/141774149/ |
| **s.276** | **"A transfer certificate must be granted by the Headmaster/Principal on receipt of an application"** — exceptions for expulsion or unpaid dues; once dues clear, issuance is mandatory. **Middle schools — same day; high schools — within three days.** | **A** — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/165183248/ |
| **s.280** | **"Transfer certificates from schools not recognised by the Department must not be accepted unless countersigned either (i) by the District Education Officer … in the case of unrecognised secondary schools or (ii) by the Sub-divisional Education Officer…"** | **A** — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/64272551/ |
| **s.281** | A **duplicate** TC may be granted where the original is lost | C — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/164218019/ |
| **s.282** | **"Every transfer certificate must contain a statement of the reasons … owing to which the pupil is leaving the school"**, and the **reverse must carry the pupil's marks in the last terminal examination with its date, authenticated by the Headmaster's/Principal's signature** | **A** — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/22933891/ |

**So Bihar's countersignature rule is condition-based, not school-type-based:** required when the
TC comes from **another State** (s.272) or from an **unrecognised school** (s.280, by the DEO or
Sub-divisional Education Officer). **No countersignature is required for an ordinary TC from a
recognised Bihar school** — s.276 puts it squarely on the Headmaster/Principal.

⚠️ **Caveats before enforcing:** (i) the Code is from 1961 and its "Inspector of Schools" /
"Sub-divisional Education Officer" designations have changed; (ii) for Classes I–VIII, RTE s.5 and
ss.14–15 override any document-based refusal of admission; (iii) **a printed TC form/proforma was
NOT FOUND** — s.282 mandates *fields*, not a layout; (iv) **a govt-vs-private split in the
countersignature rule: NOT FOUND**.

### 4.10 · MANIPUR — the most distinctive regime in the region

**Manipur does not run certificate eligibility off its RTE rules.** It has a dedicated statute: the
**Manipur Private School (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2017** and the **Manipur Private School
(Registration and Regulation) Rules, 2024**, notified in **Manipur Gazette No. 30 Part-II(A),
Wednesday 6 November 2024 (Kartika 15, 1946)**, pp. 187 onward — amended by the **(1st Amendment)
Rules, 2026**.
[A — https://manipureducation.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Manipur-Private-Schools-Registration-and-Regulation-Rules-2024.pdf,
read as page images; Act at .../2024/12/Manipur-Private-Schools-Registration-and-Regulation-Act-2017.pdf;
amendment at https://manipureducation.gov.in/the-manipur-private-school-registration-and-regulation-1st-amendment-rules-2026/]

**The two-stage model — registration ≠ recognition. This is the finding that matters most for the
product.**
- **Rule 4(3)**: on enquiry within **ninety days** of application, the Registering Authority issues a
  **Registration Certificate (Annexure-II)**. [A]
- **Rule 4(5)** verbatim: *"The certificate granted under sub-section (3) of section 4 of the Act
  shall remain **valid for a period of two years** within which the educational agency shall
  **establish the school and obtain recognition from the recognition authority**."* [A]
- **Rule 5**, proviso, verbatim: *"Provided that **admission of students to the school can be made
  only after the school obtains recognition from competent authority**."* [A]

→ **Registration is a two-year licence to *build*; recognition is the separate licence to *admit* —
and therefore the true anchor for document-issuing authority in Manipur.**

**Definitions that matter** [A]:
- **r.2(s) "Registering Authority"** = the **Director of Education(S), Government of Manipur**, or an
  officer/authority appointed or authorised by the State Government by notification.
- **r.2(w) "Zonal Education Officer" (ZEO)** = an officer of the School Education Department at
  district or sub-division level. **Manipur's field officer is the ZEO, not a BEO or DEO.**
- **r.2(n) "Local authority"** = *"a Zilla Parishad, **Autonomous District Council**, Municipal
  Corporations, Municipal Councils established under an Act"* — **Manipur expressly includes the ADC**
  (contrast Meghalaya, which expressly does not; see §5).
- **r.2(r) "Private school"** excludes an institution *"providing or imparting **only religious
  instruction**, but not any other instruction"* → **a purely religious madrasa/maktab falls outside
  the registration regime entirely.**

**Per class-range.** r.4(2)(d)(i) land norms are stated per category — Pre-Primary 0.15 acre;
Pre-Primary/I–V 0.50; I–VIII 1.25; I–X 1.50; I–XII 1.75; IX–XII 0.75; XI–XII 0.50 (+0.25 with a
hostel; a lease must run at least thirty years). Building norms likewise band by class range (up to
V: ground floor only; up to VIII: first floor; up to XII: second floor). Norms must be met **within
two years of issue of the Registration Certificate**. [A]

**Transitional rules** [A]:
- r.4(1) provisos: existing institutions apply **within 90 days** of notification; *"private schools
  already in existence with valid recognition shall be provided an **extended period of 4 (four)
  years** for obtaining fresh recognition."*
- **Rule 6**: an existing school with valid registration that applied within 90 days may continue but
  **shall close down immediately if the certificate is refused**; one that did not apply **shall not
  continue after the 90 days**; existing schools with valid recognition unable to meet s.4(2)
  conditions may function **two years without registration**, then **must close**.
- **Rule 7** suspension or cancellation by the Registering Authority; **Rule 8** appeal to the
  Appellate Authority **within thirty days**, whose order is final; **Rule 9** penalty for running a

## 5 · Autonomous councils, Sixth Schedule areas, and madrasa regimes

This was called out in the brief as *"often missed"*, and it is the section with the most product
consequence: **in several of these states the body that recognises a school, and therefore stands
behind its certificates, is not the state education department at all.**

### 5.1 · The constitutional power

**Sixth Schedule, Paragraph 6 — "Powers of the District Council to establish primary schools, etc."**
The District Council for an autonomous district may **establish, construct or manage primary
schools**, and may, **with the previous approval of the Governor, make regulations** for their
regulation and control — and in particular **may prescribe the language and the manner in which
primary education shall be imparted**. [B — quoted consistently across
https://khadc.nic.in/archive/sixth_schedule.html , https://megdca.gov.in/ and standard reproductions
of the constitutional text. ⚠️ **I did not read the gazetted bare text of Para 6 directly** —
indiacode and mea.gov.in both refused the fetch — so this is B, not A.]

**Distinguish the constitutional POWER from actual PRACTICE.** Para 6 is a power to establish and
manage *primary* schools and to make regulations with the Governor's approval. It is not, by itself,
evidence that a given council today recognises private schools or signs their TCs. Below, the two
are kept apart.

### 5.2 · Assam — the councils ARE a parallel recognition authority (established, not inferred)

**The Assam RTE Rules 2011 treat "Autonomous Council" as an authority co-equal with the State
Government and the local authority throughout** — roughly 20 occurrences, including r.11(1) (the
recognition exemption covers schools *"established, owned or controlled by the Government,
**Autonomous Council** or the local authority"*), r.11(1)(d) (inspection by an officer authorised by
*"the Government, **Autonomous Council** or the local authority"*), r.11(8), and duties rules 4, 5, 8
and 20. [A]

Definitions, verbatim [A]:
- **r.2(5)**: *"'Autonomous Council' means the Autonomous Councils of the **two Hill Districts** or the
  **Bodoland Territorial Council**, as the case may be;"*
- **r.2(25)**: *"'Autonomous Council' means the Autonomous Council constituted under sub-paragraph (i)
  of paragraph (ii) of the **Sixth Schedule to the Constitution of India**;"*

(The rules carry **two** definitions of the same term in two definition lists — a drafting
duplication worth flagging, though both point to KAAC, Dima Hasao and BTC.)

**The rules' own distribution list names the council education officers as recipients** — *"The
Principal Secretary, Karbi Anglong Autonomous Council, Diphu / Dima Hasao, Haflong / Bodoland…"*,
*"The Secretary, Education, Bodoland Territorial Autonomous Council, Kokrajhar"*, *"The Director,
Education, Bodoland Territorial Autonomous Council, Kokrajhar"*. [A]

**Operational corroboration for BTC.** Gauhati High Court, *Sewa Ashram Girls M.E. School v. The
Bodoland Territorial Council & 7 Ors*, 31 August 2016: the **Secretary, BTC "accorded
permission/recognition to the School with effect from 1.1.1997"**; the respondents include the BTC
Principal Secretary, the Secretary (Education) BTC, the Director of Education BTC, **and** the
District Elementary Education Officer, Kokrajhar. The judgment applies the Assam RTE Rules 2011
infrastructure standards to a BTC school.
[B — https://indiankanoon.org/doc/88051126/]

→ **BTC has its own Education Department (Secretary Education + Director of Education) that has in
fact accorded school recognition, operating alongside a DEEO in the same district.**

⚠️ **NOT FOUND for the Assam councils:** (i) the authoritative **transferred-subjects list** under the
Sixth Schedule / the 2003 BTC Accord — the Memorandum of Settlement was not retrieved, so I cannot

### 5.3 · Meghalaya — a genuine tension between the Constitution and the RTE Rules

- Meghalaya is **the only state in the North-East whose entire area is governed by the Sixth

  (KHADC), Jaintia Hills (JHADC) and Garo Hills (GHADC) — coordinated by a dedicated **District
  Council Affairs Department**. [B — https://megdca.gov.in/ ; https://khadc.nic.in/archive/Introductory.htm]

  administer the various functions as envisaged under the Sixth Schedule"*.
  [B — https://khadc.nic.in/executive.html]

  respective districts"** — **not** the ADC — and vest the entire recognition function in the

missed.** Constitutionally the ADCs may establish and manage primary schools and regulate them; under

## 7 · Method limitations that shaped these gaps

Disclosed so the gaps can be reproduced and closed rather than re-guessed.

- **The session's WebSearch budget (200/200) was exhausted partway through.** All three research
  streams hit the ceiling. The later work relies on WebFetch and direct HTTP against known URLs plus
  Indian Kanoon's own search, which biases coverage **toward legal texts and away from circulars and
  news**. DuckDuckGo, Mojeek, Bing and Ecosia all served bot challenges or 403s to direct fetches, so
  no general-web substitute was available. **Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim deserve a second pass with
  working search** — they are the two states where the deficit did the most damage.
- **indiacode.nic.in blocked every access path** (403 to WebFetch, 500 from its search endpoint).
  This is why **no state Act year is asserted** except where a board's own site or a court record
  supplied it.
- **Many official PDFs are image-only scans with no text layer** — the Manipur Rules 2024 and
  unrecognised-schools notification, the Nagaland registration notification, the Bihar RTE Rules, the
  Jharkhand RTE Rules (both official copies), the Jharkhand portal guideline, the Odisha recognition
  notification, and the TBSE 2025 gazette. **These were read by rendering the pages to images and
  reading them directly**, which is the source of most Evidence-A claims for Manipur, Nagaland, Bihar
  and Tripura. Jharkhand's rule text could not be read this way and is Evidence A *via a legal-text
  database* rather than via the gazette.
- **The Mizoram RTE Rules 2011 PDF has a broken (non-Unicode) font encoding** and extracts as
  garbage; it too was read as page images. **The Hindi text layer of the Bihar rules PDF is likewise
  a legacy non-Unicode font** — do not trust any tool that claims to have grepped either.
- **State domains that were unreachable on 2026-09-08:** `wbsed.gov.in`, `banglarshiksha.gov.in`,
  `wbbpe.org`, `esambandhan.*` (five variants), `education.bihar.gov.in`,
  `schooleducation.nagaland.gov.in`, `nagaland.gov.in`, `kaac.assam.gov.in`, `btc.gov.in`,
  `btcedu.in`, `ndc.gov.in`, `ssa.sikkim.gov.in`, `sikkimarchives.gov.in`. Several of these are DNS
  failures rather than 404s, so the resources may well exist and simply be unreachable from here.
- **Paragraph 6 of the Sixth Schedule was not read in its gazetted form** (indiacode and mea.gov.in
  both refused), so it is cited at **B**, not A.

## 8 · What this file should and should not change in the product

**Safe to encode now (Evidence A):**
- Recognition is **per class-range** with an **expiry** in Assam, Meghalaya and Tripura (3 years,
  provisional, non-extendable, capped at Class VIII), and per class-range in Odisha, Manipur and
  Nagaland.
- A **recognition code number** exists and is distinct from UDISE and affiliation numbers.
- **Manipur** requires a two-stage model: Registration Certificate (2 years) → recognition, with
  **admission lawful only after recognition**.
- **Bihar**: the TC is granted by the Headmaster/Principal (same day for middle schools, three days
  for high schools), must state the reason for leaving, must carry last-terminal marks on the
  reverse, and requires countersignature **only** for inter-State or unrecognised-school origin.
- **Tripura**: a TC must indicate the class the pupil is eligible to enter.
- **RTE s.5**: the TC must be issued immediately, and a missing TC may not delay or deny admission
  through Class VIII — everywhere.

**Must NOT be encoded:**
- Any recognition validity period for **West Bengal, Jharkhand, Odisha, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim or
  Arunachal Pradesh** — none was found.
- Any prescribed TC *format* for any state in this region.
- Any countersignature requirement outside Bihar/Jharkhand.
- A Bihar Act number for the recognition validity, or an Assam Act number for the madrassa repeal —
  both are unresolved conflicts.
- A Jharkhand state inspection fee or security deposit — **struck down** on 13 May 2025.
- Anything at all for **Arunachal Pradesh and Sikkim** beyond the generic profile.

**Two open questions worth a second pass**, in priority order:
1. **Who actually recognises primary schools in Meghalaya** — the state DSEO under the RTE Rules, or
   the ADC under Sixth Schedule para 6. The two texts point different ways and the practice is
   unknown. The same question is wholly open for TTAADC (Tripura) and the three Mizoram councils.
2. **Arunachal Pradesh from scratch**, with working search. It is currently a complete blank.
