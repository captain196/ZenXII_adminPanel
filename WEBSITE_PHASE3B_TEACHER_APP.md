**Why this matters:**
- You don't stop in the middle of a period because the signal dropped
- You don't risk losing attendance because you tried to submit at the wrong moment

No. The administration sees institutional data — attendance, marks, homework — that you legitimately enter. They do not see your in-app activity (when you opened the app, what screens you visited, how long you spent on each). We do not provide that data to administrators, period.

Never. Parents communicate with you through the in-app chat. Your personal number is not shared, displayed, or accessible to them.

Your school configures the default quiet-hour window for the institution. They cannot remove quiet hours entirely — those are a structural boundary in the platform. You can extend the school's default with personal quiet-hour preferences in your settings.

You can switch on "Out of Office" mode for the duration of your leave. Notifications pause. Parent messages are held until you return or routed to your substitute (where the school enables routing).

You don't need to be. We've trained teachers ranging from first-year newcomers to 30-year veterans, and most are productive within one staff training session. The interface is designed around teacher workflows, not tech-product conventions.

Nothing — you continue. The app stores your input locally and syncs the moment you're back online. You'll see a small "syncing" indicator at the top; you don't have to do anything.

# Cross-Section Design & Tone Notes

These notes keep the Teacher App page professionally consistent across all 20 sections.

## Tone Pillars

1. **Empowerment, not surveillance.** Every feature is framed as something the teacher gains, never something the teacher is monitored on. Avoid words like "track," "monitor," "audit teacher activity."
2. **Respectful of professional autonomy.** Teachers are professionals, not data-entry clerks. The copy assumes intelligence, experience, and competence.
3. **Time as the primary currency.** Across every section, the question being implicitly answered is: *"Does this give me time back, or take time from me?"* Lean into time-saved framing wherever credible.
4. **Boundaries are first-class.** Quiet hours, personal-number privacy, weekend protection are not afterthoughts — they're elevated and protected.
5. **Dignified, not heroic.** Avoid "teachers are heroes" tropes — they ring hollow. Just speak to teachers as adults doing a complex job.

## Visual Style

- **Photography:** Real teachers in real classrooms or staffrooms — candid, mid-action, commissioned. No stock smiling-teacher-with-clipboard imagery.
- **Color palette:** Calm professional palette — deep slate, muted teal, warm amber as accent. Avoid bright "celebration" colors. The visual feel should be focused, mature, and quietly confident.
- **Typography:** A clean confident sans (Inter, GT Walsheim, or similar). Slightly heavier weights for headlines than the Parent App page — teachers respond to professional clarity.
- **Phone mockups:** Real product screenshots, drop-shadowed, slightly tilted. Where possible, show teacher-specific UI screens (substitute notification, marks entry with grade preview, attendance grid).

## Conversion Funnel

Teachers arrive in three modes:
1. **Skeptical (their school just adopted [Brand]):** Worried about being asked to use "another app." The Time-Back, Boundaries, and FAQ sections are the conversion levers.
2. **Curious (they heard about [Brand] from a colleague):** Want to understand what's different from their current tools. The Daily Workflow Snapshot, Communication, and Self-Service sections answer them.
3. **Ready (they have institutional credentials and want to install):** Direct download. Hero, app store section, and final CTA serve them.

- A sticky bottom CTA bar with the Google Play badge is highly effective on mobile.
