# ZenXii — Indian School Document Ecosystem
## Research Baseline for the Document Generation, Printing, Issuance, Verification & Archival System

**Status:** RESEARCH BASELINE — no code written, nothing committed, nothing deployed.
**Compiled:** 2026-08-16
**Evidence base:** two adversarially-verified research passes (106 + 112 agents), plus a direct
source survey of the ZenXii codebase.
**Companion document:** `DOCUMENT_ENGINE_ARCHITECTURE.md` (the technical ADR — Firestore
decision, collection shape, numbering, immutability, verification).

---

## PART 1 — Executive summary

**281 documents** were identified across the Indian school lifecycle and graded for evidence.
**96** of the Level A claims were then adversarially re-checked against primary text; **41 were
refuted (43%)**.

The refutations are the most valuable output of this study, because of *how* they failed.

> **Almost nothing was refuted for being fabricated.** The citations were, overwhelmingly, real
> and quoted accurately. What failed was **scope**: requirements that bind one board, one state,
> or one scheme's participants had been written up as national obligations on all Indian schools.

That single pattern is the architectural finding of this whole exercise.

### The five findings that most change how we build

**1. The document catalogue must be scoped per school, not global.**
CBSE-affiliated schools number roughly **30,000 out of ~1.5 million schools in India**. So
"CBSE Affiliation Bye-Law 14.19 requires these registers" is true for ~2% of the market. Verifier
after verifier made the same correction: *"Binds only CBSE-affiliated schools — not CISCE, NIOS,
or any of the ~30 state boards."* Likewise the Kerala Education Rules bind Kerala government/
aided/recognised schools only; the Delhi School Education Rules bind NCT aided schools only;
the pre-matric scholarship obligations bind only institutions participating in that scheme.

**A flat, global document list would be wrong for nearly every school we sell to.** The catalogue
must resolve as `applicable_documents = f(board, state, school_type, schemes_participated)`.
This is not a nice-to-have configuration layer — it is the core data model.

**2. The legal substrate is moving underneath us, right now.**
Verification caught several authorities that were **superseded**, including within the last year:
- The **Code on Wages 2019** was brought into force by **S.O. 5322(E) dated 21-11-2025** (ss.1–41,
  43–66) — this changes payslip/wage-record obligations, and it commenced nine months ago.
- The cited **EPF Scheme** text was found repealed weeks before the research ran.
- A **CBSE bye-law repealed in 2010** (CCE-related; the scheme itself was discontinued 2017-18)
  was cited as live.
- The **GST** entry quoted "verbatim" was the **superseded 2017 original**.
- One citation carried a **fabricated verbatim quote** — caught only because a verifier pulled
  the primary PDF.

**Requirement:** every row in the document catalogue needs a `verifiedOn` date and a named owner.
A document requirements table without a freshness field will silently rot into a compliance
liability.

**3. Retention is almost entirely unlegislated — 79 of 281 documents have any stated period.**
CBSE prescribes exactly one concrete retention rule (Affiliation Bye-Law 14.19(b): exam papers,
answer sheets and internal assessment to **end-September of the next academic year**). The
admission register, attendance registers, staff service records and financial documents — the
records schools actually keep for decades — carry **no prescribed period at all**.

Every retention default we ship is therefore **our invention**, and must be labelled as a ZenXii
recommendation rather than a legal requirement.

**4. The no-dues gate on Transfer Certificates is a live legal trap.**
Rule text permitting it exists (Kerala Education Rules Ch. VI r.17(2); Tamil Nadu Educational
Rules r.40–42; CBSE Exam Bye-Laws r.8(vi)) — **but the courts have gutted it**, including at the
secondary stage where RTE does not reach: Delhi HC (LPA 393/2014), Kerala HC (2025:KER:69076),
Madras HC DB (22.07.2024, a TC "is not a tool for the schools to collect arrear fees"), Karnataka
HC (2025:KHC:5986, the school holds the certificate "as a trustee"). RTE s.5(3) independently
makes the TC immediate and non-withholdable at the elementary stage.

Every competing school ERP ships this as a hard block. **We must not.** See the ADR §9A.2.

**5. Several "documents" are not documents.**
A recurring verifier correction: the cited clause imposes a *duty* (to train staff, to constitute
a committee, to maintain a record) but prescribes **no document and no format**, so it cannot sit
in a document-requirements table as though it were a printable artefact. The GST education
exemption is the clearest case — it is Level A legal background that prescribes nothing printable.
Keeping these as "documents" would inflate the build scope with things that cannot be built.

### What this means for scope

The honest headline: **the P0 set is much smaller than 281, and much more board-conditional than
a feature list would suggest.** The engine's value is not in shipping 281 templates — it is in
being *correctly scoped, correctly graded, and honest about what is law versus what is custom*.

## PART 2 — How to read the evidence grades

| Level | Meaning | How it may be used in product |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Explicitly prescribed by a named authority with a citable clause | May be described to schools as required — **but only within its verified scope** |
| **B** | Strongly established administrative practice | Describe as standard practice, never as law |
| **C** | Common school practice, no legal basis found | Offer as a template; never imply obligation |
| **D** | ZenXii design recommendation | Our opinion. Must never inherit a Level A citation |

**Two disciplines carried from the verification pass:**

1. **The legal duty is almost always to MAINTAIN or ISSUE a record — never to "generate it in
   software."** Every "therefore the ERP must…" is Level D and must be kept in a separate column
   from the citation. Claims arriving with the two welded together were a repeat finding in both
   passes.
2. **Never cite a secondary reproduction of a bye-law.** Two were found factually wrong against
   primary text (a schoolserv.in paraphrase of 14.19; a cbseportal.com chapter index). Several
   official PDFs are scanned images with no text layer, and `cbse.gov.in` / `cisce.org` return
   403 to automated fetchers — so every citation needs both the official URL and an archived
   snapshot.

## PART 3 — Synonym and equivalence verdicts (schema guidance)

There is **no national vocabulary** for Indian school certificates. A schema that hard-merges
these will be wrong somewhere.

| Cluster | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| TC ≡ School Leaving Certificate ≡ Leaving Certificate | **EQUIVALENT** — one entity + alias field | Delhi School Education Rules 1973 r.139 lists them as alternatives; Passports (Amendment) Rules 2025 G.S.R. 156(E) treats them as one clause |
| TC vs **Migration Certificate** | **DISTINCT — never merge** | Different issuer (school vs board), trigger, recipient — CBSE r.66, r.27, r.8(vi) |
| Character ≡ Conduct Certificate | **EQUIVALENT in substance** | The only prescribed form found (TNER Appendix 5-B, r.34) is titled "Conduct Certificate" and certifies "Conduct **and** Character" in one form |
| Bonafide vs **Study Certificate** | **OVERLAPPING — do NOT merge** | Study certificate is *retrospective*, year-by-year, and named in law (AP G.O.P. 646, 10.07.1979, under Art. 371-D). Bonafide is *present-tense* with **no statutory basis found at all** (Level C) |
| **Student Status Certificate** | **DOES NOT EXIST — do not create the entity** | Negative finding: no Indian act, rule, bye-law or notification uses the name. A vendor label for "bonafide" |
| Attendance Certificate | **A field, not a document** | Attendance is prescribed as certified *content* inside the TC and the board exam application (CBSE r.12(iv), 13.1(i); KER Form 5; TNER App. 5 field 15(a)). No rule prescribes a standalone form |
| Statement of Marks ≡ Marksheet ≡ Grade Sheet | **EQUIVALENT** | CBSE Ch. 9 uses the terms interchangeably; marks-vs-grades is a property of the exam scheme |
| **Provisional vs Pass Certificate** | **DISTINCT — never merge** | CBSE r.64(ii): a Provisional Certificate is expressly available to a **Compartment** candidate who has *not* passed. Merging makes that case unrepresentable |
| Pass Certificate vs Marksheet | **OVERLAPPING** | Doctrinally two (r.63(i) vs r.63(ii)); current CBSE practice prints one "Marksheet cum Certificate" |

**Two traps this exposed:**

1. **"Leaving Certificate" is not always a TC synonym.** Kerala **Form 5A** is a distinct
   statutory instrument — a Leaving Certificate for over-age pupils removed from the rolls,
   issuable precisely where a TC may *not* be (KER r.17(3)). Blind aliasing on the string would
   collapse two different legal documents.
2. **A premise in our own research brief was wrong.** We asserted the Kerala Education Rules pair
   a TC with a Conduct Certificate. They do not — KER Ch. VI contains no pupil conduct certificate
   at all. **Tamil Nadu** is the state that pairs them (TNER r.34). Corrected before it reached
   the schema.

## PART 5 — Per-domain detail

### 3.1 UDISE+ and PEN
**What it requires.** Not a document a school issues to anyone — an **annual statistical return** (the DCF) filed into a MoE portal, plus a set of system-allocated identifiers. Cycle: portal opens ~1 May → data entry by 30 September → verification → validation → certification at block/district/state level. Since AY 2022-23 UDISE+ is a **student registry**: existing students are only "progressed"; fresh entry is required only for new admissions.

**Documents.** DCF §§1, 1A–1D, 2, 3, 4, 5; UDISE Code; PEN/Student National Code; Teacher National Code; data certification certificate; progression/import-export; Know Your School; the national annual report; SDMS bulk templates; the offline paper DCF; the Section-1 change request; the HoS/respondent update; the Guidelines; the annual D.O. letters. Plus, on the CBSE side, the OASIS return and the r.14.19(f) duty to *retain* both filed returns.

**Traps.**
- **UDISE+ issues no document a school gives a parent.** The only certificate it generates is an internal block/district/state **data-certification** certificate. Whether a school-level certificate exists could not be verified.
- **Section 1 is locked.** Schools cannot edit items 1.1–1.30; corrections route through Block/District/State MIS "on Request in Prescribed Format" — and that format was never located.
- **Section 5 is a block-officer return, not a school return.** Anything that puts §5 fields in a school ERP is building against an obligation that does not reach the school.
- **The DCF is renumbered annually.** The 2022-23 §1.15 management group became §1.12 in 2026-27. Map fields once and **version the mapping per academic year**.
- **Deadlines slip every year.** AY 2025-26 was extended three times before a "final" extension to 15 December 2025. Do not hard-code 30 September.
- **Official sources contradict each other on section count** (portal says eleven, Guidelines say five, the DCF prints nine headings, the live app renders twelve forms) and **on teacher transfer** (the About page says the National Teacher Code enables cross-school transfer; the FAQ says "there is no provision to move information on teachers from one school to another"). Do not build a cross-school teacher-transfer feature on the About-page claim.
- **The Guidelines PDF's running header says 2024-25 while its cover says 2025-26**, and it never mentions PEN, APAAR or the National Codes in 81 pages.
- **OASIS ≠ UDISE+.** Different owners, portals, field sets and deadlines. Track "OASIS updated on" and "UDISE+ certified on" as **separate** compliance dates.

### 3.2 APAAR / ABC / DigiLocker
**What it requires.** Three legal layers the prior pass conflated. (1) **APAAR** is generated *by the school* inside the UDISE+ APAAR Module against a PEN and an Aadhaar-matched name, then pushed into DigiLocker by the Academic Bank of Credits. (2) **DigiLocker issuance is done by the BOARD, never the school** — CBSE signs the marksheet-cum-certificate and migration certificate with the CoE's DSC in Parinam Manjusha. The school's entire role is clerical: log in with LOC credentials, download the 6-digit access-code file, hand each code to the right student. (3) **Legal parity comes from Rule 9A of the 2016 Digital Locker Rules**, not from the UGC letters.

**Traps.**
- **APAAR is doctrinally voluntary and operationally coerced.** MoE states in terms "APAAR is not mandatory … there is no legal requirement mandating its use", and the official consent form's Option B says refusal "will not affect my ward's admission, promotion, or access to government benefits" — yet CBSE built it into the LOC and demanded 100% saturation, then created `REFUSED`/`NOGEN` when that proved unachievable.
- **The UGC letters are weaker than everyone says.** Their operative verb is "**requested**", twice; they are advisories to universities, carry no penalty, and are not addressed to schools. CBSE's own 2024 notification paraphrases them far more strongly than the letters read.
- **The migration-certificate "transition period" has NOT been shown to have ended.** CE/CBSE/2024/163685 gives no end date, no closing circular was found across 1,025 enumerated CBSE document URLs from 2025 onward, and DADS is still promoted on the CBSE homepage. **Treat it as OPEN.**
- **MoE and CBSE issue mutually contradictory document regimes on the same portal**: MoSJE's pre-matric guidelines direct "no upload of any documents" and "no system of verification by the institutions", while DEPwD's 2024 guidelines still require uploads and NSP still publishes institute-verification deadlines. The ERP must support both.
- **Aadhaar is a liability, not an asset.** It is needed only at the moment of UDISE+ entry, and MoE's own materials disagree on whether APAAR can exist without it.
- **Do not chase DigiLocker issuer status.** SOP cl.3(iv) requires "authorization from the appropriate governmental regulatory bodies to issue and authenticate documents"; the onboarding unit for school education is the *board*; and the ongoing burden is org DSC + STQC application audit + quarterly reports, with blocking without notice for ToS breach.

### 3.3 State-board TC vs SLC vs LC
**The central answer: it is BOTH.** In most states TC/SLC/LC is one instrument under regional names — Delhi's own r.139 says "a transfer or school leaving certificate", and the 2025 Passport Rules put "Transfer or school leaving or matriculation certificate" in one clause. **But Kerala is a genuine counter-example**: KER r.17(1) prescribes the TC in Form 5, while r.17(3) prescribes a separate **Leaving Certificate in Form 5A** for over-20 pupils removed from the rolls who may *not* be given a TC — different trigger, different form, different legal effect (Form 5A cannot obtain admission elsewhere). Kerala adds a third instrument, the Certificate of School Education (r.22A). And in **Tamil Nadu "ESLC" is a departmental Class VIII examination certificate**, not a school leaving document at all.

**On the Maharashtra DOB question: YES, and it is in the Code itself.** Clause 26.4 recites that "for the purposes like an admission to another educational institution the School Leaving Certificate is relied upon as an evidence for name, surname, caste, date of birth", and cl.26.3 forbids altering the DOB figure after the pupil has left, save "obvious mistakes". Kerala goes further: KER r.1(3) — "No record to prove date of birth is required when a transfer certificate is produced."

**Traps.**
- **Countersignature is NOT routine.** No state examined requires countersignature for an ordinary in-state transfer. It appears only for **inter-state migration** (KER r.10, Inspecting Officer) and specific special certificates (KER r.8(5), DEO).
- **Correction regimes diverge sharply by state**: Maharashtra routes post-leaving DOB correction to the Education Officer via Appendix Six and largely bars it; **Gujarat routes it to a Judicial Magistrate First Class** under Regulation 12A. The correction entity therefore needs an authority *type*, not an assumed education officer.
- **Duplicate procedures differ**: Kerala requires a Gazetted Officer/MLA/MP loss attestation plus a chalan; Tamil Nadu requires only a fee and the marking.
- **Hold-back is OFF unless proven ON.** The enabling law is real (RTE Amendment Act 2019, substituted s.16, new s.38(2)(fa)), but **which States have notified their rules could not be verified for a single State**. Do not populate a per-state hold-back matrix from this pass — every entry would be a guess.
- Maharashtra's cll.26.3/26.4 have been held **directory, not mandatory** by one Bombay bench (*Suresh Gupta*, WP 6331/2026) — do not encode the post-leaving DOB bar as absolute.

### 3.4 Staff, HR and payroll
**What it requires.** As of 21 November 2025 the domain was structurally rewritten. Two commencement notifications repealed eight statutes and left the EPF Act standing. The genuinely school-specific layer is CBSE Aff. Bye-Laws rr.5.2.4, 5.2.5, 5.2.6, 5.3 and 14.19(d) (appointment letters, written contract, service books, confirmation letters, personal files) plus POSH ss.4(1), 19(b), 21, 22 with s.26 penalties.

**Traps.**
- **Almost everything schools issue daily has no prescribed format**: experience certificates, relieving letters, salary certificates, increment letters, offer letters, F&F statements. Level C, all of them.
- **The signatory trap**: CBSE r.9.3.5 vests appointment, disciplinary, termination and suspension letters in the **Manager/Correspondent**, not the Principal. An ERP where the Principal role signs everything cannot represent it — and in Delhi a termination without the Director's prior approval (DSEA s.8(2)) is liable to be set aside regardless of merits, with a three-month Tribunal appeal.
- **CBSE r.5.3.2 does not mandate a medical or character certificate** — it mandates that the *service rules cover the subject*. The clause is routinely mis-cited.
- **Only two hard retention periods exist in the entire domain**, both ESI: five years for the Register of Employees (Reg. 32(2)) and five years for the Accident Book from last entry (Reg. 66(ii)). Everything else — service books, appointment letters, contracts, payslips, POSH records, gratuity records — has none.
- **POSH has no headcount threshold on its face.** s.4(1) says "every employer of a workplace"; the "ten or more" rule is *inferred* from s.6(1). And the first proviso requires an IC at **every administrative unit at a different place** — routinely ignored by multi-campus groups.
- **Two annual POSH outputs, not one**: the IC's s.21 report (to employer + District Officer) and the employer's s.22 disclosure (in the annual report, else to the District Officer).
- **EPF paragraph 36(2)(c)'s duty to hand the employee a copy** on joining and on leaving is textual and almost never observed — but note the 2026 Scheme **deleted** it.

### 3.5 Child safety and transport compliance
**What it requires.** The statutory layer is thin and duty-shaped: POCSO s.19 (report), s.21(2) (institution head liability), and **POCSO Rules 2020 r.3(4)** — the only central provision binding a school *as an institution*: periodic police verification of every staff member and periodic child-safety training. Everything else that looks statutory is board regulation, or advisory guidance **elevated to binding status by CBSE Aff. Bye-Laws r.4.7.6**, which makes the NCPCR Manual, the NDMA School Safety Policy 2016, the *Avinash Mehrotra* directions and NBC-2005 mandatory for CBSE schools while leaving them advisory elsewhere.

**The concrete certificate set is real and precisely specified**: CBSE Circular 12/2022 names exactly **07 essential certificates** with formats at Appendices X–XVI, and non-submission in that format causes **summary rejection**.

**Transport** is governed by CBSE Circ. 8/2017 Annexure-I built on the SC order of 16.12.1997 in *M.C. Mehta* (W.P.(C) 13029/1985) and the MV Act 1988 — generating a real per-bus document set.

**Traps.**
- **A fire-equipment installation letter from a vendor is NOT a Fire Safety Certificate** — SARAS says so expressly, and schools submit it constantly.
- **The Building Safety Certificate must come from a Government works-department Assistant Engineer or above.** Private structural engineers' certificates are not acceptable.
- **The in-bus manifest is the most sensitive artefact in the domain** — every child's name and home address, kept loose in a driver's cab.
- **Two thresholds in the driver disqualification rule**, and the CBSE and NCPCR texts differ (NCPCR omits the POCSO limb). Source the rules engine to the CBSE circular.
- **The health card is not the CBSE requirement.** r.9.2.24 requires *periodical medical examination and a report to parents*; the Individual Child Health Card appears only as checklist item 118. Vendors present the card as the bye-law duty; it is not.
- **Mock drill frequency is internally contradictory in the primary source** (six-monthly in the guiding note, monthly in the action table).
- **Level A does not mean national here.** All 07 certificates, the transport annexure and the OASIS action-taken report bind **CBSE-affiliated schools only** — roughly 30k of ~1.5M schools.

### 3.6 Scholarships and welfare
**What it requires.** Far less school-issued paper than assumed, and mostly *electronic acts inside a government portal*. The load-bearing duty is **first-level institute verification on NSP**, plus the SOP §4.2.2(4)(a) duty to "maintain physical copies of supporting documents" for production on demand — an **open-ended custody duty with no retention period anywhere**. Income, caste, disability/UDID, domicile and unclean-occupation certificates are **never issued by the school**; they come from State revenue officers and are merely collected. On RTE, the Act fixes the duty (ss.12(1)(c), 12(2), 12(3), 13, 14) but prescribes **no document list, no claim form and no retention period** — all of that sits in unretrieved State rules.

**Traps.**
- **Pre-matric is not a Class IX–X-only feature.** Component 2 (children of manual scavengers, tanners, flayers, waste pickers, hazardous cleaners) reaches **Class I**, with no income ceiling.
- **The paper bonafide certificate has been replaced doctrinally** by an *electronic bona-fide confirmation on the day of admission* (§7.2) and by L1 verification — yet parents are still asked for the paper constantly.
- **The 75% attendance threshold silently forfeits the award.** Surface it inside the attendance module, not at year end.
- **RTE s.13 bars any screening procedure** — so for a 25% quota applicant the *existence* of a test or interview record is itself evidence of an offence (₹25,000 first contravention). Make it structurally impossible.
- **RTE s.14(2)**: "No child shall be denied admission for lack of age proof." An admission form with a mandatory birth-certificate upload is a fail-closed control the statute forbids.
- **s.9(d) is the local authority's duty.** Do not build a neighbourhood child register as a school compliance feature.
- **Reimbursement is asymmetric**: s.2(n)(iii) specified-category schools must admit but are **not** reimbursed under s.12(2).

### 3.7 Fee receipts, GST and audit
**What it requires.** No national fee-receipt format and no central law requiring one. School fees are GST-exempt. The audit clause is **CBSE r.6.3** (accounts on accounting standards, audited *and certified* by a CA) with r.6.5 (school books separate from the society's), r.6.4 (all transactions digital) and r.7.4 (dues capped at the month of discontinuance). Delhi adds an annual 31-July filing of audited final accounts plus six other items under r.180 and Appendix II.

**Traps.**
- **Two firm negatives, tested directly**: no counterfoil/duplicate requirement exists in the Delhi Rules, the Rajasthan Rules or the CBSE Bye-Laws (zero text matches); and no no-dues certificate exists or is authorised anywhere.
- **CBSE r.7.4 names two different dates** — the month of discontinuance, *not* the month the TC was applied for. An ERP computing dues from the TC date implements the wrong rule, and it applies "to all Heads of fee".
- **r.7.3 makes fee heads a state-regulated list**, not free text.
- **Delhi's concession caps (20% overall, 15% full-fee) are Delhi-specific and are NOT RTE's 25%.** And r.162 means a full fee waiver does **not** waive the Pupils' Fund contribution (except SC/ST) — so concessions must be per fee head.
- **The GST entry-66 text everyone quotes is the 2017 original.** Notification 2/2018-CT(R) inserted entrance-examination fees and online educational journals, **deleted "upto higher secondary" from (b)(iv)**, and narrowed the proviso to sub-items (i)–(iii). Quoting the 2017 text asserts a restriction the law has not contained since January 2018.
- **The FCRA six-year rule (FCRR r.17(7)) is the only fixed retention period in the entire domain.**

### 3.8 Real-world school print workflow
Covered in §1 finding ⑤. Two additional traps: the **Riso duplicator reproduces a master**, so nothing per-student can go through it; and **thermal paper is not archival**, so a thermal slip cannot be the school's retained copy. The one artefact with genuine pre-printed structure besides the receipt book is the **CBSE-format TC blank**, whose "Book No. …… Sl. No. ……" header is direct evidence that the doctrinal artefact is a serially numbered bound book — but the control layer around those serials (procurement rule, approved supplier, stock/issue register) **could not be established at all**, and the fact that hundreds of schools publish downloadable blank TC PDFs shows many now print it themselves.

### 3.9 The synonym and equivalence question
See §5 for the verdicts. The domain's own trap: **"Level A" and "national" are not the same thing.** Almost every certificate rule here binds one board or one state, and three of them (`Statement of Marks`, `Pass Certificate`, `Provisional Certificate`) turned out to be *Board* obligations mislabelled as school document requirements.

## PART 6 — Verification outcomes: what was refuted and why

Eleven claims were refuted or materially rescoped. Each is downgraded in the master table and each carries a "**must not be restated**" line.

**① DCF Sections 1A/1B/1C/1D — A → B.** No statutory clause; "mandatory" 0 hits, "statutory" 0, "penalty" 0 across 171,676 characters of the Guidelines. §1C Q1.59 is Govt/aided only; §1D applies only to schools with NSQF courses. **Must not restate:** that Q1.1–1.30 belong to Section 1A (they are Section 1, and are school-locked), or that the online module's twelve form-sections are a prescribed format — form9–12 are Section 2.

**② DCF Section 5 — refuted on scope; stays A.** Its own subtitle reads "(To be filled at the Level of Block Level BRC's/Block Education Officers)" and its closing note assigns 5.1–5.2 to the Teacher Module and 5.3–5.5 to Block Admin/MIS. **Must not restate:** that a school ERP must capture Section 5 fields. A school has no Section 5 obligation.

**③ Student progression / inter-school transfer — A → B.** D.O. letters are demi-official officer-to-officer correspondence addressed to States/UTs, drafted in relief language ("no fresh data entry is required"), and the 2026 one is signed by a Deputy Director General. Neither letter contains the words PEN, transfer, import or export. **Must not restate:** that the transfer/import-export limb has any authority — it rests solely on a UI translation string, `/AboutUDISE/LineF`, whose verb is "helps in".

**④ DCF Guidelines and ⑤ UDISE+ D.O. letters — A → B.** Both are executive instruments citing no enabling law; the D.O.s are per-cycle, superseded annually, and their operative verb is "requested". **Must not restate:** that the D.O. letters are a standing requirement with fixed dates, or that they impose anything on ERP software.

**⑥ APAAR consent form (Annexure-1) — A → B; APAAR ID — A → B; Virtual APAAR card — A → B; AIM return — A → B; UDISE+ APAAR MIS report — A → C.** A CBSE circular plus an MoE PowerPoint deck is not law; the deck's "as per provisions of AADHAAR act" asserts *compliance*, not prescription. FAQ 18 says status "**can be checked**". **Must not restate:** the APAAR card's issuer name, DSC, timestamp, monogram and QR as prescribed attributes — they appear in **none** of the four cited sources; nor FAQ 16's field list as an ERP schema (it lists government-side portal inputs, and importing "store Aadhaar" from it inverts the data-protection position).

**⑦ CBSE Marksheet-cum-Passing Certificate, Migration Certificate, DigiLocker access-code file, Parinam Manjusha — all A → B; DigiLocker Account Confirmation — A → D.** Every one is a Board-side instrument or a portal process, not a school document requirement. **Must not restate:** that an ERP must generate a marksheet, a pass certificate or a migration certificate. A school-generated marksheet in board layout would be forgery — CBSE's press release of 14.08.2025 warns about exactly this. Also do not restate the access-code duty as mandatory: every verb attached to the code file is permissive; the sole imperative is to download and share the user manual.

**⑧ Kerala Progress Card Form 8/8A — A → B.** Rule VIII-12(3)'s verb is "is **expected to**", with the express alternative "or in such other manner as he may deem fit" — while 12(1) and 12(2) in the same rule say "shall". **Must not restate:** Form 8/8A as a mandatory prescribed document. The mandatory, form-prescribed record in that rule group is the **Form 7 Progress Register** (Std VIII–X) plus the Mark Register.

**⑨ SMC minutes (safety agenda) — A → B.** rr.8.2.2/8.4.13/8.4.15 prescribe composition, duty and cadence; **no minutes obligation exists in Chapter 8**, r.8.5 subordinates the whole chapter to State law, and for private unaided schools the NCPCR duty attaches to the **PTA**, not the SMC. **Must not restate:** "(safety agenda)" as a requirement — no instrument requires a standing safety agenda item.

**⑩ State/UT NOC — A → B.** CBSE Notification 04/2025 (20.02.2025) amended cl.2.3.5: schools may apply "with or without NOC" w.e.f. session 2026-27, with a **deemed NOC** after 30 days plus a 15-day reminder. **Must not restate:** the NOC as a hard-required blocking field, or as applying to Govt/aided/KVS/NVS/Defence/local-body schools — it never did (cl.2.3.5 reaches only categories 2.1.5–2.1.8), and foreign schools submit an **Indian mission** NOC under r.2.5.1.

**⑪ NSP cluster — six documents downgraded.** Institute KYC form, INO form, L1 verification, bona-fide confirmation, monthly attendance upload, promotion/re-joining confirmation and GRO all **A → B**; the half-yearly self-audited report **A → C**. A portal user manual is not law; an executive SOP whose general terms read "All parties undertake to act in good faith" is not law; and CSS guidelines are conditions on a grant. **Must not restate:** that reason codes exist for L1 rejection (there are none — reasons are free text shown to the applicant); that there are four verification outcomes (there are three, plus a separate anti-fraud "Mark as Fake"); or that §11.2(e)'s "Attendance module" means the ERP's attendance module — it means an NSP module that was unbuilt when the guideline issued.

**⑫ Statement of Marks, Pass Certificate, Provisional Certificate, Board DOB Certificate — all A → C; Duplicate/Triplicate Pass Certificate — A → B; Certificate of School-Based Evaluation — A → D (REPEALED).** Wrong duty-holder throughout: these are Board obligations. **Must not restate:** the affidavit-before-a-First-Class-Magistrate condition for duplicate board certificates — CBSE notification CBSE/Coord/EC-14-02/2015 (10.07.2015) **deleted** it, leaving application form + fee + national-newspaper press clipping. And **do not build the Certificate of School-Based Evaluation at all** — CBSE deleted r.41(v) in 2010 with the stated reason "schools will not be required to give this separate certificate to the students".

**⑬ Duplicate TC — refuted on a fabricated quote; stays A.** The sentence presented in quotation marks — "The copy should clearly bear the mark duplicate **in red ink**. It shall be issued **only once**." — appears **nowhere** in TNER r.44. **Must not restate:** the red-ink requirement (the only "red ink" in TNER concerns how a pupil's *name* is entered in the admission register) or the once-only bar (a system hard-blocking a second duplicate would enforce a rule that does not exist). Also do not merge the search fee (for an *original* TC applied for more than a year after leaving) with the duplicate fee (a flat fifty paise).

**⑭ Land Certificate — refuted on currency; stays A.** SARAS Manual 5.0/7.0 renumbered it to §1.3(5), moved the format authority from "Appendix X of Circular 12/2022" to **Annexure-B of the SARAS Manual**, and Circular 13/2024 (16.08.2024) **exempts** land allotted by development authorities, PSUs, SEZs and project companies. **Must not restate:** "Appendix X" as the current format pointer or "no other format will be acceptable" as absolute.

**⑮ Education services exemption — refuted on text currency; stays A as legal background.** **Must not restate** the 2017 wording, which still carries the phrase "upto higher secondary" in (b)(iv) that Notification 2/2018-CT(R) deleted, and states the proviso as covering the whole of (b) when it now covers only (i)–(iii). Also: it prescribes **no document**, so it must not sit in a document register as "mandatory".

**⑯ Salary disbursement record — refuted on authority; stays A, re-anchored.** ss.15–17 are conduct duties imposing no record obligation; the record duty is **s.50**. **Must not restate:** "no form prescribed" — s.67(2)(x)/(y) expressly empower the appropriate Government to prescribe the register manner and the wage-slip form.

**⑰ EPF monthly joiners/leavers return — refuted on supersession; stays A, re-anchored.** The EPF Scheme 1952 was superseded by **G.S.R. 525(E), 29.06.2026, in force 01.07.2026**. **Must not restate:** Form 5, the Form 2 attachment, the monthly NIL return for domestic employees, or the para 36(2)(c) employee-copy duty — the 2026 Scheme deletes all four. And note **FORM-V in the 2026 Scheme is the one-off consolidated return**, not the monthly new-member return: carrying "Form 5" forward maps the requirement onto the wrong form.

**⑱ Staff child-safety training record — A → C (the DUTY stays A).** **Must not restate:** any cadence, expiry or reminder interval as legally required. "Periodic" is undefined and no penalty attaches to r.3(4).

**⑲ Class attendance register as fee record — refuted on scope; stays A.** Binds **Delhi Government/aided schools only**, and only where an employee physically collects money in person. **Must not restate:** it as a general obligation, nor infer that an ERP must write fee collections into the attendance module — a compliant Delhi aided school satisfies r.168(3) with a pen while its ERP keeps fees and attendance entirely separate.

**⑳ TC (generic) — refuted on framing; stays A.** **Must not restate** the TC as a mandatory *precondition to admission*: RTE s.5(3)'s proviso forbids delaying or denying admission for want of one, Delhi r.139 reaches only aided schools, and the Madras HC DB has directed TN schools "not to insist upon to produce Transfer Certificate by the child at the time of Admission" and ordered the TNER amended. The mandatory duty is to **issue**.

Two further scope corrections without downgrade: **Fee Collection Register / Cash Book** bind Delhi **aided** schools only (Ch. XIII Part A; no unaided-extension clause, contrast r.145(2)) plus Rajasthan private schools; and the **Certificate of Registration (Appendix XVI)** is a **societies-only** proforma — no format exists for a trust deed or a s.8 Certificate of Incorporation.

## PART 7 — Synonym verdicts (research synthesis; see also PART 3)

**EQUIVALENT — may share a table, distinguished by a label/alias field:**

1. **Transfer Certificate ≡ School Leaving Certificate.** Textual proof: Delhi r.139 ("a transfer **or** school leaving certificate") and Passports (Amendment) Rules 2025 Sch. III (IV)(A)(1)(b)(I)(ii) ("Transfer **or** school leaving **or** matriculation certificate"). **One entity, display-label attribute.** *Exception that must be honoured:* Kerala Form 5A "Leaving Certificate" and Tamil Nadu's ESLC are **different instruments** that happen to use the word "leaving" — so alias mappings must be **scoped per tenant/state, never global**.
2. **Character Certificate ≡ Conduct Certificate.** The only prescribed form found (TN Appendix 5-B) is titled "CONDUCT CERTIFICATE" and certifies "his/her **Conduct and Character**" in one field. **One document type with a configurable title**, plus a separate boolean for whether the tenant's TC embeds the conduct field (TN yes, field 16; Kerala no such field exists on Form 5).
3. **Statement of Marks ≡ Marksheet ≡ Grade Sheet.** CBSE r.63 itself uses "statement of marks", "statement of marks/grades" and "Marks Statement" interchangeably; marks-vs-grades is a property of the scheme of examination, not a different document.
4. **No-Dues Certificate ≡ Clearance Certificate.** Both internal, both with zero legal existence; they differ only in scope (money owed vs multi-department sign-off). **One "exit clearance" checklist entity with configurable line items**, of which fees is one.
5. **"Student Status Certificate" ≡ Bonafide Certificate** — an alias label with no Indian legal referent. **No separate type, template or numbering series.**

**OVERLAPPING — share fields, must NOT be merged:**

6. **Study Certificate vs Bonafide Certificate.** Retrospective year-by-year attestation vs present-tense enrolment attestation. The study certificate needs a **year-wise enrolment-span query and the institution's LOCATION** on its face (AP G.O.P. 646 para 9 local-candidate claims); a present-tense bonafide template carries neither, and the authority demanding one will not accept the other.
7. **Pass Certificate vs Marksheet.** Doctrinally two documents with different eligibility — *everyone who appeared* gets the statement (r.63(i)); *only those who passed* get the certificate (r.63(ii)) — which current CBSE practice prints as one "Marksheet cum Certificate". **The doctrinal split still matters**: compartment/improvement candidates get a marks statement and **no** certificate.
8. **Attendance Certificate vs the attendance FIELD in the TC / exam application.** Same computed number, different carriers. **Compute once behind one interface.**

**DISTINCT — never merge:**

9. **Transfer Certificate vs Migration Certificate.** Different issuer (school vs Board), trigger, recipient, lifecycle and fee. A student leaving in Class VII gets a TC and can never get a Migration Certificate. CBSE r.8(vi) governs the TC; r.66/r.27 govern the Migration Certificate. **The Migration Certificate is an INBOUND third-party artefact — a "generate migration certificate" feature issues a document the school has no authority to issue.**
10. **Kerala Form 5 (TC) vs Form 5A (Leaving Certificate).** Different trigger (leaving vs removal + age >20 without sanction), different form, different legal effect (Form 5A **cannot** obtain admission elsewhere). **A leaving-document entity needs a TYPE discriminator driving a different template, a different eligibility rule and a different downstream effect** — this is the single clearest proof that TC and "LC" cannot be one type with a label field.
11. **Provisional Certificate vs Pass Certificate.** r.64(ii) makes the provisional certificate available to a **Compartment** candidate who will never receive a pass certificate for that attempt. **Separate records linked by a supersession pointer**, not one record with a status flag.
12. **School report card vs Board marksheet.** Internal and revisable vs externally authoritative and immutable.
13. **Board DOB Certificate vs school TC vs civil birth-register extract.** Three issuers, three documents; and the **Class X** pass certificate carries the DOB while the Class XII one does not (r.65(i)).
14. **POSH Internal Committee vs NCPCR CSA Grievance Committee.** Different statute, different constituency (adult women employees vs children), different composition rules. Merging them satisfies neither.
15. **Staff attendance vs student attendance.** Different regulatory objects; the staff record must carry hours, rest/meal intervals and separately-entered overtime (Delhi S&E s.33(2), SS Code s.123(a)).
16. **Fee receipt vs GST tax invoice vs bill of supply vs salary certificate vs wage slip.** Separate series, separate templates, separate legal bases. The wage slip is now statutory; the salary certificate is not.
17. **PEN / APAAR / Aadhaar / OTR / admission number.** Five keys, five issuers, five lifecycles.

### The TC-withholding legal question — verdict

**There IS express Level-A rule text permitting fee-linked withholding**, in at least three instruments, and it reaches beyond the elementary stage: KER Ch.VI **r.17(2)** and r.18(2); TNER **rr.38/40/41/42**; CBSE Exam Bye-Laws Ch.3 **r.8(vi)** ("shall on a payment of all dues, receive an authenticated copy of the Transfer certificate"). This settles the prior pass's open question: it is not merely customary.

**But that basis is being read down or overridden almost everywhere:**
- **Delhi has no such rule at all** — Del HC DB, *Master Sahib Singh Riat*, LPA 393/2014, 01.08.2014: the DSE Act and Rules 1973 "do not contain any provision regarding the issuance of Transfer Certificate"; r.139 governs only admission. (In that case the condition came from the school's *own* rules, and the Court still conditioned delivery on ₹2,480 — so the 2014 Delhi position is materially softer than the 2019–2025 line.)
- **Kerala HC, 2025:KER:69076, 17.09.2025** (Nagaresh J) considered CBSE r.8(vi) squarely: institutions "are not entitled to withhold Transfer Certificate for non-payment of dues" (following *Fathima Zahara*, 2023 KHC 9278).
- **Madras HC DB, 22.07.2024**: a TC "is not a tool for the schools to collect arrear fees from the parents"; recording fee default on a TC is socio-economic stigmatisation; the Code of Regulations for Matriculation Schools is repugnant to the RTE Act to that extent; TNER to be amended in three months; circulars to all TN schools not to insist on a TC at admission.
- **Karnataka HC, WP 31492/2024, 2025:KHC:5986, 10.02.2025**: "The certificate of a student is the student's property and respondent No.1 holds the Transfer certificate/documents as a **trustee**"; a minor cannot be penalised for a parent's default.
- **Delhi HC, *Hanya v. Maxfort School*, W.P.(C) 7928/2022, 05.07.2022**, following the DB in *Court On Its Own Motion*, W.P.(C) 6658/2019, 11.07.2019: "non-payment of school fee cannot be a valid ground to withhold the School Leaving Certificate", with liberty to recover by other remedies.
- **At the elementary stage RTE s.5(3) is decisive independently of fees** — immediate issuance, absence no ground to deny admission elsewhere, disciplinary liability for delay.

**The Supreme Court did NOT decide the TC question.** *Indian School, Jodhpur v. State of Rajasthan*, C.A. 1724/2021, 03.05.2021, para 117(iv) and (vii) bar debarring students from classes, withholding **results**, and withholding **names for board examinations** for fee arrears. **Transfer certificates are not mentioned**, and the directions were a one-time COVID-year measure. Citing that case for TCs is an extension, not a holding.

**Schema guidance.** `tc_fee_gate` is a **per-tenant, per-class-range policy flag**, default OFF; where ON it produces a warning plus a Head-level override with a mandatory reason code and a full audit trail — never a silent refusal. Capture the "whether all fees paid" field (it is a data field on both the Kerala and Tamil Nadu prescribed forms) but **capturing it is not the same as gating on it** — and the Madras HC has held that stigmatising fee-default entries on a TC are themselves objectionable. The no-dues certificate gets no serial-numbered, sealed, printable template.

## PART 9 — Priority classification

**P0 — essential; a school cannot legally or practically operate without it.**

| Document | Why P0 |
|---|---|
| Transfer Certificate / SLC (all state variants) | Statutory duty to issue (RTE s.5(3) immediate at elementary); a child cannot enrol elsewhere without it; disciplinary liability for delay |
| Admission Register / General Register (KER Form 4; Maharashtra GR) | The permanent statutory record every other document derives from; DOB alteration is a quasi-judicial act |
| Application for Admission (KER Form 3, TN App. 4) | "No pupil shall be admitted except on an application in form 3" — admission is void without it |
| Student attendance register (KER Form 6) | Statutory register; feeds TC fields, board-exam eligibility (75%), scholarship continuation and MDM |
| School fee receipt | Delhi r.172(2): a proper receipt for **every** collection, in the school's own name |
| Fee Collection Register + Cash Book (where applicable) | Statutory books with a same-day posting sequence and head-of-school countersignature |
| Annual audited statement of accounts (CA) | CBSE r.6.3 affiliation condition; Delhi r.180 filing by 31 July |
| Wage slip | Code on Wages s.50(3), in force nationally since 21.11.2025, no threshold, no exemption reaching a school |
| Wage register / muster roll | s.50(1) + SS Code s.123(a); dedicated penalty for non-maintenance (s.54(2)) |
| Appointment letter | CBSE r.5.2.4 affiliation condition **and** Delhi S&E s.34 |
| Service book | r.14.19(d); the artefact a CBSE inspection asks for |
| Staff police verification record | POCSO Rules 2020 r.3(4) — the only central provision binding a school as an institution |
| POSH IC constitution order | s.4(1) "every employer"; ₹50,000 fine + licence cancellation on repeat |

| Building Safety Certificate | Same; occupancy of the premises turns on it |
| Recognition Certificate | RTE s.18 — a school may not be run without it |
| School bus fitness certificate, permit, insurance | Without these the vehicle is not validly registered and may not ply |
